How a Network "Learns" — Training and Weights, in Plain Terms

1. A Network Starts Out Knowing Nothing

When a network is first built, its weights — the "how much each input matters" dials from the previous topic — are essentially set at random. Left as they are, the network's guesses would be little better than a coin toss.

Say Rafiki Insights is building a tool to flag which savings-group members are at risk of stopping their contributions. Fresh out of the box, the network has no idea which signals (missed meetings, falling deposit amounts, and so on) actually matter. It has to be trained before it is any use to a client.

2. The Feedback Loop: Guess, Compare, Adjust

Training means repeatedly showing the network examples where the correct answer is already known, and letting it improve from its mistakes. The loop looks like this:

1. Network makes a guess ("this member looks likely to stop saving")
2. That guess is compared to what actually happened (did they stop, or not?)
3. The gap between the guess and reality shows the network how wrong it was
4. Its weights are nudged slightly, in whatever direction would have made that guess less wrong
5. Repeat with the next example

Notice there is no formula needed to understand this — it is the same feedback loop a person uses when learning any new skill: try, see how it went, adjust slightly, try again. The network does the same thing, just automatically and very fast.

3. Why It Has to Repeat, Over and Over

One correction barely moves the needle. It is only after this guess-compare-adjust loop repeats across hundreds, thousands, or millions of past examples that the weights settle into values that make consistently good guesses. Each pass nudges things a little closer to "less wrong."

This is also why the quality of the training examples matters so much: if Rafiki Insights only had a handful of past cases, or the recorded outcomes were unreliable, the network would have very little to learn from, and the training loop could not do its job properly.

Key Point. Training is not a single step — it is a repeated feedback loop: guess, compare to the correct answer, nudge the weights to be less wrong, and repeat, across many examples, until the network's guesses become reliably good.
