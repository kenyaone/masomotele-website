Common Computer Vision Tasks — Detection vs Classification

Classification: What Is This Image, Overall?

Image classification looks at a whole photo and gives it a single overall label. Rafiki Insights might build a classifier that sorts incoming photos as "receipt" or "invoice," or that labels a shelf photo as "fully stocked" or "needs restocking." Whatever is in the picture, classification answers just one question at a time, with one answer, for the whole image — it does not say where anything is, or how many of something there are.

Object Detection: What's In It, and Where?

Object detection goes further: it finds one or more objects within a single image and reports both what they are and roughly where they are, usually marked with a box drawn around each one. A delivery-yard photo might be run through a detector that finds every crate in the frame, counts them, and marks each one's position — something a single overall label could never do.

Key Point. Classification gives one label for the whole picture ("what is this, overall"). Object detection finds, counts and locates individual objects inside the picture ("what's here, and where"). Both start from the same pixel-pattern idea from the previous lesson — they just answer different questions.

Choosing the Right Task for the Business Question

When a client describes what they want from a photo-based tool, the first thing Brian needs to work out is which question they are actually asking. If the answer they need is a single overall label — "is this a receipt or an invoice," "is this shelf okay or not" — that's classification. If the answer they need involves finding, counting, or locating several things within one photo — "how many sacks are stacked here, and roughly where" — that's object detection. Getting this right at the scoping stage saves Rafiki Insights from building the wrong tool and having to redo the work later.
