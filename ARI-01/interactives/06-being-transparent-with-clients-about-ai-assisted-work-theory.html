Being Transparent With Clients About AI-Assisted Work

Why Disclosure Matters

Clients hire Rafiki Insights because they trust Brian's judgment, not just his typing speed. When an AI tool helps draft part of a report — summarising a dataset, suggesting phrasing, or drawing a first-pass chart — the client has a reasonable interest in knowing that. Disclosure is not an admission of weakness; it is simply being honest about how the work was produced, which is what keeps the client's trust genuine rather than based on a false impression.

The Risk of Not Reviewing AI Output

AI tools can write confidently even when they are wrong — inventing a statistic, misreading a trend, or drawing a conclusion the underlying data does not actually support. If Brian copies an AI-drafted paragraph straight into a client report without checking its numbers and claims against the real data, any resulting error becomes Brian's mistake, not the AI's. The tool did not sign the report; Brian did.

Key Point. AI can help Brian write faster, but only Brian's review makes the output trustworthy — and clients deserve to know AI was part of the process.

Simple Habits for Transparent, Reviewed Work

Practical habits that keep this professional rather than risky: note briefly in the report or cover email where AI assistance was used, always cross-check any figures or claims an AI tool produced against the client's actual data before sending anything out, and treat every AI draft as a first attempt that needs a human sign-off — never as a finished, ready-to-send answer.
