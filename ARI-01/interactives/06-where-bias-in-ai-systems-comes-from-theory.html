Where Bias in AI Systems Comes From

Bias Is a Data Problem, Not a "Robot Opinion"

An AI system does not sit and form opinions about people. What it does is look for patterns in the examples it was trained on, and then repeat those patterns on new cases. If Brian trains a simple model to predict which customers are likely to pay late using five years of a client's sales records, the model is not being clever or unfair on its own — it is copying whatever pattern already existed in that history, good or bad.

This is why people say "the data is the bias." If the training data mostly shows one kind of outcome for one kind of group, the system learns that pattern as normal and applies it to everyone, including new cases where it should not.

The Hiring-Screening Example

Imagine a company builds an AI tool to screen job applications, training it on ten years of resumes from people the company previously hired. If most of those past hires happened to share one university background, one gender, or one type of work experience — not because that background makes someone more capable, but simply because of who applied or was hired before — the tool learns to score resumes like those more highly. A strong candidate with a different background can be scored lower, not due to weaker qualifications, but because their profile looks different from the historical pattern.

Key Point. Bias comes from what the training data does and does not represent — not from the algorithm having an opinion. Always ask: whose examples were used to teach this system, and who is missing from them?

What This Means for Rafiki Insights

When Brian helps a client at Rafiki Insights build a scoring, screening, or ranking tool from their historical records, the historical records are the real source of any unfairness the tool ends up producing. Before trusting or recommending such a tool, Brian's job includes asking who is represented in that data, who is underrepresented, and whether the pattern being learned is actually one the client should want repeated.
