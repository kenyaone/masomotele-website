AI as a Decision-Support Tool, Not a Decision-Maker

What "Decision Support" Actually Means

Some AI tools do not just crunch data — they go a step further and produce a recommendation: a risk score, a "yes/no" suggestion, a ranked shortlist. It is tempting to treat a confident-looking recommendation as the answer. But a decision-support tool is meant to be exactly that — support. It is one input into a decision, like a second opinion, not the decision itself.

At Rafiki Insights, Brian sometimes uses an AI tool that scores how risky it would be to extend credit to a client's customer. That score is useful information. It is not, by itself, a final answer.

Why Consequential Decisions Need a Human in the Loop

Imagine the AI credit-scoring tool flags a small business as "high risk" because its last three months of cashflow look patchy. Brian, however, knows this business just came through a known seasonal dip in its trade, and has a solid history with its suppliers — context the tool never saw, because it only looked at the numbers in front of it.

If Brian just forwarded the tool's score as the final decision, a real business owner could lose access to credit they genuinely deserve, over a pattern the tool misread. The more consequential the decision — the more it can affect someone's business or livelihood — the more it matters that a human weighs the tool's output alongside everything else they know.

Key Point. An AI recommendation is an input to Brian's decision, not the decision itself — especially when the outcome affects someone's business or livelihood.

Using AI Output Responsibly

Responsible use sits between two extremes. One extreme is blindly following whatever the tool says because it feels objective and saves time. The other is ignoring it altogether because "it's just AI". Neither serves the client well.

The sensible middle ground: treat the AI's output as one data point among several, note down why Brian agreed or disagreed with it, and keep the responsibility for the final call with the human making the recommendation — not with the tool that produced the score.
