AI in Industry · Topic 3

Choosing the Right Tool for a Real Business Problem

Not Every Problem Needs AI

It is easy to assume that AI is always the most powerful, most modern option, so it should be reached for first. In practice, plenty of everyday business questions have a simpler, faster, more reliable answer already sitting in a spreadsheet. If a client asks "what was our total revenue last month?", the answer is a SUM formula on already-recorded figures — not a job for an AI tool.

Using AI for a question like that would not just be unnecessary, it could actually be slower and less trustworthy than the simple formula that already exists for it.

Matching the Tool to the Task

At Rafiki Insights, Brian thinks about three broad kinds of tools, each suited to a different shape of question:

  • A simple spreadsheet formula or rule — for well-defined, repeatable calculations: totals, averages, simple lookups, sorting a list.
  • A predictive or machine-learning model — for finding patterns across a lot of historical data to predict something, like which customers are likely to stop buying, or what demand might look like next quarter.
  • An AI chat assistant — for flexible language tasks: drafting a clear explanation for a client, summarising a messy conversation, or answering a one-off open-ended question in plain words.

A Simple Way to Decide

A quick way Brian sorts a new question: is it a well-defined, repeatable calculation? Use a formula. Does it need a prediction based on patterns across a lot of past data? Use a model. Does it need flexible, natural-sounding language, written or understood? Use an AI assistant. And if a much simpler tool would clearly do the job just as well, that is the one to reach for — not AI by default.

Key Point. The best analyst doesn't reach for AI first — they reach for the tool that actually fits the shape of the question.
