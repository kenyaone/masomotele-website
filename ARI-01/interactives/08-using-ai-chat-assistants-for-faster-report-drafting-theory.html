Using AI Chat Assistants for Faster Report Drafting

What Report Drafting Actually Takes Time

At Rafiki Insights, Brian's job is to look at a client's numbers and work out what they mean. But once the analysis is done, there is still another job left: turning it into a report a client can actually read — clear sentences, a sensible order, a professional tone. That part is often just as time-consuming as the analysis itself, and it is exactly the part an AI chat assistant is good at speeding up.

Brian's Workflow: Bullets In, Draft Out

Brian never asks an AI assistant to analyse a client's data for him. Instead, once he has worked out the real findings, he writes them down as plain bullet points — for example: "Sales dropped 12% in June", "Return requests up sharply on the Eldoret branch", "Repeat customers spent more on average than new ones." He then pastes those bullets into the assistant and asks it to turn them into a short, client-ready summary — telling it who the report is for and what tone to use. The assistant handles the phrasing and structure; Brian has already done the real thinking.

Key Point. An AI assistant can turn your bullet points into polished prose in seconds — but it cannot supply findings you never gave it, and it doesn't know Rafiki Insights' clients' data.

Editing the Draft — Never Skip This

The draft the assistant produces is a starting point, not a finished report. Brian reads it line by line, checking that every sentence matches something from his own bullets, fixing anything that sounds off, and trimming anything the assistant added that he didn't actually say. Only after that edit does the report go anywhere near a client.
