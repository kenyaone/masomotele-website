Verifying AI Output Before Presenting to a Client

Why Polished Doesn't Mean Correct

An AI assistant is good at producing text that reads smoothly and sounds confident — whether or not every detail in it is actually true. It predicts plausible-sounding wording; it doesn't check facts against Rafiki Insights' client data. A draft can look completely professional and still contain a wrong number or an invented explanation.

Brian's Verification Checklist

Before anything AI-assisted goes to a client, Brian works through four checks:

  1. Match every number and statistic in the draft back to the original source data.
  2. Read closely for any claim, reason or detail that wasn't in his own original notes.
  3. Confirm that no confidential client information — names, financial figures, internal details — was ever pasted into a public AI tool without permission.
  4. Read the whole document himself, start to finish, before it is sent.
Key Point. If Brian can't point to where a figure or claim in the draft actually came from, it doesn't go to the client until he can.

Making Verification a Habit, Not an Exception

It's tempting to skip the checklist when a deadline is close or the draft looks obviously fine — but that's exactly when a missed error is most likely to reach a client. Rafiki Insights treats this check as a fixed step for every AI-assisted piece of work, regardless of how confident the draft looks or how small the client is.
