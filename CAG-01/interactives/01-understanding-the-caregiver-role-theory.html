Understanding the Caregiver Role

What Does a Caregiver Actually Do?

A caregiver's day-to-day work is varied and built around one client's changing needs: personal and household care, health monitoring and reporting, dietary support, mobility assistance, and emotional and psychosocial support, alongside simple companionship. Every unit later in this course builds one of these pieces in depth — this lesson is the map before the journey.

Why Caregiving Matters

Aging populations and rising care needs mean more people rely on skilled caregivers than ever, and many families simply cannot provide full-time care alone. Done well, caregiving protects a client's dignity, safety, and quality of life at a vulnerable time in their lives — and it is also a genuine career, opening real employment opportunities, including placements abroad.

Scope and Boundaries of the Role

A caregiver's job is to observe, support, and refer — not to diagnose conditions or prescribe treatment. Recognising this boundary early protects both the caregiver and the client, and it's a principle Njeri will see applied again and again throughout this course, in first aid, dietary care, psychosocial support, and special needs care alike.

Key Point. Whenever in doubt, a caregiver's safest and most professional response is: notice it, record it, and tell someone qualified — never guess or act alone on a medical judgement call.

What Makes a Good Caregiver

Patience, respect for the client's dignity and independence, confidentiality, reliability, and a genuine willingness to keep learning are what separate competent, professional caregiving from simply "helping out." These qualities matter as much as any specific skill covered later in this course.
