Module 2 · Lesson 2.6

Bed Making

Beds and Bedding

A typical care setup uses a mattress protector, bottom sheet, top sheet, blanket or duvet, and pillow with pillowcase; some beds also use a draw sheet for easier repositioning of a client who has limited mobility. Knowing each piece and its purpose is the starting point before making any bed.

The Bed-Making Procedure

Bed making follows a clear order: strip and check the mattress, fit the bottom sheet snugly with mitred corners so it doesn't wrinkle under the client, position the draw sheet if used, add the top sheet and blanket with enough tuck-in to stay secure but not so tight it restricts movement, and finish with pillows plumped and cases fitted cleanly.

Key Point. A wrinkled or poorly tucked sheet isn't just untidy — for a client who spends long hours in bed, it's a real pressure-sore risk.

Decorating Guest Beds

For a guest or presentation-style bed, a few simple touches — a neatly folded top fold, a bedspread, or a matching cushion — give a finished, welcoming look without changing the practical bed-making steps underneath.

Why a Well-Made Bed Matters

Beyond appearance, a properly made bed supports hygiene, since clean, dry linen reduces skin irritation and infection risk, along with comfort and safety, particularly for a client who is bedbound or spends significant time resting.
