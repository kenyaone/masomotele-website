Cleaning and Hygiene Standards

Cleaning vs Disinfecting

Cleaning physically removes dirt and some germs using soap/detergent and water. Disinfecting uses a chemical product specifically to kill germs on an already-cleaned surface. A surface can look clean but not be properly disinfected — the two steps serve different purposes and often both matter.

Key Point. A visibly clean surface is not automatically a hygienically safe one — cleaning and disinfecting are two different steps with two different purposes.

High-Touch Surfaces

Door handles, light switches, taps, and remote controls are touched constantly and deserve more frequent attention than, say, a rarely-touched shelf — hygiene effort should be proportional to how much a surface is actually touched.

Choosing Products Safely

Different cleaning products should never be mixed carelessly (bleach and ammonia-based products together can produce dangerous fumes), and products should be stored safely away from a confused client who might mistake them for something else.

Appearance Is Not the Full Picture

Simply making a room "look tidy" is different from it actually being clean and hygienic — a properly hygienic approach follows a genuine process, not just a visual impression.
