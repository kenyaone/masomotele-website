Household Safety and Hazard Prevention

Common Fall Hazards

Loose rugs, trailing cables, cluttered walkways, and wet floors are common, preventable fall hazards — particularly dangerous for an elderly or mobility-limited client, for whom a fall can have serious consequences.

Key Point. A fall that might be a minor embarrassment for a young, healthy person can mean a hip fracture and a serious decline in independence for an elderly client — the stakes are genuinely different.

Adequate Lighting

Poor lighting, especially along a route to the bathroom at night, significantly increases fall risk — night lights and clear, unobstructed pathways are simple, effective preventive measures.

Safe Storage of Hazardous Items

Cleaning chemicals, sharp objects, and small items that could be a choking risk (for a client who may be confused) should be stored securely, out of casual reach.

Proactive vs Reactive

Regularly walking through the home looking specifically for hazards — before an incident happens — is a genuinely different and more effective habit than only fixing problems after someone has already been hurt.
