Kitchen Hygiene and Food Safety

Safe Storage Temperatures

Perishable food kept at room temperature for too long allows bacteria to multiply to unsafe levels — refrigerating promptly, and not leaving cooked food out for extended periods, are basic, important habits.

Preventing Cross-Contamination

Using separate cutting boards (or thoroughly cleaning between uses) for raw meat and other foods, and washing hands between handling different food types, prevents bacteria from raw items contaminating food that will not be cooked further.

Key Point. Cross-contamination is often invisible — you cannot see or smell that bacteria have transferred from raw meat to a salad, which is exactly why the habit must be consistent rather than judged by appearance.

Checking for Spoilage

Checking expiry dates, and using smell/appearance as a secondary check, before preparing or serving food helps catch spoiled items before they cause illness — "it was probably fine yesterday" is not a reliable safety check.

Elderly Vulnerability

Elderly clients often have weaker immune systems, making foodborne illness potentially more severe for them than for a healthy younger adult — food safety in caregiving carries genuinely higher stakes than typical home cooking.
