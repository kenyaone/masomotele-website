Time Management for Daily Care Routines

Client Wellbeing Comes First

When a client needs extra time, attention, or support on a given day, housekeeping tasks should generally flex around that need — a caregiver rushing through a client's meal or conversation to "get to the laundry" has misplaced priorities.

Key Point. A perfectly clean house with a neglected, rushed client is not a successful day of caregiving — the client's wellbeing is always the actual priority.

Realistic Scheduling

Building some flexibility into a daily plan (rather than a rigid, fully-packed schedule) allows Njeri to respond to an unexpected need without everything else falling apart — a schedule with zero slack is fragile.

Communicating Expectations

If housekeeping demands genuinely exceed what is realistically achievable alongside proper care, communicating this clearly to the family/supervisor (rather than silently trying to do everything and burning out, or quietly cutting corners on care) is the honest, sustainable approach.

What Can and Cannot Wait

A spill that creates a fall hazard cannot wait; folding laundry generally can. Distinguishing genuinely urgent tasks from ones that can reasonably be deferred is a real, practical skill that keeps priorities straight under time pressure.
