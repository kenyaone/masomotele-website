Carrying Out Feeding Care Plan Interventions

Diet Counselling

Before and during feeding, Njeri briefly talks the client through what they're eating and why, particularly for a therapeutic diet — helping the client understand and accept the plan rather than feel it is simply imposed on them.

Respecting Preferences and Cultural Practices

A feeding plan must still respect the client's personal tastes, and cultural or religious food practices, such as fasting periods or foods to avoid, as long as this doesn't conflict with an identified medical need.

Key Point. Where a preference or cultural practice genuinely conflicts with a medical dietary need, Njeri raises it with the nurse or family for guidance rather than deciding alone in either direction.

Safe Food Handling

Basic safe food handling covers washing hands and surfaces before preparation, cooking food thoroughly, keeping raw and cooked food separate, and storing prepared food at a safe temperature until served — all to prevent foodborne illness, which is especially risky for frail or elderly clients.

Feeding the Client and Cleaning Up

When feeding a client directly, Njeri paces the meal to the client's own speed, especially if they have any swallowing difficulty, and stays alert for choking or discomfort. Afterward, feeding tools such as cups, spoons, or tube-feeding equipment are cleaned according to workplace procedure before their next use.
