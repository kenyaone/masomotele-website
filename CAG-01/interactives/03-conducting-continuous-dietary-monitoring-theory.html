Conducting Continuous Dietary Monitoring

Why Continuous Monitoring Matters

A feeding plan is not "set and forget" — a client's condition, appetite, and tolerance can change, so ongoing monitoring makes sure the plan is still safe and effective, and catches problems early.

Warning Signs to Reassess

Signs that call for a fresh look at the plan include vomiting, diarrhoea, abdominal distention or bloating, unexplained weight change, or a sudden drop in appetite. Any of these can mean the current plan or feeding method needs adjusting.

Escalating and Referring

A caregiver's role is to recognise and report these signs promptly to the nurse, supervisor, or family — not to change a therapeutic diet or feeding method on her own judgement.

Key Point. Never adjust a therapeutic diet, such as a diabetic or tube-feeding plan, without proper referral; the caregiver monitors and reports, then follows the guidance given.

Documentation and Reporting

Accurate, timely documentation — what was observed, when, and what was done about it — protects the client and creates a clear record for the next person providing care, using whatever recording format the workplace requires.
