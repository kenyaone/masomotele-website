Active Listening and Empathy

What Active Listening Involves

Active listening means giving full attention, reflecting back what was heard ("it sounds like you're worried about..."), and asking clarifying questions — not simply waiting silently for the other person to finish talking before responding with your own agenda.

Empathy vs Sympathy

Sympathy is feeling sorry for someone from the outside ("that's so sad"). Empathy is genuinely trying to understand and share in what the other person is feeling, from their perspective. Empathy generally feels more supportive and less distancing to the person receiving it.

Key Point. "I feel sorry for you" (sympathy) and "I can see why that would be really hard" (empathy) sound similar but land very differently — empathy connects, sympathy can feel like being looked down on.

Non-Verbal Cues

Body language, tone of voice, and eye contact often communicate as much as words — leaning in, nodding, and a warm tone signal genuine attention, while crossed arms, a distracted glance at a phone, or a flat tone undermine even well-intentioned words.

Resist Rushing to Fix

When a client shares a difficult feeling, the instinct to immediately offer a solution ("just think positive!") can feel dismissive — often, simply being heard and acknowledged matters more in the moment than being given an instant fix.
