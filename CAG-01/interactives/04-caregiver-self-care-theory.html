Caregiver Self-Care

Why Caregiver Self-Care Matters

Caregiving is physically and emotionally demanding, and a caregiver who is exhausted, unwell, or burnt out cannot provide safe, attentive care. Self-care is not a luxury — it's part of doing the job well and sustainably.

Self-Evaluation

A simple daily checklist — how did I sleep, eat, and feel today; what went well; what was hard — helps a caregiver notice patterns before they become burnout. Honestly recognising personal strengths and weaknesses, and setting limits on what she can realistically take on, protects both Njeri and the clients she cares for.

Key Point. Recognising she needs a break isn't a failure — it's part of responsible caregiving.

Personal Hygiene

Just as Njeri supports clients' hygiene, her own — regular handwashing, clean uniform or clothing, adequate rest — protects her own health and reduces the risk of spreading infection between clients.

Recreational Practices

Deliberately making time for activities that recharge her, whether that's exercise, socialising, a hobby, or simple rest, helps Njeri manage stress over the long term rather than only reacting once she's already overwhelmed.
