Cultural Sensitivity in Care

"Normal" Varies

What feels like a "normal" way to eat, greet someone, discuss illness, or involve family in decisions varies significantly across cultures and individual families — assuming everyone shares Njeri's own default expectations can unintentionally cause offence or discomfort.

Ask, Don't Assume

Directly and respectfully asking a client or family about specific preferences (rather than guessing based on stereotypes or assumptions) is more accurate and more respectful — "how would you like me to address you?" is a simple, useful habit.

Key Point. A respectful question about preference will almost always be better received than a confident but wrong assumption.

Religious and Dietary Practices

Fasting periods, specific dietary restrictions, prayer times, and particular customs around illness or death should be genuinely accommodated where possible, not treated as an inconvenience to be minimised.

Respecting vs Personally Agreeing

Njeri does not need to personally share or agree with every value or practice a client holds — professional respect means accommodating and not judging, which is a different thing from personal agreement.
