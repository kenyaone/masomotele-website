Recognising and Responding to Abuse or Neglect

Types of Abuse and Neglect

Abuse can be physical (unexplained injuries), emotional (belittling, isolating), financial (unauthorised use of a client's money or assets), or take the form of neglect (failure to provide adequate food, hygiene, or medical care). All forms are serious, not just physical injury.

Warning Signs

Unexplained bruises or injuries, sudden unexplained withdrawal or fearfulness (especially around a specific person), poor hygiene despite adequate resources, or unusual financial transactions are all warning signs worth taking seriously, even if any single sign alone has an innocent explanation.

Key Point. One warning sign alone might have an innocent explanation — but a pattern of several signs together deserves serious attention, not dismissal.

Duty to Report

Njeri has both an ethical and often legal duty to report suspected abuse or neglect to the appropriate authority or supervisor — this is not optional or a matter of personal discretion once genuine suspicion exists.

Reporting Despite Uncertainty

"I might be wrong" is not a valid reason to stay silent — reporting a genuine, reasonable suspicion (even if it later turns out to be unfounded) is the responsible action; the appropriate authorities, not Njeri alone, are responsible for investigating and confirming.
