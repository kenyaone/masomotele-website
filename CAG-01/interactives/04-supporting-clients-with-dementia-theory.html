Supporting Clients with Dementia

Meeting the Client in Their Reality

A client with dementia who believes a deceased spouse is still alive, or insists it is a different year, is often better supported by gently redirecting rather than repeatedly correcting/arguing — insisting on "the facts" can cause real distress without changing the underlying belief.

Key Point. The goal is comfort and connection, not winning an argument about facts the client can no longer reliably process.

Simple, Clear Communication

Short sentences, one instruction at a time, and a calm, unhurried tone are generally easier for a client with dementia to process than long explanations or multiple stacked requests at once.

Managing Repeated Questions

Answering the same question calmly for the fifth time in an hour, rather than showing visible frustration, respects that the client is not being difficult on purpose — the memory difficulty itself is the underlying cause, not defiance.

Recognising Triggers for Agitation

Loud noises, sudden changes in routine, or feeling rushed can trigger agitation in a client with dementia. Noticing these patterns and adjusting the environment or pace proactively is often more effective than only reacting after agitation has already begun.
