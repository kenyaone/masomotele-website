Know Your Rights as a Migrant Caregiver

The Right to Keep Your Documents

In most legitimate arrangements, a migrant worker has the right to hold their own passport and identity documents — an employer confiscating and refusing to return them (beyond a brief, agreed administrative purpose) is a serious warning sign, sometimes associated with forced labour situations.

Key Point. Document confiscation without a legitimate, brief, agreed reason is one of the clearest warning signs of a genuinely exploitative situation.

Rest Days and Working Hours

Most destination countries have legal requirements for rest days and maximum working hours for domestic/care workers — a contract or employer that ignores these entirely is not operating within the law, regardless of what was verbally agreed.

Timely Payment

Payment should arrive on the agreed schedule and in the agreed amount — repeated "delays" or unexplained deductions beyond what was contractually agreed are a genuine problem worth addressing, not something to quietly accept indefinitely.

Getting Help

The home country's embassy (specifically the labour attaché), local worker-support organisations, or in urgent cases, local emergency services, are appropriate resources if Njeri's basic rights are being violated — she does not have to handle a serious violation alone or in silence.
