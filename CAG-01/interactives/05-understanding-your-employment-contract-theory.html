Understanding Your Employment Contract

Key Contract Sections

A legitimate employment contract should clearly state: salary and payment schedule, working hours and rest days, job duties, contract duration, accommodation/food arrangements, and the process for termination or contract renewal.

Read Before Signing

A verbal summary from a recruiter ("don't worry, it's the standard terms") is not a substitute for actually reading the full written contract — verbal promises that are not written down are not enforceable if a dispute arises later.

Key Point. If it is not written in the contract, it effectively does not exist as far as enforcement is concerned — a friendly verbal assurance is not the same as a contractual term.

Recognising Red Flags

Vague or missing salary details, an agency demanding upfront "fees" before any job placement, a contract in a language Njeri cannot read with no translation offered, or pressure to sign immediately without time to review are all serious red flags.

Getting Help

A labour attaché at the destination country's embassy, a recognised recruitment agency verification service, or a trusted legal aid organisation can help review or clarify unclear contract terms before Njeri signs — asking for help is not a sign of weakness, it is due diligence.
