Burns and Poisoning: Immediate Response

Minor Burns

For a minor burn, cooling the area under cool (not ice-cold) running water for around 10-20 minutes, then covering loosely with a clean, non-fluffy dressing, is the standard immediate response.

Key Point. Common household remedies like applying butter, toothpaste, or oil to a burn can trap heat in and worsen the injury — cool running water is the correct, evidence-based response.

When a Burn Needs Emergency Care

A burn that is large, deep, on the face/hands/joints, or caused by chemicals or electricity needs emergency medical attention — cooling with water is still the correct immediate first step while arranging that care.

Suspected Poisoning

For suspected poisoning, the immediate response is to try to identify what was ingested (keeping any packaging/container if possible) and contact emergency services or poison control for guidance — never inducing vomiting unless specifically instructed by a medical professional, since some substances cause more harm coming back up.

Key Point. Never induce vomiting for suspected poisoning unless a medical professional specifically instructs it — this is a common, dangerous myth.
