Module 6 · Lesson 6.2

Choking Response

Mild vs Severe Choking

A client who can still cough forcefully or speak has a mild airway obstruction — encouraging them to keep coughing is usually the right response. A client who cannot cough, speak, or breathe at all has a severe obstruction requiring immediate action.

Key Point. The single most important distinguishing question is: can they still cough or make sound? That answer changes everything about what happens next.

Responding to Mild Choking

For mild choking, encourage continued coughing and stay with the client, watching closely in case it worsens to severe — do not intervene physically while an effective cough is still clearing the obstruction on its own.

Responding to Severe Choking

For severe choking (no cough, no sound, difficulty breathing), immediate intervention — such as back blows and abdominal thrusts, following recognised technique — and calling for emergency help are both required without delay.

Key Point. This lesson tests recognising severity and the correct decision sequence — the certifying hands-on technique is assessed in the practicum.
