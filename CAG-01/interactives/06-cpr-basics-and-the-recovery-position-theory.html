CPR Basics and the Recovery Position

When CPR Is Needed

CPR is needed when a client is unresponsive AND not breathing normally (or breathing only with occasional, abnormal gasps). It is a response to a life-threatening emergency where the heart has likely stopped effectively pumping.

When the Recovery Position Is Used

If a client is unresponsive but IS breathing normally, the recovery position (on their side, allowing fluids to drain and keeping the airway open) is the correct response instead of CPR — CPR on someone whose heart is already beating and who is breathing normally is not appropriate.

Key Point. CPR and the recovery position are for two different situations — using the wrong one at the wrong time is itself a mistake worth understanding clearly.

Continuing Until Help Arrives

Once CPR begins, it should continue until emergency responders arrive and take over, the client shows clear signs of recovery, or the rescuer is physically unable to continue — stopping partway through, for reasons other than these, undermines the entire effort.
