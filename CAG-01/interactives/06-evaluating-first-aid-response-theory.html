Evaluating First Aid Response

Evaluating the Casualty's Response

After giving first aid, Njeri checks whether the casualty's condition has improved, stayed the same, or worsened — is bleeding controlled, is breathing steady, is the casualty more alert or less. This evaluation guides whether further action or urgent referral is needed.

Evaluating the First Aider's Own Response

Just as important is honestly reviewing her own actions — did she follow the right steps, was anything delayed or missed, what would she do differently next time. This isn't about blame; it's how a first aider genuinely improves.

Referral Procedure

Where a casualty needs further medical attention, Njeri follows the referral procedure: documenting what happened and what was done, then formally handing over the casualty and that information to medical personnel or the next responsible caregiver.

Key Point. A rushed or incomplete handover can cost the next responder valuable time — clear, accurate information matters as much as the first aid itself.
