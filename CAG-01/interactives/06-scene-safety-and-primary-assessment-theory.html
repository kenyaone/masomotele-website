Module 6 · Lesson 6.1

Scene Safety and Primary Assessment

Scene Safety First

Before approaching any client in an emergency, Njeri must check the scene is safe — a wet floor, a broken step, an aggressive dog, or a gas smell can turn one casualty into two. Helping nobody is better than becoming a second casualty yourself.

Key Point. A caregiver who is injured helping unsafely cannot help anyone — checking the scene takes seconds and is never optional.

The Primary Assessment

Once the scene is safe, the primary assessment checks, in order: is the client responsive (can they answer, do they respond to a tap and shout)? Is the airway open and clear? Are they breathing normally? This order matters — later steps are meaningless if an earlier one reveals a life-threatening problem.

When to Call for Help First

For an unresponsive adult who is not breathing normally, calling emergency services (or shouting for someone else to call while you begin care) happens immediately, in parallel with care — not after you have already tried everything yourself. Delaying the call to "try a bit longer alone" costs precious time.

Key Point. This lesson tests scenario judgement and sequence, not your physical execution — the certifying practicum session covers the hands-on part.
