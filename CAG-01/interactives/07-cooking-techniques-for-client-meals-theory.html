Cooking Techniques for Client Meals

Methods That Naturally Soften Food

Steaming, braising, and slow-cooking naturally produce softer textures than frying or grilling — useful for clients needing modified textures without resorting only to blending everything, which some clients find unappealing.

Flavouring Without Salt and Sugar

Herbs, spices, garlic, onion, lemon juice, and vinegar can add genuine flavour without relying heavily on salt or sugar — important for clients on low-sodium or diabetic diets who still deserve genuinely tasty, satisfying meals, not just bland "diet food".

Key Point. A restricted diet does not have to mean a flavourless one — thoughtful use of herbs and acidity can make a genuinely low-sodium or low-sugar meal still taste good.

Adapting Family Recipes

A familiar, comforting family recipe can often be adapted (reducing salt, modifying texture, adjusting portion size) to fit a client's dietary needs while keeping it recognisable and appealing — full replacement with generic "diet meals" is not always necessary.

Batch Cooking and Safe Portioning

Preparing and safely portioning meals in advance for the week (with proper cooling, labelling, and storage) saves time while still requiring the same food safety diligence as same-day cooking — batch cooking convenience does not reduce food safety requirements.
