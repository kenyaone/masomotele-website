Module 7 · Lesson 7.3

Food Safety and Hygiene in Meal Preparation

Handwashing During Prep

Washing hands before starting, and again after handling raw ingredients (especially raw meat), before touching other foods or utensils, is a basic but essential habit throughout meal preparation, not just a single step at the very start.

Safe Cooking Temperatures

Different foods have different minimum safe internal cooking temperatures to kill harmful bacteria — undercooking, particularly poultry or ground meat, is a genuine foodborne illness risk regardless of how the food looks on the outside.

Key Point. Food looking cooked on the outside does not guarantee it has reached a safe internal temperature — appearance alone is not a reliable safety check for meat.

Cooling and Reheating Leftovers

Cooling leftovers promptly (not leaving them out for hours) and reheating them thoroughly before serving again reduces the risk of bacterial growth that can occur during improper storage or slow cooling.

Consistency Matters

Following food safety habits carefully most of the time, but skipping them "just this once" when rushed, meaningfully reduces the real protection these habits are meant to provide — consistency is what actually keeps a client safe over time.
