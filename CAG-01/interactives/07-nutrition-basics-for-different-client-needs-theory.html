Nutrition Basics for Different Client Needs

Macronutrients

Protein supports muscle maintenance and repair (especially important for elderly clients at risk of muscle loss), carbohydrates provide primary energy, and fats support hormone function and nutrient absorption — a balanced diet includes all three in appropriate proportions.

Needs Change with Age and Condition

An elderly client often needs more protein relative to calories than a younger adult (to help preserve muscle mass), while a client with diabetes needs careful attention to carbohydrate type and timing — nutritional needs are not one-size-fits-all across a caregiver's client list.

Hydration

Elderly clients often have a reduced sense of thirst, making them prone to dehydration without deliberate reminders and easy access to fluids — dehydration can worsen confusion, cause falls, and lead to other real health problems.

Key Point. Not feeling thirsty does not mean a client is adequately hydrated — for many elderly clients, thirst is an unreliable signal, which is exactly why proactive reminders matter.

Following the Actual Dietary Plan

A client's specific dietary plan (from a doctor or dietitian) should be followed as written, rather than Njeri guessing or improvising based on general nutrition knowledge alone — the plan accounts for specific medical conditions she may not fully know.
