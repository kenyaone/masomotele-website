Assistive Devices and Equipment

Common Categories of Assistive Devices

Devices commonly encountered include mobility aids (wheelchairs, walkers, canes), communication aids (speech-generating devices, picture boards), hearing aids, and daily-living aids (adapted utensils, dressing aids) — each serving a specific, real function for that client.

Checking Equipment Condition

A quick check that a wheelchair's brakes work, a walker's legs are stable, or a hearing aid has working batteries — before relying on the equipment — prevents a malfunction becoming a safety incident.

Key Point. Equipment that looked fine yesterday can develop a fault overnight — a quick check before use, every time, is a small habit that prevents real incidents.

Not Substituting Improvised Alternatives

Using a regular chair instead of a properly fitted wheelchair, or skipping a prescribed hearing aid because "they seem to hear well enough", substitutes an improvised guess for equipment that was specifically assessed and fitted for that client's actual needs.

Respecting Independence with Equipment

Many clients can and prefer to operate their own equipment (wheeling their own wheelchair, using their own communication device) — stepping in to "help" when it is not wanted can undermine hard-won independence rather than support it.
