Conducting Psychosocial Support for Special Needs Clients

Psychosocial Support in Special Needs Care

Clients receiving special needs care, whether geriatric, terminally ill, mentally ill, disabled, or a child, often face psychosocial challenges alongside their physical condition: isolation, frustration, anxiety, or grief connected to their situation, which deserve just as much attention as physical care.

Common Psychosocial Conditions

These can include social isolation, anxiety about the future, frustration with dependency, grief over lost abilities, or low self-esteem, each shaped by the client's specific situation and needing to be recognised rather than dismissed as "just part of" their condition.

Assessment Tools and the Support Contract

Simple tools like observation, active listening, and open questioning help Njeri understand a client's psychosocial state, and a support contract agreed with the client, and family where appropriate, sets out what psychosocial support will be provided.

Key Point. The support contract should reflect what this specific client actually wants and needs — not a generic assumption based on their condition.

Support Activities and Reporting

Activities include companionship, encouraging social connection, involving family or support groups, and simply listening without judgement, with a report kept noting the support given and the client's response over time.
