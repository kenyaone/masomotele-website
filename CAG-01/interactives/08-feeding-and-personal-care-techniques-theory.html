Module 8 · Lesson 8.5

Feeding and Personal Care Techniques

Safe Feeding Positioning

A client should generally be sitting as upright as possible during feeding (not lying flat), which significantly reduces the risk of choking or aspiration (food/liquid entering the airway) — positioning is a genuine safety measure, not just comfort.

Key Point. Feeding a client who is lying flat or slumped is a real, avoidable choking/aspiration risk — proper positioning before feeding begins is a genuine safety step, not an optional nicety.

Pacing Meals Appropriately

Rushing a client through a meal, offering the next bite before they have finished swallowing, increases choking risk and can feel disrespectful — mealtimes should move at a pace that matches the client's actual capability, not the caregiver's schedule.

Dignity and Privacy in Personal Care

Personal care tasks (bathing, toileting, dressing) should be done with genuine attention to privacy (closing doors/curtains, covering the client appropriately) and respectful communication throughout — these are inherently vulnerable moments for the client.

Encouraging Remaining Independence

Allowing a client to do what they genuinely still can do themselves (holding their own spoon, washing their own face) — even if it takes longer than doing it for them — supports their dignity and helps maintain their remaining abilities over time.
