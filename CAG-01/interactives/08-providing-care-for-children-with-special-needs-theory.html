Providing Care for Children with Special Needs

Understanding Special Needs in Children

Special needs refers to a child requiring additional support because of a physical, developmental, learning, or behavioural condition, affecting how they learn, communicate, or move, but never their right to respectful, patient care.

Types of Special Needs

Common examples include physical disabilities, developmental delays, learning difficulties, speech or communication challenges, and conditions affecting behaviour or attention, each requiring a different, individualised approach rather than a single method for every child.

Care Activities

Care activities are matched to the specific child — this might include extra patience and simple, clear communication, support with mobility or daily tasks, encouraging play appropriate to the child's abilities, and close coordination with parents and specialists involved in the child's care.

Key Point. Every child with special needs is different — the plan follows the child, not the other way round.

Referral

Where Njeri notices a new concern, a skill regression, or something outside her scope, she reports it promptly to the parents and the relevant specialist involved in the child's care.
