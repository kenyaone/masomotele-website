Providing Care for Terminally Ill Clients

Assessing the Client's Condition

Caring for a terminally ill client starts with careful observation of changes in comfort, pain, appetite, mobility, and emotional state, communicated clearly to the wider care team rather than assessed in isolation.

Terminal Ill Care Activities

Care activities focus on comfort, dignity, and quality of life: gentle personal care, pain and symptom reporting, companionship, and supporting the client's emotional and spiritual needs, alongside their family.

Key Point. The goal shifts from recovery to comfort and dignity — every care activity should support that, not work against it.

Carrying Out Care Appropriately

Njeri carries out care activities gently and respectfully, following the care plan set by the medical team, always prioritising the client's comfort and wishes, and involving family in a way the client is comfortable with.

Reporting and Referral

Careful documentation of the client's condition and any changes is essential, and any new symptom, significant pain, or distress is referred promptly to the nurse, doctor, or hospice team — this is medical territory Njeri supports but does not manage alone.
