Module 8 · Lesson 8.3

Safe Lifting, Transfers and Mobility Assistance

Body Mechanics Protect Both People

Using proper posture and technique when lifting or assisting a client protects Njeri's own back and joints from injury, while also protecting the client from being dropped or moved unsafely — this is a two-way safety concern, not just about the caregiver.

Key Point. A caregiver injured through poor lifting technique cannot safely help that client (or any other) afterward — protecting your own body is part of protecting the client too.

Assess Before Attempting

Before any transfer, checking the client's current strength, alertness, and cooperation level (rather than assuming it will go the same as last time) helps Njeri decide whether to proceed alone, get help, or use additional equipment.

Using Mobility Aids Properly

Skipping a prescribed mobility aid (a transfer belt, a hoist) "just this once to save time" removes a genuine safety measure — the aid exists because it meaningfully reduces risk, not as an optional convenience.

Knowing When to Get a Second Person

Some transfers, based on the client's weight, condition, or level of cooperation, genuinely require two people to be done safely — attempting a two-person transfer alone to save time or avoid asking for help is a real, avoidable risk.
