Handling and Storing Medical Supplies

Reading Labels Carefully

Medication labels specify dose, timing, and special instructions ("take with food", "avoid alcohol") — following them precisely, rather than approximating from memory, is essential, since small deviations can matter significantly for some medications.

Proper Storage

Some medications need refrigeration, others need to be kept away from direct light or heat, and virtually all should be stored out of reach of children or confused clients who might take them accidentally. Storage instructions on the label or packaging should always be followed.

Checking Expiry Dates

Expired medication may be less effective or, in some cases, unsafe — checking expiry dates before administering anything, and flagging expired items for proper disposal rather than use, is a basic but essential habit.

Key Point. An expired medication is not automatically "close enough" to still work properly — expiry dates exist for a real pharmacological reason.

When Unsure

If Njeri is ever unsure about a medication, dosage, or storage instruction, the correct response is to ask a pharmacist, nurse, or the prescribing doctor — never guess, and never proceed on assumption when client safety is at stake.
