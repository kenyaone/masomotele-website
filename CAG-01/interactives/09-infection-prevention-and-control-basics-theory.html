Infection Prevention and Control Basics

Hand Hygiene

Washing hands (or using alcohol-based hand sanitiser) before and after any client contact, before eating, and after using the toilet is the single most effective infection-control habit available — simple, and backed by strong evidence.

Key Point. Hand hygiene is unglamorous but is consistently ranked as the single most effective infection-control measure available to any caregiver.

Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

Gloves are used when contact with bodily fluids, wounds, or broken skin is likely. A mask may be appropriate when a client has a respiratory illness, or when Njeri herself is unwell and must still provide essential care. PPE protects both directions — caregiver to client and client to caregiver.

Safe Handling and Disposal

Used gloves, dressings, and other contaminated materials should be disposed of in a designated waste container, not general household rubbish — reducing the risk of anyone else being exposed to contaminated material afterward.

Consistency Over Occasional Perfection

Doing infection control perfectly once a week but skipping it "just this once" the rest of the time provides far less real protection than consistently doing the basics every single time — habit and consistency matter more than occasional thoroughness.
