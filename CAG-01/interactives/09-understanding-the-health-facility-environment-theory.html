Understanding the Health Facility Environment

Common Departments

Most health facilities have a reception/records area for check-in, an outpatient department for routine appointments, and often a pharmacy for dispensing medication. Knowing this basic layout helps Njeri guide an anxious or confused client confidently.

Recognising Staff Roles

Different uniforms and titles indicate different roles — a nurse typically manages day-to-day care and monitoring, a doctor makes diagnoses and treatment decisions, and reception/records staff handle appointments and paperwork. Knowing who to approach for what saves time and reduces confusion.

Caregiver Etiquette at a Facility

As a caregiver accompanying a client, Njeri should let the client answer questions directed at them when able, only stepping in to clarify or assist when genuinely needed, and always identify her role clearly to staff (e.g. "I am her caregiver, not a family member").

Key Point. Speaking over a client who is capable of answering for themselves undermines their dignity and independence — a caregiver supports, rather than takes over.

Reducing Client Anxiety

Many elderly or unwell clients feel anxious about facility visits. Briefly explaining what to expect beforehand — "we will check in at reception, then wait to see the nurse" — helps reduce that anxiety through familiarity.
