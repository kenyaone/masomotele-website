Verifying AI Health Information Before Acting On It

Confident but Wrong

An AI assistant can describe an incorrect health fact in exactly the same confident, clear tone as a correct one — there is no built-in "warning tone" for a wrong answer, which is exactly why health information deserves extra scrutiny before acting on it.

Key Point. A confident-sounding health answer from an AI tool is not the same thing as a medically verified one — these are two separate qualities entirely.

Cross-Checking with a Professional

For anything that could actually affect how Njeri cares for a client (a symptom's significance, whether a reaction needs urgent attention), verifying with a nurse, doctor, or supervisor before acting is essential — an AI assistant's general answer is not a substitute for this real, specific verification.

When to Just Ask a Person Instead

For anything urgent, high-stakes, or genuinely uncertain, going directly to a nurse or supervisor is faster and safer than researching alone first — AI-assisted research is better suited to general learning and preparation than to real-time, high-stakes decisions.

Building Confidence Without Over-Relying

Using AI tools to learn general concepts and build background understanding over time is valuable — but genuine caregiving confidence should ultimately rest on trained judgement and professional verification, not on repeated reliance on an AI assistant's answers for real decisions.
