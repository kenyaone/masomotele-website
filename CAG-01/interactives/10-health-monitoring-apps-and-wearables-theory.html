Health Monitoring Apps and Wearables

What They Can Track

Consumer wearables and apps can reasonably track general trends — daily step counts, resting heart rate patterns, sleep duration — useful as a general indicator of activity level and rough pattern changes over time.

What They Cannot Reliably Do

Consumer-grade devices are generally not medical diagnostic tools — they cannot reliably diagnose a specific medical condition, and their readings (especially things like blood oxygen or irregular heart rhythm alerts) can have real inaccuracies that a genuine clinical device would not.

Key Point. A consumer wearable can raise a helpful flag worth investigating — it cannot replace an actual clinical assessment or diagnosis.

Concerning Reading vs Normal Variability

Some fluctuation in these readings is normal day to day — a single unusual reading is not automatically an emergency, but a clear, sustained pattern change (persistently low activity, a notably different resting heart rate over several days) is worth taking seriously.

Report, Don't Self-Diagnose

If a wearable shows a concerning pattern, Njeri's role is to report it to the appropriate person (nurse, doctor, supervisor) for proper clinical evaluation — not to draw her own medical conclusions from a consumer device's data and act on that alone.
