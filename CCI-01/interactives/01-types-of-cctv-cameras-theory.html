Types of CCTV Cameras

Dome Cameras

A dome camera sits inside a rounded, dome-shaped housing, usually mounted flush against a ceiling. Because the tinted dome cover hides which direction the lens is actually pointing, people in the room cannot easily tell if or where the camera is looking — this makes dome cameras a discreet, low-profile choice for indoor spaces.

Most dome housings are compact and blend into a shop or office ceiling, and many are built with vandal-resistant covers for areas where a camera might otherwise be tampered with.

Bullet Cameras

A bullet camera has a long, cylindrical body with the lens fixed at one end, usually wall or pole mounted so it points clearly in one obvious direction. That visibility is a feature, not a flaw: a bullet camera is easy to spot, which makes it an effective visual deterrent at entrances, gates, and perimeters.

Bullet housings are commonly weatherproofed for outdoor use and often include built-in infrared (IR) illuminators for night-time recording over a fairly long, straight-line range.

PTZ (Pan-Tilt-Zoom) Cameras

A PTZ camera is motorised: it can pan side to side, tilt up and down, and zoom in to bring distant detail closer, either driven live by an operator or moved automatically between preset positions on a patrol schedule.

This flexibility makes PTZ cameras well suited to large open areas — parking lots, warehouse floors, wide perimeters — where one camera needs to cover much more ground than a fixed dome or bullet ever could. The trade-off is cost and complexity: a PTZ only shows one part of the scene in detail at a time, so it works best with either an active operator or carefully planned preset tours.

Key Point. Match the camera to the use case, not the other way round: a discreet dome for a shop interior where you do not want the camera to stand out, a visible bullet for a gate or entrance where a clear deterrent matters, and a PTZ for a large open area that needs active, flexible monitoring.
