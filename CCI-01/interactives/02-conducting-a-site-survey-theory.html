Conducting a Site Survey

Why the Site Survey Comes First

At SafeView Security Systems, Dennis never quotes a CCTV job over the phone. Every installation starts with a visit to the client's premises, because a floor plan or a phone description never tells the whole story — the number of entrances, the layout of shelving, the height of the ceiling, and the way light falls through the windows all matter, and they can only be seen in person.

Skipping the survey means guessing. Guessing means either under-specifying the system (leaving real gaps in coverage) or over-specifying it (quoting cameras the client does not need). A proper survey protects both the client and SafeView's reputation.

What to Look For During the Walk-through

Dennis walks the entire property, inside and outside, looking specifically for: blind spots (corners, stockrooms, side doors that cannot be seen from a central position), entry and exit points (main doors, fire exits, loading bays, windows at ground level), lighting conditions at different times of day (a well-lit shop floor can still have a pitch-dark car park at night), and existing infrastructure such as old alarm cabling, spare power sockets, or an existing network switch that could be reused.

Key Point. A site survey is not a sales pitch — it is data gathering. Every camera decision Dennis makes later depends entirely on getting this walk-through right.

Recording What You Find

Notes in Dennis's head are notes that get forgotten. He sketches a rough floor plan, marks each entrance and blind spot on it, takes photos of tricky corners and existing wiring, and writes down lighting conditions for both day and night visits where possible. This record becomes the foundation for the actual camera layout and the quote — nothing gets designed from memory.
