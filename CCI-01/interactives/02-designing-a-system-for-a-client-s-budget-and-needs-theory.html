Designing a System for a Client's Budget and Needs

Understanding the Client's Real Risk and Needs

A small corner shop with low footfall and modest stock has very different needs from a warehouse holding expensive equipment overnight. Dennis always asks: what is actually at risk here, and how likely is it? A small shop may only need solid coverage of the till and front door, while a warehouse with high-value stock and after-hours access may justify a far more comprehensive system.

Matching Equipment to Budget

Budget decisions involve real trade-offs: fewer, well-placed cameras covering the highest-priority areas versus many cameras spread thinly; higher resolution with shorter storage retention versus lower resolution with a longer recording history; wired systems that are more reliable versus wireless options that are faster and cheaper to install. Dennis lays these trade-offs out clearly so the client can make an informed choice rather than simply picking the most expensive option out of uncertainty.

Key Point. The best design is the one that matches the client's actual risk and budget — not the one with the most line items on the invoice.

Avoiding Overselling

It can be tempting to push the priciest package to boost a sale, but SafeView's reputation is built on honest recommendations. If a small shop does not need a week of 4K footage retention, Dennis says so plainly and explains why a smaller, well-targeted system serves them just as well. Clients who feel they were sold exactly what they needed — no more, no less — are the ones who come back and refer others.
