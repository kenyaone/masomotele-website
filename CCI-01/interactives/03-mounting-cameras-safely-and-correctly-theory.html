Unit 3 · Topic 3.1

Mounting Cameras Safely and Correctly

Choosing a Secure Mounting Surface

Before Dennis drills a single hole, he checks what he is mounting into. Solid masonry, brick, and concrete give the strongest hold and are always preferred for outdoor cameras. Hollow surfaces like drywall, thin timber cladding, or old, softened fascia boards will not support a camera's weight over time, especially once wind and vibration are added.

If the only available surface is weak, the fix is not to skip the mount — it is to add backing. A short length of timber batten fixed into a solid structure behind a weak panel gives the screws something solid to bite into. Dennis always gives the bracket a firm test tug by hand before trusting it with an expensive camera.

Weatherproofing Outdoor Cameras

Outdoor cameras are only as weatherproof as their weakest entry point. Most of the water damage SafeView Security Systems sees on service calls is not the camera housing failing — it is water finding its way in through the cable entry hole at the back of the mounting bracket.

Dennis always feeds the cable in through the base of the bracket where possible, seals any drilled entry hole with silicone sealant, and leaves a small drip loop in the cable before it enters the housing, so any water running down the cable drips off instead of running straight into the gland.

Key Point. A drip loop and a bead of silicone sealant around the cable entry cost seconds to add, but they are often the difference between a camera lasting years outdoors and one that fails after the first heavy rains.

Ladder and Working-at-Height Safety

Most CCTV mounting happens above head height, so ladder safety is part of the job, not an afterthought. Dennis always uses the 4-to-1 rule: for every 4 units of height, the base of the ladder sits 1 unit away from the wall — steep enough to be stable, not so steep that it tips backward.

Before climbing, he checks the ladder is on firm, level ground, has someone footing it or is tied off where possible, and that he is not overreaching to one side. If a fitting point is out of comfortable reach, he climbs down and repositions the ladder rather than stretching for it.
