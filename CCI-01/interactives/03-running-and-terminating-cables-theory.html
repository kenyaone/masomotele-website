Unit 3 · Topic 3.2

Running and Terminating Cables

Routing Cable the Right Way

How a cable is routed matters as much as how it is terminated. Every cable type has a minimum bend radius — bend it tighter than that around a corner or through a tight conduit fitting, and the copper core or coax shield can crack or thin internally, causing signal problems that only show up weeks later.

Dennis plans a route before pulling any cable: gentle curves around corners, no cable resting directly on the sharp edges of metal trunking, and enough slack left at both ends to allow re-termination later without shortening the run.

Avoiding Interference

CCTV cable, especially unshielded runs, can pick up electrical noise from nearby sources — mains power cables, fluorescent light fittings and their ballasts, and large motors like those in air-conditioning units. That noise can show up as flickering, banding, or static in the video image.

Where CCTV cable must cross a power cable, Dennis crosses it at a right angle rather than running parallel alongside it, and keeps at least 30 cm of separation where the two run in the same direction — more if it is near lighting ballasts or motors.

Key Point. Running data or coax cable parallel and close to a mains power cable is one of the most common causes of a "noisy" picture on site — when in doubt, add distance or cross at 90 degrees.

Terminating BNC and RJ45 Connectors

For analog cameras, Dennis terminates coax with proper crimp-style BNC connectors rather than twist-on types. A crimped connector grips the cable and shield firmly, while twist-on connectors work loose over time and cause intermittent picture loss.

For IP cameras, he terminates RJ45 plugs following the T568B wiring order, keeping the amount of untwisted cable at the very tip as short as possible, then tests every run with a cable tester before calling the job complete.
