Module 5 · Lesson 5.3

Handling Client Complaints and Service Calls

1. Listening and Responding Professionally

When a client calls in frustrated — "the camera stopped recording" — Dennis's first job is to listen fully before responding. He acknowledges the concern, thanks them for calling in, and notes down exactly what they observed, when, and on which camera or channel, without getting defensive or dismissive.

A calm, professional response at this stage builds trust, even before the technical problem is solved. Clients remember how they were treated as much as how quickly the fault was fixed.

2. Triage Questions Before a Site Visit

Before booking a site visit, Dennis asks a short set of targeted questions: Is it one camera or all of them? Did anything change recently — a power outage, an internet outage, someone unplugging or moving something during cleaning? What exactly do they see — a blank screen, a frozen image, or an error message on the app? Can they check a simple thing themselves, like whether a cable looks unplugged, while still on the phone?

3. Deciding: Remote Fix, Scheduled Visit, or Emergency Callout

Some issues are resolved with simple phone guidance — a loose power cable reseated, or a router reboot. Others genuinely need a technician on site, such as physical cable damage or a failed camera. Dennis sets clear, realistic expectations with the client about timing either way, and logs the complaint and its outcome so there is a record for SafeView.

Key Point. A few well-chosen questions on the phone can save an unnecessary site visit — but a client's concern should never be dismissed outright, even if it turns out to be something simple.
