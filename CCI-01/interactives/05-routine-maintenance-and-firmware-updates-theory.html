Module 5 · Lesson 5.2

Routine Maintenance and Firmware Updates

1. Cleaning Camera Lenses and Housings

Dust, cobwebs, rain streaks, and salt air all build up on camera lenses and housings over time, gradually reducing image sharpness and, for IR cameras, weakening night vision performance. Dennis wipes lenses and domes with a soft, dry or slightly damp microfiber cloth, and avoids harsh solvents or abrasive materials that can strip protective coatings or scratch the dome.

This should happen on a fixed schedule — for example, quarterly — rather than only after a client complains about image quality. By the time a client notices a problem, the buildup has usually been affecting the picture for weeks.

2. Checking Cable Connections and Physical Condition

On every maintenance visit, Dennis re-seats connectors, checks for corrosion or water ingress at outdoor camera housings, and confirms mounting brackets are still tight — a loose bracket can slowly shift a camera's view without anyone noticing until it matters. He also looks for signs of pest or rodent damage on exposed cable runs, which is a surprisingly common cause of later faults.

3. Firmware Updates — Security and Stability

Firmware updates for cameras and recorders often patch known security vulnerabilities — the kind that attackers actively scan the internet for on outdated, unpatched devices. They also fix bugs that cause random reboots, dropped recordings, or menu glitches, keeping the system stable over the long run.

Before applying an update, Dennis always backs up the current configuration, confirms the firmware exactly matches the device model, and updates one device at a time rather than the whole system at once — so that if something goes wrong, it's easy to identify and undo.

Key Point. Skipping firmware updates does not just risk stability bugs — it leaves a known, published vulnerability open on a device that is meant to keep the client secure.
