Module 6 · Lesson 6.3

Verifying AI Recommendations Before Client Installation Changes

Why AI Suggestions Can Be Generic

An AI chat assistant is trained on a huge, general mix of information, not the exact datasheet for every camera, DVR, or switch on the market. Ask it "what's the max cable run for a PoE camera?" and it will usually give the textbook Cat5e/Cat6 Ethernet answer — around 100 metres — because that is the general standard. That answer is not wrong as a rule of thumb, but it may not match what a specific model's manual actually states, especially once PoE power draw, cable quality, or an extender is involved.

The same applies to network settings: an AI tool might suggest a plausible-sounding subnet or port setting without knowing the client's actual network layout, or that model's specific requirements.

The Cost of Getting It Wrong on a Live System

A client's CCTV system is usually already running and depended on. If Dennis applies a generic AI-suggested change — extends a cable run past what the actual equipment supports, or edits a network setting the DVR doesn't handle the way the AI assumed — the result can be cameras dropping offline, a DVR losing network access, or a return visit that costs SafeView time and the client confidence.

Small, easily-checked details (a rated distance, a supported IP range, a specific port number) are exactly the kind of thing that is quick to verify and expensive to get wrong.

Key point. Treat an AI answer as a hint about what to check — the manufacturer's manual for that exact model is the final word before you touch a client's live system.

Dennis's Verify-Before-Change Habit

Before making any change based on an AI suggestion, Dennis's habit is: find the actual manual or datasheet for that specific camera, DVR, or switch model, confirm the exact figure or setting it states, and only then apply the change to the client's system — ideally at a time that limits disruption if something still goes wrong.

If the manual and the AI's answer disagree, the manual wins. The AI answer is useful for getting him thinking in the right direction quickly; it is never the final authority on a specific piece of client equipment.
