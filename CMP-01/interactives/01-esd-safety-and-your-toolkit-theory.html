ESD Safety and Your Toolkit

Physical practicum required. This step teaches the judgement and correct sequence for a real hands-on repair task. To be certified competent in the physical technique itself (not just the decision-making), you must complete a supervised in-person practicum session — this online step alone does not certify hands-on ability.

1. What is ESD, really?

Electrostatic discharge (ESD) is a sudden flow of static electricity between two objects at different electrical potential. The human body can build up thousands of volts of static charge just from walking across a carpeted floor — far more than the roughly 100 volts needed to silently damage a modern CPU, RAM module or motherboard. Most ESD damage is invisible: the component doesn't die immediately, its lifespan is just quietly shortened.

The scary part: a static discharge below about 3,000 volts is too small for a human to even feel — but it's already enough to damage sensitive electronics.

Anti-static wrist strapClips to a grounded point (e.g. an unpainted metal part of the case); keeps your body at the same electrical potential as the components you touch.
Anti-static matA grounded work surface to rest components on.
Anti-static bagsStore spare RAM, cards and drives in these — never on a plastic table or carpet.
P

Peter's rule at Rafiki Computer Clinic: no wrist strap, no case opening — even for a 2-minute RAM check. It takes 10 seconds to clip on and it's the difference between a quick fix and an expensive mistake.

3. The core toolkit

  • Phillips-head screwdrivers (multiple sizes) — most case and component screws
  • Precision/spudger set — for prying open laptop cases and disconnecting delicate ribbon cables without cracking plastic clips
  • Thermal paste — reapplied whenever a CPU cooler is removed
  • Cable ties / velcro straps — for tidy, airflow-friendly cable management
  • Compressed air / soft brush — dust removal without static risk (never use a vacuum cleaner directly on internals — it generates static)
  • Multimeter — testing PSU output voltages, continuity checks

4. Pre-repair checklist

  1. Power off and fully unplug the machine (don't just switch off at the wall — physically unplug)
  2. Press and hold the power button for 5 seconds to discharge any residual capacitor charge
  3. Put on your anti-static wrist strap and clip it to bare metal on the case
  4. Work on an anti-static mat if available
  5. Never touch the gold connector edges of RAM or expansion cards directly
