Power Supply Units and Ratings

1. Sizing a PSU

A PSU's wattage rating is the maximum continuous power it can safely deliver. Undersizing causes instability (random shutdowns, especially under load like gaming or video editing); oversizing isn't dangerous, just slightly wasteful. A safe rule of thumb is to add up the estimated draw of every component (CPU, GPU, drives, fans) and choose a PSU with roughly 20-30% headroom above that total.

2. 80 PLUS efficiency ratings

The 80 PLUS certification measures how much of the power a PSU draws from the wall is actually delivered usefully (vs wasted as heat). Tiers, from lowest to highest efficiency: White → Bronze → Silver → Gold → Platinum → Titanium. A Gold-rated PSU wastes noticeably less power (and generates less heat) than a White-rated one at the same load — this matters for both electricity cost and internal case temperatures.

3. Voltage rails

RailWhat it powers
+12VThe heaviest load — CPU, GPU, and drive motors
+5VSome drive electronics and USB devices
+3.3VMotherboard chipset and various logic circuits
The +12V rail carries the vast majority of a modern PC's total power draw — this is why GPU and CPU power connectors are separate, dedicated cables rather than sharing the thinner rails.

4. Warning signs of a failing PSU

  • Random shutdowns or reboots, especially under load
  • A burning smell or visible discoloration around the PSU
  • Unusual coil whine or buzzing
  • System won't power on at all, but the PSU fan itself still spins (points to internal PSU failure, not necessarily the fan)
  • Failure to boot after adding a new, more power-hungry GPU
