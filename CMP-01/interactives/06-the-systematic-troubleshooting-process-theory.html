The Systematic Troubleshooting Process

1. The 6-step process

  1. Identify the problem -- get a precise description from the customer: what exactly happens, when did it start, what changed recently?
  2. Establish a theory of probable cause -- start with the simplest, most common explanation first (a loose cable is far more likely than a dead motherboard)
  3. Test the theory -- verify before you act; do not replace a part just because you suspect it
  4. Establish a plan of action -- once confirmed, decide the fix and any needed parts
  5. Implement the fix and verify full functionality -- after fixing, retest thoroughly, not just the original symptom
  6. Document findings and actions -- what was wrong, what was done, useful for warranty and future reference

2. Simplest cause first

P

Peter has a saying: check the cable before you blame the chip. The overwhelming majority of no-power, no-display and no-network complaints trace back to something simple -- a loose connector, an unplugged cable, a switched-off power strip -- not a failed component. Jumping straight to replacing parts wastes money and often does not even fix the real problem.

A good troubleshooting order moves from cheap and quick checks to expensive and slow ones: visual inspection, then cable and connection checks, then software-level checks, then component-level testing, then replacement.

3. Isolating variables

Change one thing at a time. If you swap the RAM, the GPU and the PSU all at once and the problem goes away, you genuinely do not know which one fixed it -- which means you cannot confidently diagnose the same problem faster next time, and you may have replaced parts that were never actually faulty.

4. Documentation

A simple job log -- symptom, what was tested, what was found, what was fixed -- protects you if the customer returns with a related or unrelated issue, and builds a personal reference library of common patterns over time.
