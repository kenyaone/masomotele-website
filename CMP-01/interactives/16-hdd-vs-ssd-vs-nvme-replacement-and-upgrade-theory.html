HDD vs SSD vs NVMe -- Replacement and Upgrade

Physical practicum required. This step teaches the judgement and correct sequence for a real hands-on repair task. To be certified competent in the physical technique itself (not just the decision-making), you must complete a supervised in-person practicum session — this online step alone does not certify hands-on ability.

1. The three main storage types

TypeConnectionTypical speedNotes
HDDSATA cable (data + power)Slowest, mechanical spinning plattersCheapest per gigabyte, good for bulk storage, not for the OS drive
SATA SSDSATA cable (data + power)Much faster than HDD, no moving partsA drop-in HDD replacement using the same cables and connectors
NVMe SSDM.2 slot directly on motherboard, no cablesFastest of the three by a wide marginRequires an available M.2 slot on the motherboard

2. Why SSDs are the highest-value upgrade

For an old, slow machine still running on a mechanical HDD, swapping in even a basic SATA SSD is very often the single biggest, most noticeable speed improvement available -- often more impactful than a CPU or RAM upgrade, and usually far cheaper.

3. Physical installation

SATA drive (HDD or SSD): mount in an available drive bay, connect a SATA data cable to the motherboard and a SATA power cable from the PSU.

NVMe SSD: insert at an angle into the M.2 slot, press down flat, and secure with the small retention screw. No cables at all are involved.

4. Setting the correct boot drive

After installing a new drive, especially when replacing the drive Windows was originally installed on, the BIOS boot order must be checked and set correctly, or the system may try to boot from the wrong drive, or fail to boot entirely. This is covered in more depth in the Software and Operating Systems unit.
