Backup Strategies for Client Machines

1. The 3-2-1 rule

A widely used, simple backup principle: keep at least 3 copies of important data, on 2 different types of storage media, with at least 1 copy stored somewhere off-site. This protects against drive failure, theft, fire, and simple accidental deletion all at once, since no single event can realistically wipe out every copy.

2. Local vs cloud backup

Local backup (external drive)Cloud backup
SpeedFast for large restoresLimited by internet speed
Protection from theft/fireNone, if kept in the same locationFully protected, stored off-site by design
Ongoing costOne-time hardware costUsually a recurring subscription
The strongest setup uses both: a local backup for fast, convenient restores, plus a cloud backup as genuine off-site protection against theft, fire, or a natural disaster affecting the whole premises.

3. Automatic backups

A backup strategy that depends on the customer remembering to manually run it will eventually fail -- not because the customer is careless, but because life gets busy. Peter always sets up automatic, scheduled backups wherever possible, so protection continues without requiring the customer to think about it at all.

4. Why "I have never lost data before" fails as a strategy

Every customer who has actually lost important data said the exact same thing right before it happened. Drive failure is often sudden and offers no warning beyond the SMART data covered earlier in this course -- by the time symptoms are obvious to the customer, it is frequently already too late to recover everything.
