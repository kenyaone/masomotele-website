Data Recovery Basics

1. Logical vs physical failure

Logical failurePhysical failure
What it meansThe drive hardware still works, but the file system or data structure is corrupted, or files were deletedThe drive hardware itself is physically damaged, such as a failed read head or motor
Recoverable in-shop?Often yes, with the right softwareRarely, without specialised clean-room equipment

2. What Peter can attempt in-shop

  • File recovery software, for accidentally deleted files on a functioning drive
  • Recovering data from a drive with logical file system corruption, using diagnostic and repair tools
  • Recovering data from a drive with early-stage SMART warnings but that is still readable, prioritising getting the data off immediately rather than continuing to use the drive normally

3. When to refer to a specialised lab

If a drive makes unusual clicking or grinding noises, is not detected at all by the system, or has visible physical damage, this points to physical failure. Attempting further use, including a straightforward clone, risks making the situation worse and reducing the chance of eventual recovery. These cases need a specialised data recovery lab with clean-room facilities -- genuinely beyond what a general repair shop can safely attempt.

A clicking hard drive should be powered off immediately and not powered on again casually -- every additional power-on risks further mechanical damage and reduces the odds of eventual recovery.

4. The first response to a failing drive

The moment a drive shows real signs of failure, the single most important action is to stop using it normally and prioritise getting critical data off immediately, or refer to specialists if physical failure is suspected. Continuing to use a failing drive for day-to-day work, hoping it lasts a little longer, is the single most common way customers turn a recoverable situation into a permanent data loss.
