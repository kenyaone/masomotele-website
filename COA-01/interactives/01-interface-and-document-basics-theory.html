Interface and Document Basics

The Word Screen

Word's window has three areas you will use constantly: the Ribbon at the top (tabs like Home, Insert, Layout, each holding grouped commands), the document area in the middle where you type, and the status bar at the bottom (word count, page number, zoom).

The small Quick Access Toolbar above the Ribbon holds Save, Undo, and Redo — the three commands you will click most often.

Key Point. If you cannot find a command, look for it inside a Ribbon tab first — almost everything in Word lives there.

Saving Properly

Ctrl+S (Save) writes your changes to the same file. Save As (F12) creates a new file with a new name or location — use it when you want to keep the original untouched, e.g. saving this month's price list as a copy of last month's.

Brenda at Amani Stationers lost two hours of supplier letters once because she never saved until the power went out. A document only exists safely once it has been saved to disk.

Key Point. Save early, save often — Ctrl+S costs one second and prevents hours of lost work.

AutoCorrect and Undo

Word's AutoCorrect fixes common typos automatically (teh → the) but can also "correct" things you meant, like a supplier's name. Ctrl+Z (Undo) reverses the last action — including an unwanted AutoCorrect — and Ctrl+Y (Redo) reapplies it.

Key Point. Undo works on almost anything: typing, formatting, deleting an image. When in doubt, Ctrl+Z.
