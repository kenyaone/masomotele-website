Charts

Choosing a Chart Type

A line chart suits a trend over time (sales each day this month). A bar/column chart suits comparing separate categories (sales by product type). A pie chart suits showing parts of a whole (percentage of sales per product), and works poorly with more than about 5-6 slices.

Key Point. Picking the wrong chart type (e.g. a pie chart for a monthly trend) actively confuses the reader, even with correct data.

Inserting and Editing a Chart

Select the data range (including headers), then Insert → Charts → choose a type. Clicking the chart reveals Chart Design/Format tools to add a title, label axes, and change colours. A clear title (e.g. "Amani Stationers — Daily Sales, July 2026") makes the chart understandable without extra explanation.

Charts Update Automatically

A chart stays linked to its source data — editing a number in the original range instantly updates the chart. This is why charts should be built directly from the live data range, not from a manually retyped copy.

Key Point. Never manually retype numbers just to build a chart — link it to the live data so it always reflects reality.
