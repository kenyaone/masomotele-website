Interface and Entering Data

The Grid and Cell References

A workbook is the whole file; each tab inside it is a worksheet. Every worksheet is a grid of cells, each named by its column letter and row number — B3 means column B, row 3. This reference is how formulas point at data.

Key Point. Always know the cell reference of what you are editing — formulas are built entirely from these references.

Entering Data Correctly

Excel guesses what you type: pure digits become numbers (right-aligned), text becomes a label (left-aligned), and recognisable date formats become real dates you can calculate with. Typing 0501 for a phone number can silently become the number 501 — losing the leading zero — unless the cell is formatted as text first.

Key Point. Phone numbers and reference codes with leading zeros should be entered as Text format, not left as General/Number.

Selecting Ranges and AutoFill

Click-drag selects a range like A1:A10. AutoFill (drag the small square at a selection's bottom-right corner) can continue a pattern — typing "Monday" and dragging fills "Tuesday", "Wednesday", etc.; typing "1" and "2" then dragging continues "3, 4, 5...".
