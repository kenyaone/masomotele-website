Design and Themes

What a Theme Is

A Theme (Design tab) is a coordinated bundle of colours, fonts, and slide backgrounds applied to the entire presentation at once. Switching themes instantly restyles every slide consistently — far faster than manually recolouring and refonting each slide individually.

Key Point. Apply a Theme before manually formatting individual slides — formatting on top of the wrong theme often has to be redone.

Design Ideas

PowerPoint's Design Ideas panel (Design tab) suggests polished layout variations for the current slide based on its content, automatically arranging text/images more attractively — a quick way to improve one slide without manual layout work.

Readable Design

Text should have strong contrast against its background (dark text on light background or vice versa — never similar tones). Font size should stay large enough to read from the back of a room (usually 24pt or larger for body text). A slide crammed with too much text or too many images becomes hard to read at a glance — fewer, clearer points per slide communicate better than dense paragraphs.

Key Point. If a slide needs to be read slowly to understand, it has too much on it — split it into two slides instead.
