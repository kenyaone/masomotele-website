Speaker Notes and Delivery

Speaker Notes

The Notes pane below each slide (View → Notes) holds reminders only the presenter sees — talking points, statistics to mention, or a reminder to pause for questions. Notes are never shown to the audience during a normal slideshow.

Key Point. Slides should show key points; Notes should hold the detail you plan to say out loud — keeping these separate is what stops a slide from becoming a wall of text.

Presenter View

Presenter View (Slide Show tab) shows the presenter a private screen with the current slide, the next slide, a timer, and their Speaker Notes — while the audience's screen or projector shows only the current slide, full-screen, with none of that extra information.

Rehearse Timings and Delivery

Rehearse Timings (Slide Show tab) records how long you actually spend on each slide during a practice run, helping fit a pitch into a fixed time slot. When actually presenting, looking at the audience (not reading the slide word-for-word) and pacing steadily are what keep a pitch engaging rather than a recitation.

Key Point. A slide is a prompt for the audience, not a script to read aloud — the speaker should say more than what is written on the slide.
