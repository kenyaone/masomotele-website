Interface and Publication Types

Publisher vs Word

Word is built around flowing text down a page — ideal for letters and reports. Publisher is built around free-floating design boxes (text boxes, picture boxes) placed anywhere on a page — ideal for a flyer, brochure, or business card where visual layout matters more than continuous reading text.

Key Point. Choosing Publisher over Word is a design decision: pick Publisher when a document is meant to be looked at as a layout, not read top-to-bottom as prose.

Templates

Publisher opens to a gallery of built-in templates sorted by type — Flyers, Brochures, Business Cards, Greeting Cards, and more. Starting from a matching template (e.g. "Sale Flyer") gives a professional starting layout instead of an empty page.

The Workspace and Page Size

The grey area around the white page is the scratch area — objects placed there are saved but not printed, useful for holding pieces you are still arranging. Page Design → Size lets you match the page to what will actually be printed (A5 for a small flyer, A4 for a full-page brochure).

Key Point. Always confirm the page size matches the actual paper the shop will print on before designing the whole layout around it.
