AI-Assisted Drafting in Word

Writing a Clear Prompt

A vague prompt like "write a letter" produces generic, unusable text. A clear prompt states the audience (a wholesale customer), the purpose (payment reminder), the tone (polite but firm), and any specific facts to include (amount owed, due date) — the more specific the prompt, the more useful the draft.

Key Point. A good AI prompt for a business letter answers: who is this for, what is it trying to achieve, and what specific facts must appear in it.

Draft, Then Edit

Treat the AI's output as a rough first draft, not a finished letter — exactly like handing a task to a junior assistant and then reviewing their work. Editing for accuracy, tone, and the shop's own voice is a required step, not optional polish.

Keeping the Shop's Voice

Default AI writing can sound generic or overly formal. Brenda should edit AI drafts to sound like Amani Stationers actually talking to its own customers — adjusting phrases, removing overly stiff corporate language, and re-checking every number or date against real records.

Key Point. A generic-sounding AI draft is a starting point, not a finished customer-facing letter — it should read like it actually came from Amani Stationers.
