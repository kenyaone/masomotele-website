Verifying AI Output Before You Use It

A Simple Verification Checklist

Before sending, printing, or presenting anything AI helped draft, ask: (1) Are all specific facts (names, numbers, dates) checked against real records? (2) Does it sound like Amani Stationers' actual voice? (3) Have I read the whole thing, not just skimmed it? (4) Would I be comfortable if a customer asked me to explain any part of it?

Key Point. If the answer to any checklist question is "no" or "I am not sure", the document is not ready to send yet.

Reading the Whole Thing

A common mistake is skimming only the opening line of an AI draft and assuming the rest is equally fine. Errors (a wrong figure, an odd claim) can appear anywhere in the text — the whole document needs an actual read-through, not a glance.

Knowing When to Just Write It Yourself

For very short, simple, or highly sensitive content (e.g. a one-line apology to an upset customer), writing it directly may be faster and safer than drafting a prompt, waiting for AI output, and then still needing to carefully verify it. AI assistance earns its time savings mainly on longer or more repetitive drafting tasks.
