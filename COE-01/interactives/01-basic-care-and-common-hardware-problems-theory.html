Basic Care and Common Hardware Problems

Daily Care

Dust buildup can make a computer overheat — keeping the tower's air vents unblocked (not pushed against a wall or covered with papers) helps it stay cool. Eating or drinking directly over the keyboard risks spills that can damage it permanently.

Shutting Down Properly

Always use the Shut Down option from the operating system's Start menu rather than simply cutting power at the wall or holding the power button down. A proper shutdown lets the computer close programs and save its state safely; abruptly cutting power can corrupt files that were still being written.

Key Point. Cutting power abruptly should be a last resort for a frozen, unresponsive computer — not a routine way to switch it off.

Recognising Common Problems

A completely black, silent screen often points to a power issue (see Lesson 1.1). A frozen screen (nothing responds to mouse or keyboard) may need a longer wait, or as a last resort, a forced restart. An unresponsive mouse or keyboard alone (while the rest of the computer works) often just means a loose USB connection — worth checking before assuming something is broken.

Key Point. Many "broken computer" reports turn out to be a loose cable or an unplugged switch — always check the simple, physical things first.
