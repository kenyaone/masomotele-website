Ports and Connectors

USB Ports

USB ports are the most common connector on any computer — used for a mouse, keyboard, USB flash drive, or printer. The classic rectangular USB-A plug only fits one way; the newer oval USB-C plug can be inserted either way up. Most towers have several USB ports so multiple devices can connect at once.

Video Ports

The monitor connects to the tower through a video port — commonly HDMI (a small trapezoid shape) on newer computers, or the older, wider VGA port with small pins (blue, with screws on either side) on some older machines.

Key Point. VGA pins are delicate — never force a VGA cable in at an angle, as bent pins can break the connector entirely.

Avoiding Damage

Every port and plug is shaped to fit only its matching cable in the intended orientation. If a cable does not slide in easily, it is usually the wrong port or upside down — forcing it can bend pins or crack the connector, turning a simple plug-in into an expensive repair.

Key Point. If a cable will not go in easily, stop and check the shape/orientation rather than pushing harder.
