User Accounts and Passwords

Why Separate Accounts Exist

A shared office computer often has a separate user account for each staff member (or at least separates "Administrator" from regular "Standard" accounts), keeping each person's files, settings, and access level distinct — and creating a record of who was logged in when something happened.

Strong Passwords

A strong password is reasonably long, not an obvious guess (not "password123" or a birthday), and ideally not reused across many different accounts. It is the main thing standing between Faith's account (and the patient data behind it) and anyone else who sits at that computer.

Key Point. A password that is easy for Faith to guess is just as easy for anyone else to guess too.

Locking the Computer

Pressing Windows key + L instantly locks the computer, requiring the password again to resume — this should be a habit every single time Faith steps away from the front desk, even briefly, especially since patient information may be on screen.

Key Point. Sharing a password with a colleague, "just this once", removes any way of knowing who actually did what on that account afterward — a habit worth avoiding entirely.
