Ergonomics and Avoiding Strain

Wrist Position

Keeping wrists straight and roughly level with the keyboard while typing — rather than bent sharply upward or resting heavily on a hard edge — reduces strain that can build up over long periods of daily typing.

Key Point. A wrist bent at a sharp angle for hours every day is a common, avoidable source of discomfort — small posture adjustments add up.

Regular Breaks

Taking a short break every hour or so — standing, stretching, looking away from the screen — helps prevent the stiffness and fatigue that builds up from sitting and typing continuously for long stretches.

The 20-20-20 Rule for Eyes

A simple guideline for reducing digital eye strain: roughly every 20 minutes, look at something about 20 feet away for at least 20 seconds. This gives eye muscles a break from constantly focusing at close screen distance.

Key Point. Small, regular breaks (for wrists, posture, and eyes) prevent cumulative strain far better than one long break at the end of a tiring day.
