Module 5 · Lesson 5.3

Recognising AI-Generated Scams and Misinformation

AI Can Write Convincingly

The same AI writing help that assists Faith with a polite message can also be misused by scammers to write extremely convincing fake emails or messages, with none of the spelling mistakes that used to be a common scam warning sign in the past.

Key Point. Polished, professional-sounding writing is no longer reliable proof that a message is genuine — AI has made fake messages much harder to spot by writing quality alone.

Common Scam Patterns

Regardless of how well-written a message is, certain patterns remain common warning signs: urgent pressure to act immediately, requests for a password or payment, or an unexpected attachment/link from an unfamiliar or slightly-off sender address.

Verify Separately

If a message claims to be from the clinic's bank, a supplier, or a supervisor and asks for something sensitive, the safest step is to verify through a separate, already-trusted channel (calling the known official number, or asking the supervisor directly in person) — not by replying to or clicking anything in the suspicious message itself.

Key Point. Never verify a suspicious message using contact details provided within that same suspicious message — use a separately known, trusted number or contact instead.
