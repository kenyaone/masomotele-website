What is a Network? LAN, WAN and Client-Server Basics

1. What is a network?

A computer network is two or more devices connected together so they can share resources -- files, printers, an internet connection -- and communicate with each other. That is the whole definition; everything else in this course is detail on top of it.

2. LAN, WAN and MAN

TypeScopeExample
LAN (Local Area Network)A single site -- one building or campusUhondo Networks' own office network
WAN (Wide Area Network)Spans multiple sites, often multiple cities or countriesThe internet itself; a company connecting its Eldoret and Nairobi offices
MAN (Metropolitan Area Network)Spans a cityAn ISP's network across Eldoret town
Simple rule of thumb: if you can run the cable yourself without leaving the building, it is a LAN. If it needs a service provider's infrastructure to connect two distant sites, it is a WAN.

3. Client-server vs peer-to-peer

In a client-server network, dedicated servers provide services (file storage, email, websites) that client devices request -- most business and school networks work this way, since it centralises control and security. In a peer-to-peer network, every device is roughly equal, sharing resources directly with each other with no dedicated server -- simple, cheap, but harder to secure and manage at scale.
