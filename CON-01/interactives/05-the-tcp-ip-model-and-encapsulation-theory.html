The TCP/IP Model and Encapsulation

1. The TCP/IP model\'s 4 layers

TCP/IP layerRoughly matches OSI layers
ApplicationApplication, Presentation, Session (5-7)
TransportTransport (4)
InternetNetwork (3)
Network Access (or Link)Data Link, Physical (1-2)

TCP/IP is simpler -- 4 layers instead of 7 -- and it is the model actually implemented by real-world network hardware and software, including the entire internet. OSI remains extremely useful as a detailed teaching and troubleshooting reference, even though nothing runs "pure OSI" in production.

2. Encapsulation

As data moves down through the layers for transmission, each layer wraps the data from the layer above it with its own header (and sometimes trailer) information -- like nesting envelopes inside envelopes. This process is called encapsulation. At the receiving end, each layer strips off its corresponding header as the data moves back up -- called de-encapsulation.

Technicians use these names constantly and precisely: "check the packet" (Layer 3), "check the frame" (Layer 2) -- using the right term signals, and reinforces, understanding which layer is actually being discussed.
