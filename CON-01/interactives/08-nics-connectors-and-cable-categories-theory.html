NICs, Connectors and Cable Categories

1. Network Interface Cards (NICs)

A NIC is the hardware component, built into or added to a device, that lets it physically connect to a network -- wired (Ethernet) or wireless (Wi-Fi). Every network-connected device has one, even if it is built directly into the motherboard rather than a separate card.

2. RJ45 connectors and twisted pair cable

RJ45 is the standard connector used on Ethernet cables -- the familiar clear plastic clip-in plug. Inside the cable are 8 copper wires, twisted in pairs (hence "twisted pair") specifically to reduce electrical interference between the wires.

3. Cable categories

CategoryTypical max speedNotes
Cat5e1 GbpsStill common in older installations, adequate for most basic needs
Cat61-10 Gbps (10 Gbps over shorter distances)Better shielding, the current standard choice for new installs
Cat6a10 Gbps at full distanceThicker, more rigid cable, used where full 10 Gbps over longer runs is needed
For a foundational understanding, what matters most is knowing the general speed tiers and that higher categories mean better performance and shielding -- you do not need to memorise exact technical specifications to be an effective technician.

4. Fiber optic cable, briefly

Fiber optic cable carries data as pulses of light through a glass or plastic core, instead of electrical signals through copper. It supports much higher speeds and longer distances than copper, and is immune to electrical interference -- but is more expensive and more fragile to install and terminate. Fiber is common for connections between buildings or for an ISP's main connection into a building, less common for connecting an individual desk.
