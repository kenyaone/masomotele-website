Security Policies and Acceptable Use

Why Written Policies Matter

A written security policy sets clear, consistent expectations — what is and is not allowed — rather than relying on individual judgement, which varies person to person. It also gives a documented basis for enforcement and training.

Acceptable Use Policy (AUP)

An AUP typically covers: appropriate use of company devices/accounts, prohibited activities (installing unauthorised software, using company systems for personal business ventures), and consequences for violations. Every SafariPay employee should read and acknowledge it.

Least Privilege

The principle of least privilege means giving each person only the access they actually need to do their job — a customer support agent does not need database administrator access. This limits the damage if that account is ever compromised or misused.

Key Point. Least privilege is not about distrust of employees — it limits the blast radius of any single compromised account, regardless of how it happened.

Policy Without Enforcement Fails

A written policy nobody reads, or that is never actually enforced, provides little real protection. Regular awareness training and consistent enforcement are what make a policy meaningful rather than a document that just exists on paper.
