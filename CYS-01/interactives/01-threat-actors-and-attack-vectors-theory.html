Threat Actors and Attack Vectors

Types of Threat Actors

Script kiddies use existing tools with little skill, often opportunistically. Organised cybercriminals run financially-motivated operations (fraud, ransomware) and are the most relevant threat to a fintech like SafariPay. Insiders (employees, contractors) already have some legitimate access, making their potential misuse harder to detect. Nation-state actors are highly resourced and typically target larger, more strategic organisations.

Key Point. For a mobile-money company like SafariPay, organised financially-motivated cybercriminals are the most realistic day-to-day threat, not exotic nation-state attackers.

Motivation

Understanding motivation helps predict likely attacks: financially-motivated actors target payment systems and customer funds directly; disruptive actors might aim for denial-of-service; insiders might act out of grievance or financial pressure.

Attack Vectors

An attack vector is the path used to gain access — a phishing email, a vulnerable web application, an infected USB drive, or a disgruntled employee's legitimate credentials. Different vectors need different defences; there is no single control that blocks all of them.

Key Point. "Are we a target?" is the wrong question for a fintech — SafariPay handles real money and customer data, making it an attractive target regardless of its size.
