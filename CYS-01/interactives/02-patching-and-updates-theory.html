Patching and Updates

What Patches Fix

A patch or update often fixes a specific, sometimes publicly disclosed, security vulnerability. Once a vulnerability is publicly known, attackers actively scan for and target systems that have not yet applied the fix — turning "patch promptly" into a race against exploitation.

Key Point. A disclosed vulnerability with a known fix is often more dangerous than an undisclosed one, precisely because attackers know exactly what to look for.

Patch Management

Applying every patch instantly, untested, on production systems risks breaking something that was working. A sensible patch management process tests updates on a small subset first, then rolls out broadly within a reasonable window — balancing stability against the risk of staying unpatched too long.

End-of-Life Software

Software that has reached end-of-life (no longer receiving security updates from its vendor) accumulates unpatched, permanently open vulnerabilities over time. Running end-of-life operating systems or software on any system touching customer data is a serious, growing risk.

Key Point. An end-of-life system is not "fine as long as it still works" — every day past end-of-life, its unpatched vulnerabilities become more widely known and exploited.
