Firewalls: What They Do and Don't Do

How Firewall Rules Work

A firewall filters network traffic based on rules — allowing or blocking connections by source/destination address, port, or protocol. A rule like "allow inbound traffic on port 443 (HTTPS) to the web server, block everything else inbound" is typical.

Default-Deny vs Default-Allow

A default-deny policy blocks everything except what is explicitly allowed — safer, since anything not specifically permitted is blocked by default. A default-allow policy permits everything except what is explicitly blocked — riskier, since anything not yet thought of to block gets through.

Key Point. Default-deny is the security-conscious default for a fintech like SafariPay — start closed, then deliberately open only what is actually needed.

Network vs Host-Based Firewalls

A network firewall sits at the boundary protecting an entire network segment. A host-based firewall runs on an individual device, filtering traffic to and from that specific machine — useful as an additional layer even for devices already behind a network firewall.

What Firewalls Do Not Protect Against

A firewall does not stop a user clicking a phishing link, does not detect malware already inside an allowed connection, and does not protect data once traffic it permitted reaches its destination. It is one important layer, not complete protection on its own.

Key Point. A firewall permitting a connection says nothing about whether the traffic inside that connection is malicious — that is a different problem, handled by different tools.
