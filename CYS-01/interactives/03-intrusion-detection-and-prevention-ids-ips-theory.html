Intrusion Detection and Prevention (IDS/IPS)

IDS: Detect and Alert

An IDS monitors network traffic for suspicious patterns (matching known attack signatures, or unusual behaviour) and generates an alert for someone like Grace to investigate — it does not block anything itself, only notifies.

IPS: Detect and Block

An IPS does everything an IDS does, but can also automatically block or drop traffic matching a detected threat in real time, without waiting for a human to act — faster response, but with more risk if it blocks something legitimate by mistake.

Key Point. IPS trades some risk of blocking legitimate traffic (a false positive) for faster automatic response — this trade-off needs to be tuned deliberately, not left on defaults.

False Positives vs Missed Detections

A system tuned too aggressively generates many false positives (flagging/blocking legitimate activity), frustrating real users and wasting investigation time. A system tuned too loosely risks missed detections (letting real attacks through unnoticed). Tuning is an ongoing balance, not a one-time setup.

Where It Fits

IDS/IPS complements firewalls (which filter by rule, not by attack pattern) and logging (a passive record) — it actively watches for known attack patterns and unusual behaviour in real time, adding detection capability firewalls alone do not provide.
