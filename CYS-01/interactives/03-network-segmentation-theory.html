Network Segmentation

What Segmentation Means

Network segmentation divides a network into separate zones with controlled, limited communication between them, rather than one single "flat" network where every device can freely reach every other device.

The Risk of a Flat Network

On a flat network, a single compromised device (an infected employee laptop, say) can potentially reach and attack anything else on the network, including SafariPay's core payment systems — the compromise spreads as far as the network allows, not just as far as the original entry point.

Key Point. Segmentation does not prevent every breach — it limits how far a breach can spread once it happens, which is a different and complementary goal to prevention.

VLANs

A VLAN (Virtual Local Area Network) is a common way to logically separate devices into distinct network segments, even if they share the same physical network hardware — grouping, for example, general office devices separately from payment-processing servers.

Segmenting by Sensitivity

SafariPay's core payment-processing systems (handling actual money movement) deserve stricter isolation than the general office network (handling email, browsing) — segmenting by sensitivity ensures the most critical systems have the fewest, most controlled paths of access.
