VPNs and Secure Remote Access

What a VPN Does

A VPN (Virtual Private Network) creates an encrypted tunnel between a remote device and SafariPay's internal network, so traffic traveling across the internet in between cannot be easily read or tampered with by anyone intercepting it along the way.

Public Wi-Fi Risk

An open, unencrypted public Wi-Fi network (a café, an airport) is a common place for traffic to be intercepted. A VPN's encryption protects data even over such an untrusted network — making VPN use especially important, not optional, when working from public Wi-Fi.

Key Point. The riskier the network in between, the more a VPN's encryption actually matters — public Wi-Fi is exactly when it matters most, not a situation to skip it.

Split-Tunnel vs Full-Tunnel

A full-tunnel VPN routes all of a device's internet traffic through the encrypted tunnel. A split-tunnel VPN only routes traffic destined for SafariPay's internal systems through the tunnel, letting other browsing go directly — faster for general use, but with a smaller security scope.

VPN Is Not a Complete Solution

A VPN protects data in transit, but does not verify who is actually connecting — an attacker with a stolen VPN credential can still connect. Combining VPN access with MFA (Unit 1) closes this specific gap.
