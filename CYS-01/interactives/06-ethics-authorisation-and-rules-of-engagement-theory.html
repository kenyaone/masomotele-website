Module 6 · Lesson 6.1

Ethics, Authorisation and Rules of Engagement

Authorisation Is Not Optional

Testing systems without explicit authorisation is illegal in most jurisdictions, regardless of good intentions or a genuine desire to help — the exact same technical actions that are legitimate penetration testing with permission become unauthorised computer access without it.

Key Point. The line between "penetration tester" and "criminal", from a purely technical-actions standpoint, is authorisation — nothing else.

Scope Documents

A scope document defines exactly which systems, applications, and network ranges are included in the test — and just as importantly, which are explicitly excluded. Testing something outside the agreed scope, even accidentally, can cause real problems and legal exposure.

Rules of Engagement

Rules of engagement specify permitted testing hours (e.g. not during SafariPay's peak transaction times), acceptable techniques (is actual data exfiltration testing allowed, or just proving access is possible?), and emergency contacts if something unexpected happens during testing.

Written Sign-Off

Verbal permission is not enough — a formal, written authorisation document, signed by someone with genuine authority to grant it, protects both SafariPay and the tester, and creates a clear record of exactly what was agreed.
