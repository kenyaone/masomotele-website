Reconnaissance Techniques

Passive Reconnaissance

Passive reconnaissance gathers information without any direct interaction with the target's systems at all — reading a company's public website, checking public DNS records, or reviewing employee social media profiles. The target has no way of detecting this activity, since nothing actually touches their systems.

OSINT

Open-Source Intelligence (OSINT) refers to using publicly available information — job postings (revealing what technologies a company uses), press releases, public code repositories — as a genuine intelligence resource, often revealing more than organisations realise they are exposing.

Key Point. A job posting listing specific software versions used internally, or a public code repository accidentally including a hardcoded credential, are real examples of OSINT revealing more than intended.

Active Reconnaissance

Active reconnaissance involves some direct interaction with the target (like a basic ping or a DNS query) — still exploratory rather than exploitative, but detectable in a way passive reconnaissance is not.

Why Reconnaissance Quality Matters

Thorough reconnaissance shapes everything that follows — a more complete picture of the target's technology, structure, and exposed information leads to more efficient, better-targeted scanning and testing later, rather than working blind.
