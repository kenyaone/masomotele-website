The Penetration Testing Methodology

Reconnaissance

Reconnaissance gathers information about the target before any direct interaction — publicly available information (company website, employee names on LinkedIn, DNS records) that helps build a picture of the target without yet touching its systems directly.

Scanning and Enumeration

Scanning actively probes the target to identify live hosts, open ports, and running services. Enumeration goes further, extracting more detailed information from those services (software versions, usernames, shared resources) to identify specific potential weaknesses.

Exploitation

Exploitation is the phase where an identified weakness is actually leveraged to gain some level of unauthorised access — proving the vulnerability is real and exploitable, not just theoretical, within the agreed rules of engagement.

Key Point. A vulnerability that looks concerning on paper but cannot actually be exploited in practice is a different (usually lower) priority than one proven exploitable — this is exactly what the exploitation phase establishes.

Post-Exploitation and Reporting

Post-exploitation assesses what further access or impact is possible from the initial foothold (without necessarily going further than agreed). The final report documents findings, severity, evidence, and remediation recommendations — often the single most valuable deliverable of the entire engagement for SafariPay's actual security improvement.
