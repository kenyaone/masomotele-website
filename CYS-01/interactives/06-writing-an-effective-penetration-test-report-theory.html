Module 6 · Lesson 6.5

Writing an Effective Penetration Test Report

Executive Summary

An executive summary is written for non-technical leadership — a concise overview of overall risk posture, the most critical findings, and business impact, without requiring deep technical background to understand and act on.

Key Point. If SafariPay leadership cannot understand what needs to happen next from reading just the executive summary, the report has not done its job for that audience.

Technical Findings

Detailed technical findings give engineers exactly what they need to reproduce and understand each issue — precise steps, evidence (screenshots, request/response data), and the specific systems affected — not vague, unverifiable claims.

Risk Ratings

Each finding should carry a risk rating (e.g. Critical/High/Medium/Low), based on both the severity of potential impact and the realistic likelihood of exploitation — helping SafariPay prioritise fixing the most dangerous issues first rather than treating everything as equally urgent.

Actionable Recommendations

A finding without a clear remediation recommendation leaves the reader knowing there is a problem but not what to actually do about it. Effective reports pair every finding with specific, practical guidance for fixing it.
