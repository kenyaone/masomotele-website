Business Continuity and Disaster Recovery

BCP vs DRP

A Business Continuity Plan (BCP) covers keeping critical business functions running during a disruption (e.g. a manual fallback process for processing urgent transactions). A Disaster Recovery Plan (DRP) focuses specifically on restoring IT systems and data after a disruption. They overlap and work together, but address distinct aspects of resilience.

RTO and RPO

RTO (Recovery Time Objective) is the target maximum time a system should be down before being restored. RPO (Recovery Point Objective) is the maximum acceptable amount of data loss, measured in time (e.g. "we can tolerate losing up to 1 hour of transaction data"). Both numbers directly shape what backup frequency and recovery infrastructure are actually needed.

Key Point. An RPO of "1 hour" is meaningless without backups actually happening at least that often — the target and the actual backup frequency must match, or the objective is not really achievable.

The 3-2-1 Backup Rule

A widely used guideline: keep at least 3 copies of data, on 2 different types of media, with 1 copy stored offsite (or otherwise isolated from the primary systems) — protecting against a single point of failure taking out every copy at once.

Testing Recovery Plans

A backup that has never actually been tested by restoring it is not truly verified — backups can silently fail, or a written recovery plan may skip a step nobody noticed until attempting a real restore. Regularly testing actual recovery, not just writing the plan, is what proves it will work when genuinely needed.
