Digital Forensics Basics

Chain of Custody

Chain of custody is a documented record of exactly who handled a piece of evidence, when, and what was done with it — establishing that evidence was not altered or tampered with between collection and any later use (including potential legal proceedings).

Key Point. Evidence with a broken or missing chain of custody can become far less useful or credible, even if its actual content is genuinely accurate — the documented handling matters, not just the content itself.

Working from Copies

Investigators typically create a forensic copy (a bit-for-bit image) of a device or drive and examine that copy, rather than the original — preserving the original evidence untouched in case it needs independent re-verification later.

Order of Volatility

Some evidence disappears faster than others: data in RAM vanishes when a device powers off, while data on a hard drive persists much longer. The order of volatility guides investigators to capture the most fleeting evidence (like RAM contents) first, before it is lost, then move to more persistent sources.

Documentation Throughout

Every step of a forensic investigation — what was done, when, by whom, and why — should be documented as it happens, not reconstructed from memory afterward, supporting both the chain of custody and the overall credibility of the investigation.
