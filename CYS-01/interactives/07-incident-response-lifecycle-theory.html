Incident Response Lifecycle

Preparation

Preparation happens before any incident — having a written incident response plan, defined roles/responsibilities, and contact lists ready in advance. An incident is the worst possible time to be figuring out the plan for the first time.

Key Point. An incident response plan that has never been tested or practiced is often only marginally better than having no plan at all — untested plans frequently reveal gaps exactly when it matters most.

Identification, Containment, Eradication, Recovery

Identification confirms an incident is actually occurring. Containment limits its spread (e.g. isolating an infected system from the network). Eradication removes the actual root cause (the malware, the unauthorised access). Recovery restores affected systems to normal, verified-clean operation.

Lessons Learned

The final, often-skipped lessons learned phase reviews what happened, what worked, and what should change — feeding improvements back into preparation for next time. Skipping this step means the organisation is more likely to repeat the same mistakes in a future incident.

Written and Tested Plans

A plan that exists only in someone's head, or has never been rehearsed via a tabletop exercise or simulation, tends to reveal serious gaps in the middle of a real, high-pressure incident — exactly the wrong time to discover them.
