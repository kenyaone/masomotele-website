SIEM and Log Monitoring

What a SIEM Does

A SIEM (Security Information and Event Management) platform collects logs from many different sources across SafariPay's environment — servers, firewalls, applications — into one central place, making it possible to search, analyse, and correlate activity that would otherwise sit scattered across dozens of separate systems.

Correlation

Correlation connects related events across different systems that might look harmless individually but reveal something significant together — a failed login on the VPN, followed minutes later by an unusual database query from that same account, correlated together tells a very different story than either event alone.

Key Point. The real power of a SIEM often lies in correlation — spotting the meaningful pattern across multiple individually-unremarkable events, not any single log line in isolation.

Detection Rules

A SIEM uses detection rules to automatically flag patterns matching known suspicious behaviour (e.g. "alert if the same account fails login 10 times within 2 minutes"), turning raw log data into actionable alerts rather than requiring manual review of every single log entry.

Only as Good as Its Logs

A SIEM cannot detect or correlate what it never receives — if a critical system is not sending its logs into the SIEM at all, that entire source is effectively invisible to it, regardless of how well the SIEM itself is configured otherwise.
