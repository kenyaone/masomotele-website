The Security Operations Center (SOC)

What a SOC Does

A Security Operations Center (SOC) continuously monitors an organisation's systems and networks for signs of security incidents, using tools like SIEM platforms (next lesson) and defined processes to detect, triage, and escalate suspicious activity as it happens.

Tiered Analyst Structure

Many SOCs use tiers: Tier 1 analysts perform initial triage of alerts (is this likely a real issue?), Tier 2 investigates confirmed or ambiguous cases more deeply, and Tier 3 handles the most complex incidents and often leads broader threat-hunting efforts.

Alert Fatigue

Alert fatigue happens when analysts are overwhelmed by a high volume of low-quality or false-positive alerts, risking a genuinely critical alert being missed or dismissed among the noise. Tuning detection rules to reduce unnecessary noise is an ongoing, essential SOC task, not a one-time setup.

Key Point. A SOC drowning in noisy, low-value alerts is not actually safer than one with fewer, well-tuned, meaningful alerts — volume of alerts is not the same thing as quality of detection.

Why 24/7 Matters

SafariPay's mobile-money platform operates continuously, and attackers do not restrict themselves to business hours — a SOC with coverage only during office hours leaves a real gap that an attacker could deliberately exploit.
