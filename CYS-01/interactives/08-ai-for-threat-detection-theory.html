Module 8 · Lesson 8.1

AI for Threat Detection

Anomaly Detection

Modern security tools increasingly use machine learning to establish a baseline of "normal" behaviour for users and systems, then flag statistically unusual deviations — a login from an impossible travel distance within minutes, or a sudden spike in data downloaded by one account — patterns that would be hard to catch with simple fixed rules alone.

Processing Scale

SafariPay generates enormous volumes of log and transaction data — far more than Grace's team could ever manually review. AI-assisted tools can process this volume continuously, surfacing candidates for human review that would otherwise be practically impossible to find manually.

Key Point. AI's real advantage here is scale — processing volumes no human team could realistically review manually, not some mysterious ability to "understand" threats better than a person.

Human Judgement Still Required

An AI system flags a login from an unusual location as anomalous — but only Grace knows that the account owner mentioned traveling for a family event that week. Context, business knowledge, and judgement calls remain a human responsibility; AI surfaces candidates, it does not replace the decision.

Still Imperfect

AI-based detection still produces false positives (flagging legitimate unusual-but-fine behaviour) and false negatives (missing a genuinely disguised attack) — it is a significant capability improvement, not a perfect or infallible one.
