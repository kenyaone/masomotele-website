AI-Powered Attacks: Deepfakes and Enhanced Phishing

AI-Written Phishing

AI writing tools let attackers produce grammatically flawless, well-structured, personalised phishing messages at scale — removing the "obviously poorly written" red flag that used to help people spot many phishing attempts (also covered in the COA-01/COE-01 courses' AI units).

Deepfake Audio and Video

Deepfakes use AI to generate fabricated but convincing audio or video — a fake voice call that sounds exactly like SafariPay's CEO requesting an urgent wire transfer, for example. This is no longer purely theoretical; real-world incidents using deepfake voice for exactly this kind of fraud have already occurred at other companies.

Key Point. A voice or video that sounds/looks completely convincing is no longer reliable proof of who it actually came from — this is a genuinely new category of risk, not just "phishing but better".

Business Email Compromise (BEC)

BEC attacks impersonate a trusted figure (an executive, a supplier) to request an urgent, unusual payment or action. AI-generated content makes these impersonations more convincing in both writing and potentially voice/video, increasing their success rate.

Verification Matters More, Not Less

As fakes become harder to detect by "does this look/sound right?" alone, independent verification through a separate, already-trusted channel (calling a known number, an established secondary approval step for large transfers) becomes more important, not less — exactly the same principle from the earlier AI-scam lesson, now applying to a more sophisticated threat.
