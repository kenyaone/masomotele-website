Building Layouts in CorelDraw

Setting Up Guides and the Document Grid

Everything Ivy learned about grids and alignment applies directly in CorelDraw, except now the software can enforce it precisely instead of relying on her eye. Dragging horizontal and vertical guides from the rulers gives her fixed reference lines she can snap objects to. Turning on the document grid (View > Grid, or the grid settings in the document properties) overlays a repeatable pattern of squares across the page, and enabling "Snap to Grid" makes objects lock to it automatically as she moves them.

Setting these up before placing any content means every element Ivy adds afterwards has something precise to align to, rather than being nudged into place by eye and hoping it looks right.

Align & Distribute: Precision Instead of Eyeballing

CorelDraw's Align and Distribute panel (Object > Align and Distribute, or Ctrl+Shift+A) takes selected objects and aligns them to a shared edge or center — left, right, top, bottom, or horizontal/vertical center — in one click. This replaces manual nudging entirely and guarantees the objects land on exactly the same line.

The Distribute options go a step further: if Ivy selects several icons or logos, Distribute can space them evenly across a given area, so the gaps between each one are mathematically equal rather than approximately equal by eye.

Page Setup for Print: Bleed and Margins

Before designing anything meant for print, Ivy configures the document in Layout > Page Setup. This is where she sets the exact page size, and — critically for anything going to a print shop — the bleed and margins. Bleed extends the artwork a few millimetres past the actual trim edge (commonly 3mm), so that when the printed sheet is cut, there is no risk of a thin white sliver showing at the edge if the cut lands slightly off. Margins (or a "safety area") define how far important content like text should stay from the trim edge, so nothing critical gets clipped.

Setting these correctly at the very start of a project, rather than after the design is finished, avoids having to rework a layout that was built to the wrong page dimensions.

Key Point. In CorelDraw, guides, the document grid and Align & Distribute together do mechanically what Ivy's eye can only approximate — precise, repeatable alignment. Combine that with correct page setup (size, margins, bleed) from the very first step, and the layout is both visually consistent and ready for professional print.
