Building a Simple Brand Identity Kit

Why a Logo Alone Isn't a Brand

A logo is only one piece of how a business presents itself. If every flyer, signboard, and social media post uses different fonts and clashing colours, the brand feels inconsistent and less trustworthy, even if the logo itself is strong.

A brand identity kit bundles the logo together with a small, fixed set of fonts and colours, so that anyone producing new materials for the client — a flyer, a menu, a banner — can keep everything looking like it belongs to the same business.

Choosing Brand Fonts

Most simple brand kits use two fonts: one bold, distinctive font for headings, often close in feel to what's used in the logo mark itself, and one clean, easy-to-read font for body text like addresses, prices, or descriptions.

Sticking to just these two fonts, rather than picking a new font for every new job, is what makes a client's materials feel consistent over time.

Key Point. A brand kit isn't extra decoration — it's what stops a client's flyer, signboard, and business card from looking like they came from three different businesses.

Locking In a Colour Palette

A locked colour palette usually has one or two primary brand colours, often taken directly from the logo, plus one or two supporting colours for backgrounds, text, or accents.

Once chosen, these exact colours — not close approximations — should be reused on every future piece of material, so a customer instantly associates that colour combination with the brand.
