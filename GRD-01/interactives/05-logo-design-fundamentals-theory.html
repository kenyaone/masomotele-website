Logo Design Fundamentals

One Logo, Every Size

A logo is rarely used at just one size. The same mark might sit on a tiny business card at 3cm wide, get stitched onto a polo shirt, printed across an A0 signboard outside a shop, or shrunk down into a browser favicon. A logo that looks impressive on a large poster but turns into a blurry smudge on a card has failed at its most basic job.

Good logo design starts with a simple question: will this still be recognisable at the smallest size it will ever be used?

Simplicity Reads Faster

A logo often has less than a second to be recognised. Simple shapes, bold outlines, and a small number of elements are read almost instantly, while intricate detail needs the eye to slow down and study it. This is why so many well-known logos are built from a small handful of clean shapes rather than a detailed illustration.

Simplicity is not a lack of creativity — it is a design decision that makes the logo work harder in real-world conditions.

Key Point. A logo that only looks good large is not finished. Always test it small before calling it done.

Spotting a Logo That Won't Scale

Watch for warning signs: thin hairline strokes that disappear when printed small, small text baked directly into the logo mark, gradients or fine shading that turn to mush on a single-colour print, and too many separate colours that get expensive and messy on merchandise.

If a design only works as a large, full-colour image, it needs simplifying before it can be considered a finished logo.
