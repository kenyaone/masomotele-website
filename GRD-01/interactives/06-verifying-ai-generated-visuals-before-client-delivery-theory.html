AI Tools for Graphic Designers · Topic 3 · Lesson

Verifying AI-Generated Visuals Before Client Delivery

Common AI Visual Mistakes

AI image tools often produce garbled, nonsense lettering inside an image, along with subtle inconsistencies: mismatched repeating patterns, odd shadows or reflections, or uneven small details. These are easy to miss glancing at a small screen preview, but obvious once a client zooms in on a printed banner or business card.

Copyright and Likeness Concerns

AI image tools are trained on huge datasets and can sometimes produce results that resemble an existing brand's logo, a recognisable illustration style, or even a real person's likeness. Using such an image commercially for a paying client can create real legal and ethical risk for the studio, so Ivy avoids anything that looks too close to existing copyrighted material or an identifiable real person without rights or consent.

The Final-Check Routine

Before any AI-assisted visual goes to a client, Ivy runs a short checklist: zoom into all text areas, check fine details for consistency, and ask whether anything resembles an existing logo, brand, character, or real person too closely. Anything questionable gets replaced with original work — for example, typing real text with CorelDraw's text tool rather than leaving AI-garbled lettering in place.

Key Point. The designer — not the AI tool — is responsible for what leaves the studio. Every AI-assisted visual gets a careful human check before it reaches a paying client.
