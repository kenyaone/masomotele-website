Body Systems Overview

1. The major body systems

SystemBasic function
CardiovascularHeart and blood vessels -- circulates blood, oxygen and nutrients
RespiratoryLungs and airways -- brings in oxygen, removes carbon dioxide
DigestiveStomach, intestines and related organs -- breaks down food for energy and nutrients
MusculoskeletalBones, muscles and joints -- provides structure and movement
NervousBrain, spinal cord and nerves -- controls and coordinates the body
UrinaryKidneys and bladder -- filters waste and manages fluid balance

2. Systems work together

No system operates in isolation. For example, the respiratory system brings oxygen in, the cardiovascular system carries it to the whole body, and the nervous system controls the breathing rate itself based on the body\'s needs. Recognising these connections helps explain why a problem in one system (like poor circulation) can produce symptoms that look like they belong to a different system (like confusion, a nervous-system symptom, caused by reduced oxygen delivery).

3. Why this foundation matters for support staff

Mercy will never be the one making a clinical diagnosis -- but understanding basic anatomy helps her describe what she observes accurately ("the patient\'s left leg is swollen" is more useful reported with the right terminology and context), follow instructions correctly, and recognise when something genuinely seems wrong versus routine.

4. Common terms by system

  • Cardiovascular: pulse, circulation, blood pressure
  • Respiratory: breath sounds, oxygen saturation, wheeze
  • Musculoskeletal: range of motion, mobility, joint
  • Nervous: alertness, orientation, responsiveness
