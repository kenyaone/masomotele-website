Receiving, Storing and Issuing Medical Supplies

1. Receiving new stock

When new supplies arrive, they must be checked against the delivery note and purchase order before being accepted: correct item, correct quantity, correct expiry dates, and no visible damage. Any discrepancy is reported immediately, not accepted and sorted out later.

2. Storage principles

  • FEFO (First-Expiry, First-Out): items with the earliest expiry date are placed at the front and used first, regardless of when they arrived.
  • Cold chain items (like certain medications and vaccines) must be stored at the correct temperature at all times -- a broken cold chain can make an item unsafe even if it looks fine.
  • Segregation: different categories of items (medications, dressings, equipment) are stored in designated areas, not mixed together.

3. Issuing supplies to wards

Mercy says: "Every item that leaves the store for a ward is recorded -- what, how much, to which ward, and who authorised it. If it's not written down, officially it didn't happen."

Issuing follows a requisition process: a ward submits a request, an authorised person approves it, and the store records the transaction before releasing the items.

4. Why documentation matters at every step

Accurate stock records let the facility know what it actually has on hand, catch shortages before they become emergencies, track expiry dates proactively, and maintain accountability if something goes missing or is used incorrectly.
