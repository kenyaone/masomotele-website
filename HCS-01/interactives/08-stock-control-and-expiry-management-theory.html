Stock Control and Expiry Management

1. Regular stock counts

Physical stock counts -- actually counting what's on the shelf and comparing it to what the records say should be there -- are done on a regular schedule. This catches errors, loss, or misfiling before they cause a real shortage at a critical moment.

2. Minimum and reorder stock levels

Every essential item has a minimum stock level: the point at which more must be ordered. Waiting until an item completely runs out before reordering is too late -- by the time new stock arrives, the ward may already be without something it needs.

3. Proactively tracking expiry

Mercy says: "I don't wait for an item to expire to notice it. Checking expiry dates regularly, and flagging anything approaching its date early, means we can use it, redistribute it, or replace it in time -- instead of throwing away stock we could have used."

4. Handling discrepancies

When a stock count doesn't match the records -- more or less physically present than expected -- it's reported, not silently corrected or ignored. Unexplained discrepancies can point to a documentation error, a process gap, or something more serious, and all of those deserve a proper look.
