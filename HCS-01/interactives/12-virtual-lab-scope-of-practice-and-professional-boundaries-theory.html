Virtual Lab -- Scope of Practice and Professional Boundaries

1. What scope of practice means

Scope of practice defines the specific tasks a role is trained, authorised and legally permitted to perform. It exists precisely because different roles have different training -- a task that's safe and appropriate for a nurse may be outside a support worker's scope, not because the support worker is untrusted, but because the training and authorisation genuinely differ.

2. Why working outside scope is risky, even with good intentions

Mercy says: "Even if I think I know how to do something, and even if I'm trying to help, doing a task outside my scope puts the patient at real risk and puts me in a position I have no training or authorisation to be in. Good intentions don't change that."

3. The correct response when asked to work outside scope

If asked -- by a patient, a family member, or even a colleague -- to do something outside your scope, the correct response is a polite but clear decline, combined with directing the request to someone who is authorised to help. This isn't unhelpfulness; it's the safe and correct action.
