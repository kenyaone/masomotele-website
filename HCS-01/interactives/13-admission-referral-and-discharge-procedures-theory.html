Admission, Referral and Discharge Procedures

1. Admission

Admission is when a patient formally enters the facility's care. It typically involves registering the patient's details, an initial assessment, orientation to the ward, and starting the patient's official record. Getting this right sets the foundation for everything that follows.

2. Referral

A referral happens when a patient needs care beyond what the current facility or department can provide -- moving to a specialist, a different department, or a higher-level facility. A proper referral includes clear documentation of why, and relevant information travelling with the patient, not just a verbal handoff.

3. Discharge

Mercy says: "Discharge isn't just 'the patient is done, they can go.' It means confirming they're actually ready, that they (or their family) understand any follow-up instructions, and that their paperwork and any belongings are properly sorted before they leave."

4. Why these transitions are high-risk

Admission, referral and discharge are exactly the points where information can be lost, miscommunicated, or dropped between people and departments. Careful procedure at each transition is what keeps continuity of care intact across the whole patient journey.
