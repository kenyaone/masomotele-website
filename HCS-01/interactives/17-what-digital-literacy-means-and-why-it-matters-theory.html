What Digital Literacy Means and Why It Matters

1. What digital literacy means

Digital literacy is the ability to find, use, evaluate and communicate information using digital devices -- computers, tablets, phones -- confidently and safely. It includes practical skills (typing, using software), judgement skills (spotting a suspicious message, knowing what information is safe to share), and the habit of continuing to learn as technology changes.

2. Core digital skills for a support worker

  • Basic keyboard and mouse use
  • Logging into and navigating hospital software, such as an electronic patient record system
  • Sending and understanding professional email
  • Recognising safe versus risky online behaviour
  • Organising digital files sensibly

3. Digital literacy and patient care quality

Mercy says: "At first I thought computer skills were separate from patient care. Then I realised: if I can't confidently log a patient's vitals into the system, or can't find a shift report when I need it, that directly affects the care the patient gets. It's not a separate skill -- it's part of the job now."

4. Building digital literacy gradually

Nobody becomes digitally confident overnight, and that is completely normal. Digital literacy is built skill by skill -- typing comfortably, then navigating software confidently, then handling email and online safety -- exactly the order this course builds it in.
