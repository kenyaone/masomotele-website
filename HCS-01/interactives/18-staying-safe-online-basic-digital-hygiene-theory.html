Staying Safe Online -- Basic Digital Hygiene

1. Strong passwords

A strong password is long, not easily guessed, and not reused across multiple accounts. Avoid obvious choices like a name, birthday, or "password123." A password manager, or a memorable but not-obvious phrase, is far safer than reusing one simple password everywhere.

2. Recognising phishing and scam messages

Phishing is a fake message -- often an email or text -- designed to trick you into giving away a password, personal details, or clicking a harmful link, often by pretending to be someone trustworthy, like IT support or a manager. Warning signs include urgent pressure to act immediately, requests for a password, unexpected attachments, and sender addresses that look slightly wrong.

3. Locking your screen

Mercy says: "At the hospital, a computer left unlocked at the nurses' station is a real problem -- anyone walking past could see, or even edit, patient information under my login. I lock my screen every single time I step away, even for a minute."

4. Keeping software updated

Software updates often fix security weaknesses that could otherwise be used to break into a device or account. Installing updates promptly, rather than repeatedly postponing them, is a simple but genuinely important habit.
