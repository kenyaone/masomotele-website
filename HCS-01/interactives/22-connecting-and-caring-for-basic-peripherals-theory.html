Connecting and Caring for Basic Peripherals

1. Connecting peripherals safely

Most modern keyboards, mice and printers connect through a USB port, and many devices today are designed to work as soon as they are plugged in. Connecting a peripheral is usually straightforward -- but always plug and unplug USB cables gently, without forcing them, since the connector only fits one way.

2. Everyday care of computer equipment

  • Keep drinks and food away from the keyboard and system unit
  • Avoid pressing hard on the monitor screen when cleaning it -- use a soft, slightly damp cloth, not a wet one
  • Keep the area around the system unit free of dust build-up and clutter, since blocked airflow can cause overheating
  • Avoid unnecessarily unplugging and replugging cables, which wears connectors out over time

3. Safe shutdown

Mercy says: "Early on, I used to just turn off the power strip at the end of a shift. My supervisor showed me that's actually risky -- it can cause data loss or even damage the system over time. Now I always shut the computer down properly through the on-screen menu first, and only then switch off the power strip."

4. When a peripheral stops working

If a mouse, keyboard or printer suddenly stops responding, simple first checks include confirming the cable is properly connected, trying a different USB port, and checking the device is actually powered on if it has its own power switch. If none of this resolves it, report it rather than attempting to open or repair the equipment yourself.
