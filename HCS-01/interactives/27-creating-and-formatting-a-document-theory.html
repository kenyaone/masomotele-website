Lesson

Creating and Formatting a Document

1. Starting a new document

Opening Word usually shows a start screen with recent files and template options. Choosing Blank document gives a clean page to begin typing, which is the right starting point for a fresh shift report rather than editing an old, unrelated file.

2. Typing and selecting text

Text is typed directly onto the page. To format a specific word, sentence or heading, it must first be selected (highlighted) -- click and drag across it, double-click to select one word, or triple-click to select a whole paragraph. Formatting always applies only to whatever is currently selected.

3. Basic formatting

The Home tab holds the most common formatting tools: B for Bold, I for Italic, U for Underline, a font size box, and alignment buttons (left, centre, right, justified). Applying Bold and a slightly larger size to a heading like "Shift Handover Report" makes it stand out clearly from the body text below it.

Mercy says: "I save my shift report every few minutes with Ctrl+S, not just at the end. A computer freezing or the power blinking is exactly the kind of thing that erases unsaved work in a busy hospital where you can't always predict an interruption."
