Simple Formulas (SUM, AVERAGE) and Basic Formatting

1. Why formulas matter

Instead of manually adding up a column of numbers by hand -- and risking a mistake, or having to redo the maths every time a new number is added -- a formula does the calculation automatically and updates itself whenever the underlying data changes.

2. Writing a SUM formula

To total the values in cells B2 through B6, click the cell where the answer should appear and type =SUM(B2:B6), then press Enter. The colon between B2 and B6 tells Excel "everything from here to there."

3. Writing an AVERAGE formula

Mercy says: "A formula is only as correct as the range you select. If I mean to average rows 2 to 6 but accidentally select rows 2 to 5, I get a confidently wrong answer -- always double-check the range before trusting the result." Typing =AVERAGE(B2:B6) calculates the average of that same range.

4. Basic formatting

Selecting the header row and applying Bold (and optionally a light fill colour) makes headers stand out clearly from the data beneath them, keeping a busy spreadsheet easy to scan at a glance.
