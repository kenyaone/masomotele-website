Slide Design and Delivering a Presentation

1. Applying a design theme

The Design tab offers a gallery of pre-built themes -- consistent colour schemes, fonts and layouts applied across every slide with a single click, rather than manually formatting each slide differently.

2. Avoiding clutter

Cramming a slide with tiny font because there's "a lot to say" backfires -- if colleagues sitting at the back of the room can't actually read it, that information never really reached anyone. A large, readable font size and a handful of short points per slide matter more than fitting everything onto as few slides as possible.

3. Adding visuals where they genuinely help

A simple, relevant image -- such as a diagram of proper handwashing steps -- can reinforce a point far better than text alone. Images added purely as decoration, with no real connection to the content, tend to distract rather than help.

4. Delivering with Slide Show mode and presenter notes

Clicking Slide Show > From Beginning (or pressing F5) starts the full-screen presentation view used for actually presenting to a room, rather than the editing view. Presenter notes, typed underneath each slide, give a speaker reminders of what to say without needing to read the slide's own text aloud word-for-word.
