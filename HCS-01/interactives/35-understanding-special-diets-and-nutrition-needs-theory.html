Understanding Special Diets and Nutrition Needs

1. Why nutrition matters

Food is not just comfort -- it is part of treatment. Proper nutrition supports wound healing, helps the body fight infection, maintains energy and mood, and helps manage chronic conditions. A client who is not eating properly, even if the reason seems minor, can decline in ways that affect their whole recovery.

2. Common special diets

Diet typeTypically used for
Diabetic dietClients managing blood sugar -- controlled carbohydrate and sugar intake
Low-salt (renal/cardiac) dietClients with kidney disease, heart failure or hypertension
Soft or pureed dietClients with chewing or swallowing difficulties
High-fibre dietClients managing constipation or certain digestive conditions

3. Reading and following the care plan

Mercy says: "Every client on a special diet has a written care plan for it. Before I serve a single meal, I check that plan -- not what I assume the client needs, and not even what the client asks for in the moment."

4. Mercy's role -- support, never prescribe

Mercy does not decide or change a client's diet. Dietary interventions are prescribed by a clinician or dietitian based on the client's medical needs. Her role is to follow the written plan accurately, notice if a client is struggling with it, and report concerns -- not to make her own judgment call about what the client should eat.
