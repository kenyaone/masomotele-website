Effective Communication with Patients and Clients

1. Verbal and non-verbal communication

Communication is not just the words used -- tone of voice, facial expression, eye contact, posture and even how close someone stands all carry meaning. A rushed tone or crossed arms can undercut even the kindest words, while calm body language can reassure a worried client before a single word is spoken.

2. Active listening

Active listening means genuinely focusing on what the other person is saying, rather than simply waiting for a turn to speak. It includes making eye contact, not interrupting, reflecting back what was said to confirm understanding ("So what I'm hearing is..."), and asking clarifying questions rather than assuming.

3. Respect, empathy and dignity

Mercy says: "I always speak to a client the way I would want someone to speak to a member of my own family -- using their name, explaining what I'm about to do before I do it, and never talking about them in the third person while they're right there in the room."

4. Communication as a safety matter

Miscommunication is a leading cause of errors in healthcare. Accurately passing on what a client said, what was observed, and what was done during handover is not just professional courtesy -- it is part of keeping the client safe as care moves between different staff members.
