Understanding Special Needs and Person-Centred Care

1. What "special needs" covers

In a caregiving context, special needs can come from a physical disability, an intellectual or developmental disability, a chronic illness, a mental health condition, or age-related changes like reduced mobility or memory changes. Each client's actual needs are different, even within the same broad category -- which is exactly why a fixed routine applied to everyone does not work well.

2. What person-centred care means

Person-centred care means starting from the individual client's own needs, preferences, and goals, rather than fitting the client into a standard routine. It means asking a client how they like something done, rather than assuming, and adjusting the approach to fit them -- not the other way around.

3. Dignity, independence and choice

Mercy says: "Even when I could do something faster myself, I try to let a client do what they are still able to do on their own. Taking that away, even with good intentions, can quietly take away their sense of independence too."

4. Mercy's role

Mercy supports the client's individual care plan -- which reflects a proper clinical and personal assessment of their needs -- rather than deciding for herself what a client with special needs requires. Where she notices something the plan does not seem to account for, she reports it rather than improvising her own solution.
