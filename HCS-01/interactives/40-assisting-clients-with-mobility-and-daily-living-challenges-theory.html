Assisting Clients with Mobility and Daily Living Challenges

1. Common mobility aids

AidPurpose
Walking stick / caneExtra balance support for clients with mild mobility difficulty
Walking frame / walkerMore stable support for clients needing greater assistance
WheelchairMobility support for clients unable to walk safely or for longer distances
Transfer/gait beltUsed by a caregiver to safely support a client during a transfer

2. Safe body mechanics

Assisting with a client's movement without proper technique risks injury to both the client and Mercy. Basic safe practice includes bending at the knees rather than the back, keeping the client close during a transfer, using a gait belt where trained to, and never attempting a transfer alone if it is meant to be a two-person task.

3. Supporting ADLs without taking over

Activities of daily living (ADLs) -- bathing, dressing, grooming, toileting, eating -- should be supported at the level the client actually needs, not fully taken over by default. Mercy assists with the part a client genuinely cannot manage, and encourages and allows them to do the rest themselves.

4. Recognising fall risk

Mercy says: "I watch for the small signs -- unsteady footing, reaching for furniture to balance, a client who seems more tired or dizzy than usual. Reporting a rising fall risk early, before an actual fall happens, is far more useful than only reacting after one occurs."
