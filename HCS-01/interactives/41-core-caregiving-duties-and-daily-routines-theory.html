Core Caregiving Duties and Daily Routines

1. The structure of a daily routine

A typical caregiving day includes waking and morning personal care, assistance with meals, monitoring and support through the day (mobility, medication reminders where within scope, activities), and an evening routine that supports rest. Consistency matters -- a predictable routine helps a client feel secure, especially one with memory changes.

2. Assisting with personal hygiene and care

Assisting with bathing, grooming, oral care and toileting is one of the most personal parts of caregiving. It should always be done with the client's consent, as much privacy as possible, and at the pace and level of assistance the client actually needs -- never rushed simply to move to the next task.

3. Observing and reporting

Mercy says: "Because I spend so much time with a client day to day, I often notice small changes first -- a new bruise, a change in appetite, a client seeming more withdrawn than usual. Noticing and reporting these things promptly is one of the most valuable things I do, even though I'm not the one deciding what they mean clinically."

4. Dignity and privacy in every task

Even routine, everyday tasks like bathing or toileting involve real vulnerability for the client. Knocking before entering, covering the client appropriately, explaining what is about to happen, and never discussing their care where others can overhear are not optional extras -- they are the standard.
