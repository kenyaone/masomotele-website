Preparing Documents and Requirements for Deployment

1. Core documents needed

DocumentPurpose
Valid passportRequired for international travel and the work visa itself
National ID / birth certificateConfirms identity for both Kenyan and host-country processes
Certificate of good conductPolice clearance, almost always required by employers and immigration
Academic/training certificatesProves the qualification the job offer is based on
Medical certificate / fitness reportConfirms fitness to work, from an accredited medical centre
Work permit / visaLegal authorisation to work in the host country

2. Reviewing the contract before signing

Never sign a contract you do not fully understand. Before signing, check: the job title and duties actually match what was advertised, the salary and payment schedule are stated clearly in figures both sides understand, the contract duration, the accommodation and food arrangement, and the repatriation clause -- who pays for the return ticket home. If the contract is in a language you don't read fluently, insist on a certified translation before signing anything.

3. Medical clearance and fitness-to-work exams

Most host countries require a medical fitness exam from an accredited medical centre before issuing a work visa. This commonly includes a chest X-ray, blood tests, and screening for certain communicable diseases. This exam must be done through a centre the destination country actually recognises -- an exam from an unapproved clinic may simply be rejected, wasting time and money.

4. Working through recognised channels

Mercy says: "A classmate of mine nearly lost her passport to an agent who wasn't even licensed. Now I always tell people: check that the agency is registered with the National Employment Authority, ask for their licence number, and never, ever hand over your original passport to anyone as 'safekeeping.'"

Working only through agencies licensed by Kenya's National Employment Authority (NEA), and registering with the Kenyan embassy or high commission on arrival, are both real protections -- not just formalities.
