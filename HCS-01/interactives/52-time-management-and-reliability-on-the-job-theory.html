Time Management and Reliability on the Job

1. Why punctuality and attendance matter in healthcare

A hospital ward runs on continuous coverage -- one shift depends on the next person arriving on time for a proper handover. Unlike many workplaces where a late arrival is simply inconvenient, in healthcare it can leave a gap in patient supervision, delay care, or leave a colleague unable to leave on time after their own long shift.

2. Practical habits for managing a shift

  • Arrive prepared and slightly early, not exactly on time
  • Review handover notes carefully at the start of the shift, not skim them
  • Identify the most urgent tasks first, rather than working strictly in the order they come to mind
  • Use short windows of time (between tasks) productively rather than losing them

3. Handling unavoidable delays or absences

Mercy says: "Things genuinely come up -- I've been stuck behind an accident on the way to a shift. What matters is calling in as early as I possibly can, not just not showing up and explaining later. That early warning is what actually lets the ward plan around it."

4. Prioritising tasks under pressure

When several things feel urgent at once, safety-critical tasks (like responding to a call bell, or repositioning a patient at risk of pressure sores) come first, ahead of routine tasks that can reasonably wait a short while. This kind of prioritising is itself a skill built with experience -- and it stays firmly within Mercy's scope, since it is about sequencing her own support tasks, not making clinical decisions about a patient's care.
