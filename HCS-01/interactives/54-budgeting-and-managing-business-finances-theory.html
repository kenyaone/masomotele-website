Budgeting and Managing Business Finances

1. Estimating costs

Two categories of cost matter from the start: startup costs (equipment, registration fees, initial supplies -- one-time expenses to get going) and running costs (transport, restocking, phone and data -- ongoing expenses that recur every week or month). Underestimating running costs is one of the most common reasons a promising business struggles early on.

2. Setting a price

A price has to cover real costs plus a reasonable margin -- it cannot simply be "what feels fair" or "what a friend charges for something different." A basic approach: add up the actual cost of delivering the service (materials, transport, your time), then add a margin on top so the business can actually sustain itself and grow, not just break even.

3. Separating business and personal finances

Mercy says: "I keep a separate M-Pesa till for anything related to the care service I'm building, completely apart from my personal number. If I can't see clearly what the business actually earned versus spent, I can't make good decisions about it -- and neither can anyone helping me with a loan application later."

4. Simple record-keeping

Keeping receipts, writing down every transaction (even a simple notebook or basic spreadsheet works), and reviewing the numbers weekly are habits that pay off quickly. Good records show whether the business is actually making a profit, not just staying busy, and are exactly what a bank or lender will ask to see if the business later needs a loan to grow.
