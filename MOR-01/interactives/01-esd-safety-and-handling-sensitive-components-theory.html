ESD Safety and Handling Sensitive Components

What Is ESD?

Electrostatic discharge (ESD) is a sudden, brief flow of static electricity between two objects at different electrical charge. It is often triggered by simple friction — walking across a carpet, sliding off a chair, or even shifting a plastic bag on the bench — and it can happen without Purity ever feeling a shock or seeing a spark.

Why It Silently Damages Electronics

A logic board is packed with tiny, sensitive transistors and chips. A static discharge far below what a person can feel is still enough to degrade or destroy these components. Worse, the damage does not always show up immediately — a board can work fine right after a repair and then fail days later, sometimes called "walking wounded" damage. This makes ESD one of the sneakiest causes of comeback repairs.

Key Point. ESD damage is invisible at the moment it happens. The habit of grounding yourself before touching a logic board costs a few seconds, but can prevent a repair that quietly fails days later.

Practical ESD Prevention Habits

At QuickFix Phone Repairs, Purity wears an anti-static wrist strap connected to a grounded point while working, and rests boards on an anti-static mat rather than bare plastic. When no wristband is available, at minimum she touches a grounded, bare-metal surface before handling an open logic board. She also avoids working on carpet, keeps plastic bags away from exposed boards, and handles boards by their edges instead of touching chips or gold connectors directly.
