Essential Repair Tools and Safety Practices

Precision Screwdriver Sets

Phones use tiny, often proprietary screws — commonly Phillips #000, tri-point (Y-type) and pentalobe heads. A good precision screwdriver set with interchangeable bits covers most models Purity sees at QuickFix Phone Repairs. Using the wrong size or type of bit is one of the fastest ways to strip a screw head, turning a two-minute step into a frustrating extraction job.

Spudgers and Suction Cups

A spudger is a flat plastic or nylon prying tool used to pop clips loose and gently disconnect delicate connectors without scratching the case or shorting anything nearby — metal tools should stay away from the battery and logic board for exactly this reason. A suction cup creates a lifting point on glued screens or back glass panels, giving Purity a controlled edge to work a pick or heat around, rather than prying blindly.

Key Point. Mixing up screw positions is one of the most common causes of comeback repairs. A labeled tray or magnetic mat turns a jumble of identical-looking screws into an easy, ordered map back to the phone.

Heat/Hot Air Stations and Bench Safety

A heat gun or hot air station applies gentle, even heat to soften the adhesive holding a screen or back glass so it can be lifted without cracking — the heat should keep moving rather than lingering in one spot. Just as important as the tools themselves are simple bench habits: using a screw tray or magnetic mat with labeled sections matching each disassembly step, since screws are often different lengths at different positions, and a wrong-length screw reinstalled in the wrong hole can pierce a component underneath.
