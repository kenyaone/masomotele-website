Smartphone Hardware Overview

The Screen Assembly

What most customers just call "the screen" is actually a laminated stack: a glass/OLED display bonded to a touch-sensing digitizer layer, often called the screen assembly or "combo" in the shop. At QuickFix Phone Repairs, Purity learned early that this whole assembly connects to the logic board through one or two short, delicate flex cables near the top or bottom edge — not through any screws.

Because the display and touch layers are bonded together, a cracked screen usually means replacing the entire assembly, not just the outer glass.

Battery, Logic Board and Charging Port

The battery is usually held down with adhesive strips and connects to the logic board through a small, easily-damaged connector. The logic board is the phone's brain — it holds the processor, memory and most of the tiny chips that make everything work, and nearly every other part connects back to it in some way. The charging port is often its own small flex board that plugs into the logic board, and on many phones it also carries the microphone and part of an antenna.

Key Point. Before disconnecting anything inside a phone, identify which connector belongs to which part. Guessing during reassembly is how technicians accidentally create a "new" fault that was not there before.

Cameras and How Everything Connects

Front and rear cameras attach to the logic board (or to a small camera connector board) through their own thin flex cables. Once Purity started mapping a phone this way — screen assembly, battery, logic board, charging port and cameras, all linked by small connectors and ribbon cables — a phone stopped feeling like a sealed mystery box and started feeling like a small, logical system she could work on safely.
