Diagnosing Screen Damage

Two Layers, One Screen

What looks like a single "screen" on a smartphone is actually two separate components stacked tightly together: the outer glass and touch-sensing digitizer on top, and the LCD or OLED display panel underneath that actually produces the picture. On many phones these are fused into one pre-built assembly, but the damage each layer suffers is still distinct and shows up differently. Purity's first job on any cracked or misbehaving phone at QuickFix Phone Repairs is deciding which layer — the glass, the digitizer, the display, or some combination — has actually failed, rather than assuming the whole screen must be replaced.

Reading the Symptoms

Glass-only damage typically shows as spiderweb cracks or a chipped corner, while the display underneath shows the picture with correct colors and touch responds accurately everywhere. Display panel (LCD/OLED) damage shows as dark blotches or patches of discoloration, colored or black lines running across the screen — often starting from the point of impact — a section of the screen that stays completely dark, or the whole display staying black even though the phone is clearly powered on and making sound. Digitizer/touch damage is different again: the picture can look completely normal, but taps do not register in some or all areas, or the screen registers "ghost touches" with no one touching it.

Key Point. If the glass is cracked but the picture displays perfectly and touch works everywhere, the display panel is very likely undamaged. But any lines, discoloration, dead zones, or blackout on the display, or touch problems on a screen that still displays clearly, both point to deeper damage that a simple glass-only repair will not fix.

Confirming Before You Quote

Before promising a customer a price, Purity powers the phone on with its current cracked screen, when it is safe to do so, and runs a quick two-part check: does the picture display cleanly with no lines or dark patches across the whole panel, and does touch respond accurately in all four corners and the middle? A simple swipe test across those five points catches partial digitizer failures that are easy to miss at a glance. Only after this check does she decide whether a cheaper glass-only fix is realistic on that model, or whether the full screen assembly needs replacing — and she explains the reasoning clearly to the customer before ordering any part, so QuickFix does not waste time or money on the wrong component.
