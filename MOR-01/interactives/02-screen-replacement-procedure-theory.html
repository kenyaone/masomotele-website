Screen Replacement Procedure

Preparing the Device Before Opening It

Before Purity touches a single screw, the phone must be fully powered off — not just asleep or sitting on a lock screen. A phone that is still powered on has live electrical connections throughout the board, and opening it while it is on risks a short circuit the moment a metal tool or an exposed ribbon cable touches an active contact. She also confirms with the customer whether important data has been backed up, removes any SIM tray or protective case, and lays out a clean, well-lit workspace with the correct screwdriver bits, a plastic pry tool, and a screw tray before starting anything.

Opening the Casing and Disconnecting Power First

With the phone fully off, Purity removes the back cover or opens the front assembly using a plastic pry tool rather than metal, working slowly around the edges to avoid snapping clips or hidden ribbon cables near the frame. As soon as the internals are exposed, and before disconnecting the screen's ribbon connector, she disconnects the battery connector from the logic board. This removes power from the board completely, so that if a tool slips or a metal edge brushes across two contacts while she works near the screen connector, there is no live current available to cause a short circuit or damage a chip.

Key Point. Always disconnect the battery connector before disconnecting or working near the screen connector. This removes power from the board first, so an accidental short while handling delicate ribbon cables cannot damage the phone.

Fitting the New Screen and Reassembling

With the battery safely disconnected, Purity disconnects the old screen's ribbon cable or cables from the board, removes the mounting screws or adhesive holding the old screen in place, and lifts it out. She fits the new screen, reconnecting its ribbon cables firmly and evenly into their sockets, checking that each connector sits flat with no bent pins. Before closing the casing completely, she reconnects the battery and briefly powers the phone on to test the display and touch response while everything is still accessible — catching a loose connector now is far easier than discovering it after the case is fully closed and sealed.
