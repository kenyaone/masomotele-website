Charging & Component Repair · Lesson 2

Charging Port Replacement

Preparing for the Job

Before touching a single screw, Purity sets up a clean, well-lit workspace with a soft mat to prevent scratching the phone's casing. A small parts tray, or a magnetic mat with labelled sections, keeps the many tiny screws sorted by where they came from — this single habit prevents most of the "leftover screw" problems that plague rushed repairs. Purity also confirms the replacement charging port or flex assembly is the exact correct part for the phone model, since ports that look similar are often not interchangeable.

An anti-static wrist strap, or regularly touching an earthed metal surface, helps protect the sensitive components on the board from static discharge while the phone is open.

The Replacement Procedure

While exact steps vary by phone model, the general order is similar across most repairs. Purity powers the phone off completely, removes the back cover or screen (depending on the model) and the screws holding the board and shielding in place, then — critically — disconnects the battery connector from the logic board before touching anything else. This removes power from the board and protects against accidental short circuits while other flex cables are being handled.

With the battery safely disconnected, Purity carefully disconnects the charging port's flex connector and any other cables routed near it, removes the screws or adhesive holding the old port or flex assembly in place, and lifts it out. The new part is aligned carefully into its housing, seated fully, and its connector reconnected. Before fully reassembling the phone, Purity reconnects the battery and tests charging — plugging in a cable to confirm the new port works — since it is far easier to fix a problem now than after the case is closed up again.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

The two mistakes that cause the most damage in port replacements are forcing a connector and losing tiny screws. Flex connectors are designed to seat in one correct orientation with light, even pressure; if a connector does not slide or click into place easily, forcing it risks bending pins or cracking the connector housing, turning a simple part swap into a much bigger repair. The fix is always to stop, check the alignment and orientation, and try again gently.

Losing tiny screws is just as costly — using the wrong screw length in the wrong hole can crack the board or puncture a component underneath. Keeping screws sorted in a labelled tray as they are removed, and returning them to the same spot on reassembly, avoids this entirely.

Key Point. Never force a connector into place — if it does not seat easily, stop and check the orientation and alignment first.
