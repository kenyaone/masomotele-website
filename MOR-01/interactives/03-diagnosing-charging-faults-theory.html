Charging & Component Repair · Lesson 1

Diagnosing Charging Faults

Cable, Charger, or Port?

Before Purity ever reaches for a screwdriver, the very first question on any "phone won't charge" job at QuickFix Phone Repairs is: is the problem the cable, the charger, or the phone itself? Cables and chargers fail far more often than the port inside the phone, simply because they get bent, stepped on, and yanked out of sockets every day.

The fastest way to find out is substitution: try a cable and charger that are already known to work well on a different phone. If the phone charges normally with a known-good cable and charger, the original accessory was the fault — not the phone. If it still will not charge, the problem is more likely inside the phone itself, and it is time to look at the port.

Inspecting the Port Itself

With a bright flashlight or head torch, Purity looks straight into the charging port opening. Common things to spot at this stage include a build-up of pocket lint or dust blocking the pins, a greenish or white residue from corrosion (often after a phone has been in a humid or rainy environment), and pins inside the port that look bent, pushed in, or missing entirely.

Lint and light debris can often be cleared safely with a wooden toothpick or a plastic pick — never metal, and never anything sharp poked in with force, since it is easy to bend a pin that was actually fine. A can of compressed air, held at a slight angle, is also useful for blowing loose debris out without touching the pins at all.

A Simple Continuity Check

If the port looks physically fine but the phone still will not charge, a multimeter set to continuity (or resistance) mode gives Purity one more check before opening the phone. With the phone powered off, probing between the correct points on the charging flex (following the phone's service diagram) shows whether there is a break (open circuit) or an unexpected short. A steady beep on continuity mode across a line that should be continuous is a good sign; silence where there should be a connection points to a broken trace or a failed connector.

This test is quick, non-destructive, and tells Purity a great deal about whether the fault is really in the charging path — all before a single screw is removed.

Key Point. Always rule out the cable and charger with a known-good swap before assuming the phone's charging port is at fault — it is the fastest, cheapest check and it is right more often than not.
