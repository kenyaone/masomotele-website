Common Software Faults and Factory Resets

Spotting a Software Fault

At QuickFix Phone Repairs, Purity sees the same handful of software symptoms again and again: the phone freezes and stops responding to touch, it restarts itself over and over without fully starting up (a "boot loop"), certain apps crash the moment they open, or the whole phone has become slow and laggy after an update. These are all signs the problem lives in the software — the operating system or an app — not in a broken physical part.

Software Symptom or Hardware Symptom?

The difference usually shows in the pattern. A software fault tends to be inconsistent and screen-based: freezing at random moments, one app misbehaving while others work fine, or the fault appearing right after installing an update or app. A hardware fault tends to be physical and consistent: a cracked screen with a visible dead zone, a battery that swells or will not hold charge, or a phone that only powers on when the charging cable is wiggled at a certain angle. When Purity is unsure, she notes exactly when the fault happens and whether it is tied to a specific action — that pattern is the clue.

What a Factory Reset Actually Does

A factory reset erases all apps, settings, and personal data, and returns the phone's software to the state it was in when it left the factory. It is a powerful fix for many software problems — corrupted settings, a badly misbehaving update, or a phone clogged with faulty apps — because it wipes away whatever went wrong in software. But because it deletes everything on the phone, it is never Purity's very first move; it is a deliberate step taken after simpler things (like restarting the phone, or clearing one troublesome app) have already been tried, and only after data backup has been discussed with the customer.

Key Point. A factory reset is a strong software-only fix, not a first reflex — Purity should suspect software (not hardware) first, then confirm data is backed up or the customer has been warned, before resetting.
