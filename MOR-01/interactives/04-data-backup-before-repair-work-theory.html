Data Backup Before Repair Work

Why Backup Comes First

Some of the repairs Purity does at QuickFix Phone Repairs carry a real risk of erasing the customer's photos, contacts, and messages — factory resets, OS flashing, and even some screen or motherboard repairs where the storage chip can be disturbed. A customer's data is often more valuable to them than the phone itself. Before starting any repair that carries this risk, Purity's rule is simple: back the data up herself if possible, or clearly tell the customer the risk exists so they can decide, before any work begins.

Simple Backup Methods

If the phone still turns on and responds to touch, backup is usually straightforward: an Android phone can sync photos and contacts to its Google account, and an iPhone can back up to iCloud or to a computer using a cable. Where a cloud account is not set up or is slow, Purity can also connect the phone to a shop computer and copy photos and files across directly, or move files onto a memory card if the phone supports one. Whichever method she uses, she confirms the important folders (photos, contacts, WhatsApp if used) have actually copied across before continuing.

When the Phone Cannot Be Backed Up Normally

Sometimes the phone is too damaged to allow a normal backup — a shattered, unresponsive screen, or a phone that will not boot at all. In these cases, Purity cannot back up the data herself before starting, so instead she clearly explains this risk to the customer, in plain language, and gets their agreement before proceeding. She notes on the repair ticket that the customer was informed of the data-loss risk, so there is a clear record that the warning was given.

Key Point. Whenever a repair risks data loss, back the data up if the phone allows it — and if it does not, warn the customer clearly and record that the warning was given, before any work begins.
