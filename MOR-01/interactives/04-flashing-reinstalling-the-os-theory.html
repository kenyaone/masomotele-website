Flashing/Reinstalling the OS

What "Flashing" Means

When a factory reset does not solve a software problem — or when the operating system itself is badly corrupted and the phone will not even boot to the reset menu — the next step is "flashing". Flashing means completely reinstalling the phone's operating system firmware from scratch, writing a fresh copy over the old, broken one. Purity thinks of it like replacing a badly damaged book with a brand new printed copy, rather than trying to repair torn pages.

Why the Correct Firmware Matters

Every phone model needs its own exact matching firmware file — the right brand, the right model number, and often the right region or carrier version. Flashing the wrong firmware onto a device can leave it non-functional, because the firmware is built specifically for that device's hardware. Before Purity starts any flash, she always confirms the exact model number printed on the phone or in its settings, and downloads firmware only from a source she trusts for that specific model.

Staying Cautious: Power and Connection

Flashing writes new data directly onto the phone's storage, and if that process is interrupted partway through — by a lost USB connection, a phone battery dying mid-flash, or a power cut at the shop — the phone can be left with no working operating system at all. This is called "bricking" a device, because it becomes about as useful as a brick. To avoid this, Purity always makes sure the phone has a good charge (or stays connected to power), uses a reliable cable, and does not disconnect or interrupt the process once it has started, however long it takes.

Key Point. Confirm the exact device model and matching firmware first, then ensure stable power and connection throughout — flashing must never be rushed or interrupted.
