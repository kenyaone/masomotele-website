Assessing Water Damage

Reading the Liquid Damage Indicator (LDI)

Most phones have one or more small Liquid Damage Indicator stickers, usually inside the SIM tray slot or near the battery compartment. They are normally white or silver, and turn red or pink once they make contact with liquid. When a customer brings in a phone at QuickFix Phone Repairs, Purity's first job is to find these stickers and check their colour, before she does anything else.

A tripped LDI does not tell you exactly how much damage was done inside, but it is strong evidence that liquid reached the inside of the case — even if the phone looks completely dry on the outside. Some models have two or three indicators in different spots, so it is worth checking all of them.

Spotting Corrosion and Mineral Deposits

Once the case is open, Purity looks for a white, greenish or bluish crust on connector pins, the charging port, the battery terminals, and sometimes on screws. This crust is corrosion, caused by minerals in the water reacting with the metal contacts.

Corrosion is a problem because it does not conduct electricity cleanly — it can create weak, unpredictable connections, and in some spots it can bridge two contacts that were never meant to touch. The longer a phone sits uncleaned after water exposure, the more this corrosion spreads and the worse the damage becomes.

Key Point. Never power on a device that has visible water exposure or a tripped LDI until it has been fully inspected, cleaned and dried. Powering on a wet board can push current through corroded or bridged contacts and cause a short circuit — turning a simple cleaning job into a dead board.

Why Immediate Power-On Is Risky

Water, especially tap water or sweat, carries dissolved minerals and salts that make it conduct electricity far better than pure water. If the board is still wet and someone applies power, that moisture can create an unintended path between components that should never be connected — this is a short circuit, and it can permanently burn out chips in an instant.

This is why the very first physical step, before any testing, is to disconnect the battery. A damaged battery can also swell or overheat if it is powered under load, so leaving it connected while the board is still wet or corroded is not worth the risk.
