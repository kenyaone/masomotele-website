Cleaning and Drying Procedures

Safe Disassembly Before Cleaning

Before Purity cleans anything, the phone must be powered off and the battery connector disconnected from the board — this removes the power source so no current can flow through any wet or corroded contact while she is working. Only after that does she remove the back cover and screws.

She keeps every screw organised in a labelled tray or magnetic mat, since phone screws vary in length and putting the wrong one in the wrong hole can crack the board later. Taking a quick photo of the layout before removing parts also makes reassembly far easier.

Cleaning Corrosion with Isopropyl Alcohol

The standard cleaning agent for corrosion is isopropyl alcohol (IPA) at a high concentration — 90% or higher. High-concentration IPA has very little water in it, so it evaporates quickly and does not leave a conductive residue behind, unlike water or lower-concentration rubbing alcohol (around 70%).

Purity applies it with a soft, anti-static brush, gently working it over corroded pins, connectors and the board until the white or greenish crust lifts away. For boards with corrosion in hard-to-reach spots, an ultrasonic cleaner filled with IPA can clean more thoroughly.

Key Point. Only use high-concentration isopropyl alcohol (90% or higher) on a powered-off board with the battery disconnected. It dissolves mineral deposits and evaporates cleanly, unlike water or low-concentration alcohol, which can leave residue behind.

Proper Drying — Why Rice Is a Myth

Putting a wet phone in a bag of rice is a popular home remedy, but it does not properly dry a device. Rice grains are too large to draw moisture out of tiny gaps, ports and connectors, there is no real airflow inside a sealed bag, and rice dust can even end up inside the phone.

A far better approach is to place the phone in a sealed container with proper silica gel desiccant for at least 24–48 hours, or to use gentle, controlled airflow at room temperature. Direct heat — like a hairdryer or heat gun aimed at the battery — should always be avoided, since it can cause a damaged battery to swell or even catch fire.
