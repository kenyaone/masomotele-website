Component Testing After Water Exposure

Why Test Before Full Reassembly

After cleaning and drying, a corroded or moisture-affected fault may not be obvious until the phone is powered on. Purity tests each part individually while the case is still open, connecting only what she needs for each test rather than closing everything up at once and hoping for the best.

Testing this way isolates problems: if the screen misbehaves, she knows it's the screen, not something else on the board. It also limits the risk of any remaining fault spreading further if something is still not quite right.

Testing the Battery and Charging

Before reconnecting the battery, Purity inspects it closely for any swelling or puffiness — a sign of internal damage. A multimeter check on the battery's voltage is a quick way to confirm it is within a normal range before it goes anywhere near the board.

Once she's satisfied the battery is safe, she reconnects it alone and checks that the phone powers on and reaches the boot logo. Then she tests the charging port with a cable, watching for a stable charging indicator without needing to wiggle the connector to keep contact.

Key Point. A swollen or puffy battery must never be reconnected or charged. It should be safely set aside and replaced — a damaged lithium battery can leak, overheat, or even catch fire if it is powered.

Testing Screen, Touch and Buttons

With the battery connected and the phone powered on, Purity checks the display carefully: full, even backlight with no dead spots or discolouration, since corrosion can sometimes reach the display connector. She then taps across the entire touch surface, including the edges, to confirm the touch response is consistent everywhere.

Physical buttons — power, volume, home — should each give a firm, consistent click and register correctly. Only once the battery, charging, screen and buttons have all tested cleanly does Purity move on to closing the case and fully reassembling the phone.
