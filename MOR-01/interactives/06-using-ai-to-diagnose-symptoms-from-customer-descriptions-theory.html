Using AI to Diagnose Symptoms From Customer Descriptions

Why Customer Descriptions Are Vague

When a customer walks into QuickFix Phone Repairs, they rarely describe a fault the way a technician would. A customer says "my phone gets hot and the battery dies fast" — not "the charging IC is drawing excess current" or "there's a parasitic drain from a background process." That vagueness is normal. Customers are not technicians, and Purity's first job is to translate that everyday description into something she can actually investigate.

Turning a Description Into a Useful AI Prompt

Before opening the phone, Purity can type the customer's own words into an AI assistant and ask it for a first-pass list of likely causes. A prompt like "An Android phone gets hot during normal use and the battery drains much faster than before — what are the most likely causes to check?" is enough to get a useful starting list. Adding extra details the customer mentions — the exact phone model, when the problem started, whether it followed a software update, which apps are used most — makes the AI's list far more specific and useful.

Key Point. The AI narrows down where to look. It never confirms a diagnosis on its own — only hands-on testing at the bench does that.

Using the List as a Checklist, Not a Verdict

For a "hot phone, fast battery drain" description, an AI assistant might suggest checking: battery health/degradation, a background app stuck running, a faulty charging port or IC, malware, or a failing hardware component causing a short. Purity treats this as a checklist of things worth testing in order — starting with the quickest, least invasive checks — rather than as a finished answer. She still opens the device, runs diagnostics, and confirms the real cause herself before doing any repair.
