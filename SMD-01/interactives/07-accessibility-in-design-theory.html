1. Who accessibility is for

In Kenya, roughly 7% of people live with some form of visual impairment. 4% live with a hearing impairment. Add every customer trying to read your post on a cheap Tecno in bright Nairobi sun, or watching a video with sound off in a meeting — and you're at 10–15% of Sarah's Karibu Kitchen customer base.

  • Colour contrast — for low vision, colour blindness, and sunlight reading.
  • Alt text — for screen readers and for Google image search (SEO bonus).
  • Captions on video — 85% of Reels are watched sound-off.
  • Readable fonts — no cursive scripts for body text.
  • No flashing content — safety rule for photosensitive epilepsy.
  • Consent — Kenya DPA 2019 requires it for identifiable customer photos.
The business case: Sarah is currently leaving 15% of Kenyan buyers on the table. That's roughly KES 15,000 of monthly revenue for a Karibu Kitchen-sized operation. Accessibility isn't charity — it's revenue.
The six rules of accessible design covered in the video: (1) colour contrast 4.5:1 minimum, (2) never rely on colour alone — label & colour, (3) alt text on every image, (4) 16 px minimum body text and no decorative scripts, (5) captions burned into every video, (6) no flashing content faster than 3 flashes per second (photosensitive epilepsy).

Targets: body text needs at least 4.5:1 (WCAG AA). Large text (18 pt bold or 24 pt regular) needs 3:1. Aim for AAA (7:1) if the poster is a critical CTA — high sunlight readability makes a real difference in Nairobi at 1 PM.
Never do this: grey text on a slightly-lighter grey background. Common in trendy Canva templates. Fails contrast and hides your price. If in doubt, dark text on light background is a safe default.

4. Kenya DPA 2019 — consent for customer photos

The Kenya Data Protection Act 2019 treats an identifiable photo of a person as personal data. Sarah needs consent before posting any customer's face on Karibu Kitchen's channels.

  • Verbal consent is not enough. Get a WhatsApp message: "Yes, you can post the photo of me eating your chapati."
  • Consent is specific. "Yes for Instagram" does not mean "Yes for a Meta ad campaign."
  • Consent is revocable. If Joyce later asks Sarah to take the post down, Sarah must comply within a reasonable time.
  • Children (under 18): require parental consent. Best practice: avoid identifiable children in commercial marketing.
  • Public places: even in a public street, the DPA still applies if the person is the subject of the photo.
Sarah's simple system: keep a WhatsApp folder called "Photo consents". Every customer photo pinned there with the consent message. If Joyce asks to be removed, the folder tells Sarah which posts to check.
DPA-safe default: if you're unsure whether you have consent, don't post the photo. Use a plate-only shot, a wide back-of-head shot, or an illustration. The upside of one identifiable customer photo is not worth an ODPC complaint.

Quick consent script Sarah uses on WhatsApp

"Habari Joyce! I love the photo of you and the team enjoying today's chapatis. Would you be OK if I posted it on Karibu Kitchen's Instagram and Facebook this week? Reply YES and I'll tag you; reply NO and I won't post it. Asante. — Sarah"

Direct, specific, revocable, in writing. That single message is Sarah's DPA compliance for the whole photo. She screenshots the YES reply into her Photo consents folder.

Bonus SEO: good alt text also feeds Google Image Search. "Ugali and beef stew in Karibu Kitchen takeaway container" appears in Google when someone in Eldoret searches for "ugali stew delivery near me". Accessibility and SEO share the same file.
