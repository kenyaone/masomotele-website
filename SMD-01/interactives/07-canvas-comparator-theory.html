Notice the pattern: four out of six modern canvases are vertical 9:16. Two are landscape. Only one is square. In 2026, mobile-first means portrait-first. Sarah shoots every dish in portrait so the same asset survives Reels, Stories, and TikTok without re-shooting.
Legacy trap: older Canva templates still ship at 800 × 800 or 1200 × 1200. Upgrade to 1080 × 1080 minimum — anything smaller looks fuzzy on 2026 Kenyan smartphones which routinely run at 2340 × 1080 native resolution.

2. Orientation-to-platform simulator

Pick a design orientation — the tool highlights which canvases accept it natively and which will crop or pillarbox your artwork.

Square (1:1): accepted natively by Meta feed. Meta / Instagram / TikTok Story canvases will pillarbox (grey bars top and bottom). LinkedIn and YouTube will center-crop the sides — logos in the corners get cut off.
Sarah's mistake: she posted a beautiful landscape food photo to her Instagram Story. Instagram cropped it to the vertical safe zone — the plate disappeared, only the tablecloth remained. Total waste. Pick the canvas before you design.

3. Three ratios that dominate mobile

Instead of memorising 6+ pixel sizes, memorise the three ratios that cover 95% of paid work:

RatioBest forSarah's use case
1:1 squareMeta feed, Instagram feedMenu cards, price posters
9:16 portraitStories, Reels, TikTok, Shorts, WhatsApp StatusBehind-the-scenes cooking, customer greetings, delivery-driver clips
16:9 / 1.91:1 landscapeYouTube thumbnails, LinkedIn, some Facebook link postsRecipe tutorial thumbnails, blog banners
Never design for 4:3 or 3:2. Those are print ratios. In 2026 nobody prints anything, and every social crop will destroy them.

Extra sizes worth memorising

  • Instagram profile picture: 320 × 320 uploaded, displayed as a 170 px circle — keep faces centred.
  • Facebook Page cover: 820 × 312 on desktop, cropped to ~640 × 360 on mobile — put the logo dead-centre.
  • X (Twitter) post image: 1200 × 675 preferred; header 1500 × 500.
  • Pinterest Pin: 1000 × 1500 vertical (2:3) — the only major platform where taller-than-16:9 still wins.
  • YouTube channel banner: 2560 × 1440 with a 1546 × 423 safe zone in the middle.

4. The safe-zone rule

Every platform crops differently on different devices. Protect critical content (logo, price, CTA, phone number) by leaving a safe zone — no critical content in the outer band.

  • Feed posts (1:1): keep 50 px in from every edge.
  • Stories & Reels (9:16): keep the top 220 px and bottom 320 px free of critical content — Instagram places its username, buttons, and caption there.
  • LinkedIn feed (1200×627): the ends get cropped on mobile — centre your subject.
  • YouTube thumbnail (1280×720): the bottom-right corner is often covered by the duration overlay — put your face and logo top-left.
Practical shortcut: In Canva, drop a rectangle guide sized to the safe zone, set it to 20% opacity, then design inside it. Delete the guide before exporting.

Sarah's three-template system

Sarah standardises on three Canva templates for the whole business — no more accidental wrong-size uploads:

  1. Instagram Feed Square — 1080 × 1080. Menu cards, price posters, quote tiles.
  2. Meta Story / Reel Vertical — 1080 × 1920. Reels, Stories, WhatsApp Status, TikTok — one asset, four surfaces.
  3. Facebook Feed Landscape — 1200 × 630 (close enough to LinkedIn 1200×627 for both). Link posts, blog banners, LinkedIn re-shares.
Free-plan workaround: Canva's Magic Resize is Pro-only. On the free plan, Sarah duplicates the design and manually re-frames — 5 minutes per asset. Batch on Sunday and it doesn't slow her week.
