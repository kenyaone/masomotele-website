2. What "trend-jacking" actually means

Trend-jacking is NOT posting your brand on top of a random trending sound. That is a corporate ad in a costume — viewers can smell it in three seconds.

Real trend-jacking is a two-step:

  1. Find a trending format, sound, or transition that fits your brand's world.
  2. Swap the original context for your product context while keeping the format intact.
Chapa Fitness example: A trending Nairobi TikTok sound goes viral for "when your matatu tout gives you exact change". Brian's swap: "when you find shorts that survive both leg-day AND a matatu ride". Same sound. Same rhythm. Chapa Fitness context. Native trend-jacking.

3. Hook Generator — 4 hook styles

TikTok's average watch time on a scrolled video before swipe-away is under 3 seconds. If the hook does not land, the ad dies. Four hook types to master. Pick one below to generate 3 first-line examples for Chapa Fitness.

Rule: every hook is a promise the video must keep. If the first three seconds tease something the rest doesn't deliver, retention collapses at second 8 and TikTok stops serving.

4. The 30- and 60-second arcs

Two useful lengths. Both start with the hook.

6. Challenge — pick the right trend-jack for Chapa Fitness

The scenario

Three trending sounds are on Creative Center this week for Kenya. Brian must pick which one to trend-jack for Chapa Fitness training shorts. Pick the best fit — this is auto-marked.

1. Which sound is the strongest trend-jack for Chapa Fitness?

Correct — the matatu sound is punchy, urban, and used for relatable everyday Nairobi moments. Brian's swap: "when you find shorts that survive both leg-day AND a matatu ride". Fits the brand's world without stretching.

2. Brian's video is 42 seconds. Where should the CTA land?

Correct — the CTA comes AFTER the hook, product story, and proof land. Rushing it kills conversion. The 60-second arc puts CTA at 55–60s; a 42-second video puts it in the last 4.

3. Auto-captions or burnt-in captions?

Correct — TikTok's auto-captions are inconsistent. Burnt-in captions match brand every time. Both Brian and Sarah use CapCut's caption template.
