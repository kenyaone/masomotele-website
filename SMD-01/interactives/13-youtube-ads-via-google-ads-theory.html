MODULE 13 · YOUTUBE MARKETING · LESSON 13.5

YouTube Ads via Google Ads

Where video advertising on YouTube actually lives, how it's set up, and how Sarah spends her KES 15,000/month

1. The rule beginners break

The single most common beginner mistake: opening YouTube Studio and looking for a "Boost this video" button. It doesn't exist there. YouTube video advertising is bought and managed entirely through Google Ads, at ads.google.com — a completely separate platform from YouTube Studio.

Remember this distinction: YouTube Studio is where you manage your channel and organic uploads. Google Ads is where you spend money to have a video shown to people who haven't chosen to watch it yet. They share your Google account, but they are two different tools with two different purposes.

2. The Video campaign type, and the ad formats inside it

Inside Google Ads, you create a new campaign and choose Video as the campaign type. From there, a video campaign can run several different ad formats — each suited to a different goal:

Skippable in-stream

Plays before/during another video; viewer can skip after 5 seconds. You only pay if they watch 30s or interact. Good for consideration.

Non-skippable in-stream

Up to 15 seconds, cannot be skipped. Guarantees the full message is seen — good for pure awareness, but pricier per view.

In-feed video ads

Shown in YouTube search results and the homepage feed, styled like a normal video thumbnail. Viewer chooses to click — good for consideration.

Bumper ads

Exactly 6 seconds, non-skippable. Built for quick, memorable awareness at a low cost per impression.

Kenyan-business rule of thumb: with a small monthly budget like Sarah's, skippable in-stream is usually the best starting format — you only pay when someone actually watches, so a tight budget stretches further than a non-skippable, pay-regardless format.

3. The targeting wizard, step by step

After picking the format, Google Ads walks you through targeting — the same wizard for every video campaign:

  1. Locations — for a Kenyan small business, this is locked to Kenya (or even narrower, e.g. Eldoret + a radius), not left worldwide by default.
  2. Languages — typically English and/or Swahili, matching how your audience actually searches and watches.
  3. Demographics — age range, gender, parental status, household income band. A common starting range for a general local business is ages 18–55, refined later using performance data.
  4. Audience segments — interests and "in-market" categories (people actively researching things like "restaurants" or "food delivery"), or your own remarketing list of past website visitors.
Common mistake: leaving location targeting on its broad default. An Eldoret restaurant advertising to all of Kenya (or worse, left un-set to a country at all) burns budget on viewers who could never become customers.

4. Budget: how KES is actually spent

Google Ads video campaigns are usually billed on a CPV (cost-per-view) basis for in-stream formats — you're charged only when someone watches 30 seconds (or the whole ad, if shorter) or interacts with it, not simply for it being shown.

  • Average daily budget — you set this in KES; Google Ads paces spending across the day and month, and may spend somewhat more on a strong day and less on a slow one, balancing out over the billing cycle.
  • Maximum CPV bid — the most you're willing to pay per qualifying view; a higher bid can win more competitive auctions but spends your budget on fewer total views.
Worked example: a KES 15,000 monthly budget is roughly KES 500/day. At a modest CPV of around KES 2–4 in a smaller market like Eldoret, that's very roughly 125–250 views per day — enough for a focused local campaign, not a national one.

5. Objective → format mapping

Google Ads asks for a campaign objective before you pick a format — matching the two correctly makes the campaign far more effective for the money spent.

ObjectiveBest-fit formatWhy
Brand awareness & reachBumper ads, non-skippable in-streamGuarantees the full (short) message is seen by as many people as possible.
Consideration (drive interest)Skippable in-stream, in-feedViewer opts in by watching or clicking, signalling real interest — and you only pay for that engagement.
Action (drive orders/visits)Skippable in-stream with a strong call-to-action + linkCombines a watched ad with a direct next step, like "Order on WhatsApp".
