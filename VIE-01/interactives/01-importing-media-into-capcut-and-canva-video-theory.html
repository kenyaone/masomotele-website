Importing Media into CapCut and Canva Video

Starting a New Project and Reaching the Gallery

In CapCut, everything begins with tapping New Project on the home screen. CapCut immediately opens the phone's Gallery/Albums tab so Otieno can select the footage he wants to bring in — there is no separate "import" step before this; selecting clips here is the import.

The first time CapCut needs this, the phone asks for permission to access photos and videos. If Otieno denies this, the Gallery tab stays empty, which is a common reason a new phone or client-borrowed device seems to have "no footage" available.

Selecting and Adding Clips in CapCut

Clips can be tapped one at a time or multi-selected in the Gallery grid, and CapCut numbers each clip in the order it was tapped. Tapping Add brings every selected clip onto the timeline in that same selection order — so for a shop promo made of five phone clips, Otieno should tap them in the order he wants them to play, not just in the order they happen to sit in the gallery.

If the order comes out wrong, nothing needs re-importing: clips can simply be dragged left or right along the timeline afterward to fix the sequence.

Importing into Canva Video

When Otieno edits a bigger job on his laptop in Canva Video, footage comes in through the Uploads tab on the left-hand panel instead of a phone Gallery. He can drag video files in directly from a folder on the laptop, or upload straight from a connected Google Drive if that's where the client's files were shared. Once uploaded, clips are dragged from the Uploads panel onto the timeline, the same way CapCut clips are dragged into place.

Common Import Pitfalls

Clients often forward footage over WhatsApp because it's easy, but WhatsApp automatically compresses video, throwing away detail and sharpness before Otieno ever sees the file. Where possible, he asks for the original file by cable transfer, Google Drive link, or direct phone-to-phone copy instead of a WhatsApp-forwarded copy, especially for paid promo work that clients expect to look sharp.

Right after import, it is worth a quick scroll through the timeline to confirm every clip landed in the right order and that nothing was accidentally imported twice.

Key Point. Import from the best-quality source available (original file, not a WhatsApp-compressed forward), select clips in the order they should play, and always double-check the timeline order right after import rather than discovering a mix-up halfway through editing.
