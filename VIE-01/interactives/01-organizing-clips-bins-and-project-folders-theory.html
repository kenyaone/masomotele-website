Unit 21 · Topic 2

Organizing Clips, Bins, and Project Folders

Why Organization Matters Before You Edit

A wedding shoot filmed on three different phones can easily produce 80+ unsorted clips of ceremony, speeches, reception, and drone footage all mixed together. Diving straight into CapCut or Canva Video with a pile of clips named "VID_2041.mp4" wastes far more time than organizing them first — every scene search becomes a guessing game.

Sorting footage before opening the editor is the video-editing equivalent of tidying a workbench before starting a repair: it turns "which clip was that again?" into "the one in the Speeches folder."

Building a Project Folder Structure

Otieno starts every event job with one main project folder named clearly for the client and event, for example Achieng_Wedding_2026. Inside it, he creates subfolders for each scene — Ceremony, Speeches, Reception, Drone — and copies each phone's raw clips into the matching subfolder before any editing begins.

This same folder becomes the single source Otieno imports from, whether he's editing on his phone in CapCut or on his laptop in Canva Video, so nothing gets left behind on someone else's phone.

Using CapCut's Media Panel as a Bin

CapCut does not have named "bins" the way desktop editors do, but its Media/Import panel works the same way in practice: once clips are imported, they sit in that panel and can be previewed before being dragged onto the timeline. Treating that panel like a bin — only dragging in clips from the already-sorted project folder, scene by scene — keeps the timeline itself from becoming just as messy as an unsorted camera roll.

Labeling Clips for Fast Retrieval

Before importing, Otieno renames the strongest clips in each subfolder with a clear label, such as Vows_Wide_01 or FirstDance_CloseUp_02, instead of leaving generic camera filenames. When he's later scrubbing through 40 imported clips looking for "the wide shot of the vows," a clear name saves minutes that add up across a whole project.

He also keeps the full raw dump of footage separate from a smaller "Selects" folder containing only the clips he actually plans to use — so the editing timeline is built from the Selects folder, not from wading through every unused clip each time.

Key Point. Sort footage into a clear folder structure by scene before opening the editor, label the clips worth using, and keep raw footage separate from your selects — the editor's media panel should only ever hold clips you've already organized, never an unsorted dump.
