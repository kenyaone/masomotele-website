Unit 21 · Topic 3

Project Setup — Aspect Ratio, Resolution, and Frame Rate

Aspect Ratio: Choosing the Right Canvas for the Platform

Aspect ratio is the shape of the video frame. A church event highlight destined for Instagram Reels, TikTok, or WhatsApp Status needs a tall 9:16 vertical canvas, since that's the shape those apps display full-screen on a phone. The same event's YouTube upload instead needs a wide 16:9 canvas, matching how YouTube displays video on both phones and larger screens. A square 1:1 canvas is sometimes used for feed posts rather than Reels.

Because CapCut and Canva Video let Otieno set the canvas ratio right when a project is created, he chooses it based on where the finished video will actually be posted — not by habit or guesswork.

Resolution: Balancing Sharpness and File Size

Resolution is how many pixels make up the video frame — common options are 720p, 1080p, and 4K. Higher resolution looks sharper, especially on a big screen or when the client zooms in, but it also produces a larger export file that takes longer to upload and more space to store. For most social and YouTube deliveries, 1080p gives a clean, professional look without an unnecessarily large file.

Frame Rate: Matching Footage to the Project

Frame rate is how many frames play per second — commonly 24, 25, 30, or 60fps. If Otieno shot some clips at 60fps for smooth slow-motion reception dancing and others at a standard 30fps, the project's frame rate should be set before footage is imported and edited, matching the rate most of the footage was actually shot at. Setting frame rate late, after clips are already arranged, risks stutter or unwanted slow-motion playback on clips that don't match the project setting.

Setting Up Correctly the First Time

Locking in aspect ratio, resolution, and frame rate at the very start of a project — before importing footage — means Otieno only has to make each decision once. If he instead changes the aspect ratio after every clip is already arranged on the timeline, every single clip may need repositioning or cropping to fit the new shape.

When a clip genuinely was filmed in the wrong ratio for a specific export (say, a 16:9 clip needed inside a 9:16 Reel), CapCut's Auto Reframe tool can automatically detect the subject and reposition the crop to keep it in frame within the new canvas, rather than Otieno manually repositioning every frame by hand.

Key Point. Decide the target platform first, then set aspect ratio, resolution, and frame rate before importing any footage — this avoids re-cropping or re-editing every clip later, and Auto Reframe is there for the exceptions where a clip was shot in the wrong ratio.
