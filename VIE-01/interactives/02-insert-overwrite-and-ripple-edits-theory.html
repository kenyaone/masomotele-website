Insert, Overwrite, and Ripple Edits

Insert Edits

An Insert edit adds a new clip into the timeline and pushes every clip after that point later in time to make room — nothing already on the timeline is lost or overwritten. In CapCut, dragging a new clip from the media library and dropping it into the gap between two existing clips does exactly this: the new clip slots in, and everything downstream shifts later by the new clip's length.

Otieno uses Insert whenever he wants to add footage — a cutaway, a drone shot, a reaction — without losing anything he has already arranged.

Overwrite Edits

An Overwrite edit replaces the content already sitting at a specific point on the timeline without shifting anything else's timing. In CapCut, this is done with the Replace tool: long-press the clip to swap out, tap Replace, and pick the new clip — it drops into that exact slot, keeping the same position and duration as before.

Otieno reaches for Overwrite when a placeholder or filler clip is already sitting in the right spot and he just needs to swap in the real footage, without disturbing anything before or after it.

Ripple Delete

A Ripple Delete removes a clip from the timeline entirely and automatically slides every clip after it earlier, closing the gap the deletion would otherwise leave. This is CapCut's default behaviour when you select a clip on the timeline and delete it — unlike simply overwriting a clip with black or silence, a ripple delete shortens the overall timeline by exactly the length of the removed clip.

Key Point. Insert adds new footage and lengthens the timeline; Overwrite swaps footage in place and leaves the timeline length unchanged (when the replacement is the same duration); Ripple Delete removes footage and shortens the timeline by closing the gap.
