Splitting and Trimming Clips on the Timeline

The Split Tool

In CapCut, tapping the Split icon while the playhead sits over a clip cuts that single clip into two separate clips at exactly that point. Otieno uses this constantly: he scrubs to the moment he wants to keep or remove, taps Split, and now has two independent clips he can delete, move, or trim on their own.

Splitting is non-destructive to the original media — it only changes how the clip is divided on the timeline, so Otieno can always undo a split or delete one half without damaging the other.

Trim Handles and Precise Timecodes

Every clip on the CapCut timeline has small trim handles at its left and right edges. Dragging the left handle inward moves the trim-in point later (removing the start of the clip); dragging the right handle inward moves the trim-out point earlier (removing the end). Zooming into the timeline lets Otieno see and set these points down to individual frames rather than rough seconds.

The clip's resulting duration is simply trim-out minus trim-in — if a 42-second raw clip is trimmed to run from 0:18 to 0:33, the kept segment is 15 seconds long, no matter how long the original clip was.

Why Exact Duration Matters

Otieno's export destinations each expect specific lengths: an Instagram Reel slot might need exactly 15 seconds, a WhatsApp Status clip is capped at 30 seconds, and a TikTok teaser might need to land at 60 seconds or under. Trimming isn't just about removing shaky footage — it's about landing on the exact runtime the platform and the client's brief call for.

Key Point. Always compute trim-out minus trim-in before exporting, and check both that the trim points fall inside the original clip's length and that the resulting duration matches what the platform slot actually needs.
