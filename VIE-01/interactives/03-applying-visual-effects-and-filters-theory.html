Applying Visual Effects and Filters

Filters vs Effects

In CapCut and Canva Video, a filter (found in the Filters tab) adjusts the overall color and mood of a clip: Vibrant boosts saturation and punch, Warm pushes toward orange/yellow tones, Cool pushes toward blue, Vintage adds a faded, slightly grainy look, and B&W removes color entirely. Filters are Otieno's everyday grading tool — something he applies to almost every clip he shoots.

An effect (found in a separate Effects tab) is a different kind of tool: Vignette, Glitch, VHS, Sharpen, and Zoom Blur layer a stylistic treatment on top of the image, beyond simple color grading. Effects are special-occasion tools — used for a hype intro or a stylistic beat — not something applied to every clip by default.

Reading What the Clip Needs

Footage shot indoors under weak fluorescent shop lighting, or on an overcast day through a window, often comes out dull and flat — low contrast, washed-out color. That kind of clip usually benefits from a Vibrant filter at a moderate intensity, sometimes with a light touch of Sharpen to bring back some crispness.

Footage that already looks rich and well-lit — golden-hour sunlight, a well-lit storefront sign — usually needs very little help. Piling filters and effects onto footage that already looks good tends to make it look overprocessed rather than better, because the "problem" the filter is meant to fix was never actually there.

Intensity and Restraint

Most filters in CapCut and Canva Video have an intensity slider, usually 0–100%. For a genuinely dull, flat clip, a moderate intensity — roughly 20–50% — is usually enough to bring the color back to life without looking artificial. Pushing a filter all the way to 100% often blows out colors or makes the image look unnaturally stylized rather than simply "corrected."

Stacking several heavy effects at once — Vintage plus Glitch plus Vignette, all at high intensity — can also work against the goal of a shop promo, where the point is to show the product clearly, not to look artsy. Keeping the same filter and intensity consistent across every clip in one promo also helps the finished video feel like one coherent piece rather than a patchwork of different looks.

Key Point. Match the strength of the fix to the actual problem: a moderate Vibrant filter (around 20-50%) for genuinely dull, flat footage; little to no filter for footage that already looks good. Save heavier effects (Glitch, VHS, Vintage) for stylistic, special-occasion moments rather than everyday shop-promo clips, and keep the grade consistent across all clips in the same video.
