Exposure, Contrast, and White Balance Basics

Exposure and Contrast

Exposure controls how bright or dark the overall clip looks. A clip filmed in a dim room — like a client's living room lit by one ceiling bulb — often comes out underexposed: dark, muddy, with details lost in shadow. In CapCut's Adjust panel, dragging the Exposure slider to the right brightens the image; dragging it left darkens it.

Contrast controls the gap between the darkest and lightest parts of the image. Raising contrast makes shadows deeper and highlights brighter, which can add punch to a flat-looking clip, but pushing it too far can crush shadow detail to pure black or blow highlights to pure white, losing information permanently.

White Balance and Color Temperature

Every light source has a color temperature, measured in kelvin. Ordinary indoor tungsten bulbs (the warm yellow ones common in Eldoret homes and small shops) sit around 2700–3200K, which reads on camera as a strong orange-yellow cast. Daylight sits much higher, around 5600K, and reads as neutral to slightly blue.

White balance correction cancels out that cast. In CapCut's Adjust panel, the Temp slider works on a scale from cool/blue (negative) to warm/orange (positive). A clip shot under tungsten light already looks too warm, so Otieno corrects it by dragging Temp toward the cool/blue side — a negative adjustment — to bring the footage back toward neutral. Overcorrecting too far the other way just replaces one wrong cast (orange) with another (blue).

Reading Before You Adjust

Before touching any slider, Otieno first names the problem in plain terms: is the clip too dark, too flat, too warm, too cool? Only then does he decide which slider to move and in which direction. Moving a slider without first identifying the actual problem often means guessing, and guessing tends to either undercorrect (the cast is still visible) or overcorrect (a new, different-colored problem appears).

Key Point. Diagnose first, then correct in the opposite direction of the problem: brighten an underexposed clip with a positive Exposure move, and cool down an orange, tungsten-lit clip with a negative Temp move — landing in a sensible target range rather than swinging to an extreme.
