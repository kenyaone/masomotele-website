Matching Shots for a Consistent Look

Why Shots Mismatch

A promo video often mixes footage from more than one source: Otieno's phone camera and a client's DSLR, or clips shot at different times of day. Even when each clip looks fine on its own, cutting between them can look jarring if one is noticeably brighter, darker, warmer, or cooler than the other — the viewer's eye catches the mismatch immediately, even if they couldn't explain exactly what changed.

Using a Reference Shot

The standard approach is to pick one shot as the reference — usually whichever one already looks the way the client wants the whole sequence to feel — and note its key values: its Exposure and Temp settings after correction. Every other shot in the same scene is then adjusted to bring its own Exposure and Temp as close to those reference values as reasonably possible, rather than each shot being corrected in isolation with no shared target.

Working Within a Tolerance

Because different cameras and lenses render color slightly differently, hitting the reference values exactly is rarely realistic or necessary. Otieno works within a tolerance — commonly within about ±5 units on Exposure and Temp — close enough that a viewer perceives the shots as matching, without demanding pixel-perfect precision that isn't achievable anyway.

Key Point. Pick a reference shot, note its Exposure and Temp values, and adjust every other shot in the same sequence to land within a small tolerance (roughly ±5) of those values — matching happens on top of individual correction, not as a replacement for it.
