Using Color Filters and Presets

Correction vs. Creative Look

Correction (exposure, contrast, white balance) fixes a clip so it looks natural and accurate. A filter or preset is a separate, additional step: a stylistic layer applied on top of a corrected clip to give it a particular mood — warm and golden, cool and moody, high-contrast and punchy, and so on. Applying a filter to an uncorrected clip usually just makes existing problems (like an orange tungsten cast) worse, so Otieno always corrects first and styles second.

Filters and Intensity in CapCut

CapCut's filter library groups presets by mood — names like Golden, Vibrant, Moody, Vintage, and more — each of which shifts color and tone in its own direction. Every filter comes with an intensity slider from 0% (no effect) to 100% (full strength).

A filter at 100% intensity is rarely the right choice: it tends to push color so far that skin tones look unnatural, shadows clip, or the image starts to look like a heavy Instagram filter rather than a graded video. A moderate intensity — commonly somewhere in the 40–70% range, depending on the filter and footage — usually keeps the mood while still looking like real footage.

Consistency Across a Project

For a multi-clip project like a wedding highlight reel, the same filter and the same intensity should be applied to every clip that needs the look, not a different filter per clip. Otieno typically settles on one filter and one intensity level after testing it on a representative clip, then applies that exact combination across the whole reel so the finished video feels like one consistent piece rather than a patchwork of different looks.

Key Point. Correct first, style second. Choose the filter that matches the mood the client wants, and keep intensity moderate (commonly 40-70%) and identical across every clip in the project — enough to feel like a genuine graded look, not so much that it becomes an obviously overprocessed effect.
