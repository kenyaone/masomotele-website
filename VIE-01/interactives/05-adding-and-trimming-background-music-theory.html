Audio Editing · Topic 1

Adding and Trimming Background Music

Adding a Music Track

In CapCut, Otieno taps Audio on the toolbar and chooses Sounds to browse CapCut's built-in commercial sound library, or Extract audio to pull the soundtrack out of another video he owns. The track drops onto its own audio row underneath the video track on the timeline. On his laptop, Canva's Video editor works the same way: an Audio panel on the left holds uploads and a stock-music library, and dragging a track onto the timeline places it on its own layer below the clips.

Otieno sticks to CapCut's commercial sound library rather than ripping audio from someone else's song, since tracks from the library are cleared for use on Instagram and TikTok and won't get a client's Reel muted or flagged after posting.

Trimming to Match Video Length

A downloaded track is rarely the exact length of the video it needs to sit behind, so Otieno drags the small handles at each end of the audio clip inward to set a trim-in point (where the music starts playing) and a trim-out point (where it stops). He watches the waveform while scrubbing to find the track's best hook or drop, then sets the trim-in there so the most energetic part of the song lines up with the start of the reel.

If an unwanted sound sits in the middle of the track instead of at either end — a stray vocal ad-lib, for instance — Otieno uses the Split tool to cut the clip on either side of it, deletes that middle piece, and uses ripple delete so the two remaining pieces snap together without leaving a silent gap.

Fading Out Cleanly

Ending a trimmed track with a hard, instant cut sounds jarring, so Otieno adds a short fade-out just before the trim-out point. In CapCut he drags the small triangular fade handle on the top corner of the audio clip, or opens the clip's Volume settings for exact Fade in / Fade out duration fields in seconds. Canva's Video editor has the same pair of Fade in/Fade out duration fields in its audio settings panel.

The fade-out duration has to fit inside the trimmed clip: a 1–2 second fade works cleanly on most reels, but a fade set almost as long as the whole trimmed clip eats into the track's usable content and makes the music sound like it is dying out for most of the reel instead of ending crisply right at the last second or two.

Key Point. Trim first to the exact length needed, choosing a trim-in point that keeps the track's best hook, then finish with a short 1–2 second fade-out that lands right at the trim-out point — never a hard cut and never a fade so long it swallows most of the clip.
