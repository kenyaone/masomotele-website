Audio Editing · Topic 3

Audio Levels, Fades, and Noise Reduction

Balancing Voice vs Music Volume

CapCut's volume control on each audio clip is a percentage slider, defaulting to 100% and adjustable well above or below that. When background music sits at the same volume as narration, the two fight for attention and the words become hard to follow. Otieno typically leaves his narration close to its recorded level (roughly 90–110%) and ducks the music underneath it, down to somewhere around 20–35%, so the track is still felt in the background without ever competing with his voice. Canva's Video editor offers the same kind of volume control on each audio layer.

Noise Reduction Without Harshness

A phone mic recording in a hall or outdoors often picks up a faint hiss or hum alongside the voice. CapCut's Noise Reduction effect, applied directly to the narration clip, uses a strength slider from 0 to 100 to reduce that background noise. The temptation is to push the slider to its maximum to erase every trace of hiss, but overly aggressive noise reduction strips too much from the voice itself, leaving it sounding metallic, muffled, or "underwater."

A moderate setting — typically somewhere around 40–60 — usually removes most of the hiss while keeping the voice sounding natural. A very faint trace of background noise is a smaller problem for a viewer than a voice that suddenly sounds robotic.

Bringing It Together

Otieno works in a deliberate order: first he cleans the narration clip with noise reduction, then he balances relative volumes (narration up front, music ducked underneath), and only after both of those are set does he add any fades. Doing noise reduction last, after volumes are already balanced, makes it harder to judge how aggressive the noise reduction setting really needs to be.

Key Point. Aim for narration that is clearly audible and free of harsh, artificial noise-reduction artifacts, with music felt underneath rather than competing with the voice — clean the audio first, balance the levels second, and add fades last.
