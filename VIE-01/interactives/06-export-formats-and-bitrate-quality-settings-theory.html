Export Formats and Bitrate/Quality Settings

Choosing a Format/Container

The export format (or container) is the file type the finished video is packaged in — MP4 using H.264 encoding is the standard, most widely compatible choice, playing back reliably on phones, WhatsApp, Instagram, YouTube, and almost anything else. Newer H.265/HEVC encoding can produce a similar-quality file at a smaller size, but is not always as universally supported on older phones or apps. MOV files (common from Apple devices and used for high-quality masters) tend to be larger and are mostly useful when handing off an uncompressed-feeling master to another editor, not for direct delivery to a client's phone.

For almost everything Otieno delivers — shop promos, wedding highlights, church event edits — MP4 (H.264) is the safe default format.

Bitrate and Quality Presets

Bitrate is how much data is used to encode each second of video, usually measured in megabits per second (Mbps). A higher bitrate preserves more visual detail, especially in fast motion or busy scenes, but produces a larger file. A lower bitrate shrinks the file but can introduce visible compression artifacts — blockiness or blur — especially in motion-heavy shots.

CapCut and Canva's Video editor simplify this on the export screen with quality presets like Low, Medium, and High (sometimes labelled by resolution and a quality slider), which quietly set the bitrate behind the scenes so Otieno doesn't have to type an exact number for every job.

Balancing File Size vs Quality for the Destination

The right balance depends entirely on where the video is going. A clip Otieno sends straight to a client over WhatsApp benefits from a smaller, more compressed export — lower resolution and bitrate keep the file small enough to send quickly over a mobile data connection, and WhatsApp re-compresses media anyway, so extra quality is often lost in transit regardless. A video uploaded to YouTube is the opposite case: YouTube has effectively no meaningful size cap for Otieno's content and re-encodes uploads on its own servers, so exporting at the highest resolution and bitrate he reasonably can gives YouTube the best source material to work from, resulting in a sharper final upload.

Key Point. Pick format and bitrate/quality around the destination, not out of habit: MP4 (H.264) at a lower bitrate and resolution for a fast WhatsApp send where file size matters most; MP4 (H.264 or H.265) at the highest bitrate and resolution reasonable for a YouTube upload where quality matters most and size is not the limiting factor.
