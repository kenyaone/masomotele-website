Compression and File Size Trade-offs

What Bitrate Controls

Bitrate is how much video data is used per second of playback, usually measured in megabits per second (Mbps). A higher bitrate keeps more picture detail and produces a cleaner image, especially in motion or fine texture (like beadwork at a wedding or fabric at a shop shoot), but the trade-off is a larger file. A lower bitrate shrinks the file but starts to show compression artefacts — blockiness, smudged detail, banding in skies or plain backgrounds — once pushed too far.

Estimating File Size

File size roughly follows a simple relationship: file size (megabits) ≈ bitrate (Mbps) × duration (seconds), then divided by 8 to convert megabits to megabytes. A 60-second clip exported at 8 Mbps is roughly (8 × 60) ÷ 8 = 60 MB. Dropping the bitrate to 4 Mbps for the same 60 seconds roughly halves that to about 30 MB — useful maths when Otieno needs to hit a specific size limit, like a client's email attachment cap or a WhatsApp Status upload limit.

Hitting a Target Size Without Ruining Quality

When a client needs a small preview file — say, emailing a 90-second highlight teaser and staying under a 25 MB inbox limit — Otieno works backward: target size × 8 ÷ duration gives the maximum bitrate he can use. In CapCut and Canva Video, export quality presets (Low/Medium/High, or a resolution + quality slider) roughly control this bitrate. Dropping resolution (e.g. exporting at 720p instead of 1080p) also shrinks file size at a given visual quality level, often with less visible damage than simply crushing the bitrate at full resolution.

There is a quality floor below which compression artefacts become obviously distracting to the client — blocky faces, muddy skin tones, smeared text overlays. Otieno's job is to find the smallest file that still clears that floor, not the smallest file possible.

Key Point. Estimate first, export second: target size × 8 ÷ duration (seconds) = the bitrate ceiling to aim for. If hitting that bitrate would drop below an acceptable quality floor, reduce resolution or trim duration instead of crushing quality further.
