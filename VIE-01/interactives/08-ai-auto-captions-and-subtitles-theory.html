AI Auto-Captions and Subtitles

What Auto Captions Actually Do

CapCut's Auto Captions feature listens to a clip's audio track and uses speech-recognition AI to guess the words being spoken, then places them on screen as timed subtitle text automatically. For Otieno, this is a huge time-saver on a client testimonial video — instead of typing out every word a customer says by hand, he can generate a full caption track in under a minute.

But Auto Captions is a transcription guess, not a guaranteed transcript. The AI is matching sound patterns to the words it thinks are most likely, and it has no idea that "Rift Valley Fashions" is a real shop name it should get right.

Where the AI Gets It Wrong

Speech-recognition AI struggles most with exactly the words that matter most on a client video: proper nouns like shop names, people's names, and local brand terms it has never encountered before. Background noise, overlapping speech, accents, and fast talking all increase the error rate further.

On a real Eldoret Reels Studio job, a testimonial from the owner of a shop called "Rift Valley Fashions" came back from Auto Captions reading "Rift Valley Passions" — a single misheard word that changes the client's own business name on screen. Homophones (their/there, whose/who's), dropped words, and wrong punctuation are just as common.

The Review-Before-Export Habit

The professional workflow is always the same: generate the auto-captions, then play the clip back from start to finish with the caption track visible, reading every line against what is actually being said, correcting anything the AI got wrong before touching export. This includes checking that captions are not cut off before a viewer can finish reading them, especially on fast cuts common in Reels-length videos.

Auto Captions is a first draft the AI hands Otieno, not a finished product. Treating it as finished and exporting straight away is how a client's own shop name ends up misspelled on a video meant to promote that shop.

Key Point. Never export an auto-captioned video without reading every caption line against the audio first. Auto Captions gives Otieno a fast starting draft, not a guaranteed-correct final subtitle track — the review step is not optional, especially for names and brand words the AI has never seen before.
