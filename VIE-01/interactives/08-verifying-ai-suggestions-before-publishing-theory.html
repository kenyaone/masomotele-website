Unit 194 · Lesson 3

Verifying AI Suggestions Before Publishing

AI Suggestions Are a Starting Point, Not a Final Answer

Across this unit, the same pattern keeps showing up: CapCut's Auto Captions guesses at spoken words, Auto Reframe guesses at what to keep in frame, and both CapCut and Canva's Video editor can auto-suggest things like background music tracks or a one-click auto colour grade. In every case, the AI is making its best statistical guess with no real knowledge of the specific client, their brand, or what they actually asked for.

That means every AI suggestion Otieno accepts into a finished video is really a decision he is making on the client's behalf, whether he reviews it or not. Treating an AI suggestion as automatically correct is the same as skipping a proofread — it just moves the mistake further downstream, to the moment the client sees it.

Building a Verification Checklist

Before export, Otieno runs through the same short checklist regardless of which AI tool was used: do the captions say what was actually spoken? Does the reframed crop keep every subject and graphic fully in view? Does the suggested music match the mood and genre the client asked for? Does an auto colour grade still match the client's real brand colours and product colours, rather than some generic preset look?

Any "no" on that checklist is not a reason to panic — it is exactly what the check is there to catch. The fix is always the same: adjust the AI's output to match the brief, using the manual tools CapCut and Canva provide, then re-check before exporting.

When AI Conflicts With the Client's Brief

The clearest test of this habit is when an AI suggestion actively conflicts with what the client asked for — for example, Canva's Video editor suggesting a slow, somber piano track and a cool blue auto colour grade for a shop's vibrant product promo, when the client explicitly asked for upbeat, warm, on-brand energy. In that moment, the AI's suggestion is simply wrong for this job, no matter how polished it looks.

The professional response is to replace the music with something that matches the brief, adjust the colour grade back toward the shop's real brand colours, and review the whole edit again before export. "The AI suggested it" is never an answer a client will accept if the final video does not match what they asked for — the AI does not carry that responsibility, Otieno does.

Key Point. Every AI suggestion in CapCut or Canva — captions, reframe crop, music, colour grade — is a draft, not a delivery. Always check it against what the client actually asked for, adjust anything that does not match, and only then export. AI is the starting point; Otieno's judgement is the final answer.
