Semantic HTML5

What "Semantic" Means

Semantic elements describe what their content actually is, not just how it should look — <header>, <nav>, <main>, <article>, <aside>, and <footer> all communicate real structural meaning, unlike a generic <div>.

Why It Matters Beyond Looks

Semantic structure helps search engines understand a page's content and importance, helps screen reader users navigate by landmark ("jump to main content"), and makes the underlying code easier for another developer to understand later.

Key Point. A page built entirely from generic &lt;div&gt;s can look identical to a semantic one visually, while being significantly harder to navigate, understand, or index.

Div Still Has a Place

Semantic elements do not replace <div> entirely — a generic <div> is still the right choice for a purely visual/styling wrapper with no real semantic meaning of its own.
