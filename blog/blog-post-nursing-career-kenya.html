What is a Certified Nursing Assistant (CNA)?

CNA Definition

A Certified Nursing Assistant is a trained healthcare professional who provides direct patient care under supervision of nurses and doctors.

Not a nurse. Not a doctor. But essential healthcare support.

What CNAs Actually Do

  • Patient hygiene: Bathing, grooming, toileting assistance
  • Vital signs: Take temperature, blood pressure, pulse monitoring
  • Patient comfort: Help patients move, change positions, prevent bed sores
  • Support nurses: Assist RNs with procedures and patient care
  • Documentation: Record patient information accurately
  • Infection control: Follow safety protocols to prevent disease spread

Training Path to Become a CNA

Step 1: Get Trained (6 Months)

  • Program: TVETA-accredited CNA Course
  • Duration: 6 months (full-time)
  • Cost: KES 59,000
  • What you learn: Patient care, first aid, infection control, communication
  • Where: MTTI or other TVETA-accredited institution

Step 2: Get Certified (1-2 Weeks)

  • Certification exam: TVETA assessment
  • Cost: KES 2,000-5,000
  • Duration: 1-2 weeks after course completion
  • Credential: National CNA Certificate

Step 3: Get Employed (1-4 Weeks)

  • Job hunting: Apply to hospitals, clinics, nursing homes
  • Interviews: Usually within 1-2 weeks
  • Hiring: Fast process (healthcare needs staff)
  • Start date: Often immediate or within 1 month

Total time from decision to employment: 7-8 months

Skills You'll Learn as a CNA

  • Patient Care: Bathing, grooming, toileting, feeding assistance
  • Vital Signs: Blood pressure, temperature, pulse, breathing monitoring
  • First Aid: Emergency response, CPR (cardiopulmonary resuscitation)
  • Infection Control: Hygiene, sterilization, safety protocols
  • Communication: Patient interaction, reporting, teamwork
  • Empathy & Compassion: Emotional support for patients
  • Documentation: Record-keeping, patient information accuracy

Common Concerns (Answered)

❓ "Is CNA training hard?"

Answer: No. It's practical and hands-on, not heavily academic. If you can learn patient care basics and follow instructions, you can pass.

❓ "Will I find a job after training?"

Answer: Yes. Healthcare has a chronic shortage of CNAs. Hospitals actively hire trained staff. Jobs are abundant.

❓ "Is the work physically demanding?"

Answer: Yes, moderate physical demands (standing, lifting, moving). But it's manageable and improves with experience.

❓ "Can I advance beyond CNA?"

Answer: Yes! Many CNAs pursue nursing (RN) or management roles. CNA is often a stepping stone.
