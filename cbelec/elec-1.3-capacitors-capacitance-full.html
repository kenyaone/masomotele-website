What is a Capacitor?

Structure, symbol and principle of operation

Definition & Structure

Capacitor: An electrical component that stores electrical energy in an electric field. It consists of two conducting plates separated by an insulating material called a dielectric.
Dielectric: The insulating material between the plates of a capacitor (e.g. air, paper, ceramic, electrolyte). It prevents current from flowing between the plates but allows the electric field to form.
Capacitance (C): The ability of a capacitor to store electric charge. Measured in Farads (F). In practice: microfarads (µF = 10⁻⁶F), nanofarads (nF = 10⁻⁹F), picofarads (pF = 10⁻¹²F).

The Capacitance Formula

📐 Key Formulas

Q = C × V  →  Charge (Coulombs) = Capacitance (F) × Voltage (V)

C = Q / V  →  Capacitance = Charge ÷ Voltage

C = ε₀ × εᵣ × A / d  →  depends on plate area (A), separation (d), dielectric constant (εᵣ)

Electrolytic and tantalum capacitors are polarised — they have a positive (+) and negative (−) terminal. Connecting them the wrong way around can cause explosion or fire. Always check the polarity before connecting.

Charging & Discharging a Capacitor

Voltage-time and current-time characteristics

How a Capacitor Charges

When a capacitor is connected to a DC supply through a resistor, it charges up. Current flows initially at maximum, then decreases as the capacitor voltage builds up. The capacitor is fully charged when its voltage equals the supply voltage — at which point current stops flowing.

Time Constant (τ): τ = R × C  (seconds). The time for the capacitor to charge to approximately 63.2% of the supply voltage. After 5τ the capacitor is considered fully charged (≈99.3%).

✏️ Time Constant Example

A 100µF capacitor charges through a 10kΩ resistor from a 12V supply.

τ = R × C = 10,000 × 100×10⁻⁶ = 1 second

After 1τ: V = 0.632 × 12 = 7.58V

After 5τ = 5 seconds: fully charged ≈ 12V

Capacitors in Series

Total capacitance decreases in series

Series Capacitor Formula

Series connection: Capacitors connected end-to-end. The same charge Q is stored on each capacitor. The total capacitance is less than any individual capacitor.
PropertySeries Capacitors
Total Capacitance1/CT = 1/C1 + 1/C2 + 1/C3 (decreases)
ChargeSame on each capacitor: QT = Q1 = Q2 = Q3
VoltageSplits: VT = V1 + V2 + V3
2 capacitors onlyCT = (C1 × C2) / (C1 + C2)

✏️ Worked Example — Series

C1=4µF, C2=6µF, C3=12µF in series with 24V supply.

1/CT = 1/4 + 1/6 + 1/12 = 3/12 + 2/12 + 1/12 = 6/12 = 1/2

CT = 2µF (less than smallest = 4µF ✓)

Q = CT × V = 2×10⁻⁶ × 24 = 48µC on each

V1=Q/C1=48/4=12V | V2=48/6=8V | V3=48/12=4V | Check: 12+8+4=24V ✓

Capacitors in Parallel

Total capacitance increases in parallel

Parallel Capacitor Formula

Parallel connection: All capacitors connected across the same two nodes. The same voltage appears across each. Total capacitance is the sum of all individual capacitances.
PropertyParallel Capacitors
Total CapacitanceCT = C1 + C2 + C3 (increases)
VoltageSame across all: VT = V1 = V2 = V3
ChargeSplits: QT = Q1 + Q2 + Q3

✏️ Worked Example — Parallel

C1=3µF, C2=5µF, C3=2µF in parallel with 10V supply.

CT = 3+5+2 = 10µF (greater than any individual ✓)

QT = CT × V = 10×10⁻⁶ × 10 = 100µC

Q1=3×10=30µC | Q2=5×10=50µC | Q3=2×10=20µC | Check: 30+50+20=100µC ✓

💡 Kenya Context — Traffic Light Timer

Many traffic light systems use RC timing circuits to control how long each light stays on. A capacitor charges through a resistor — when the voltage across the capacitor reaches a threshold level, the circuit switches the light. The time delay is controlled by the values of R and C: t = R × C.

⚠️ Capacitor Safety

Large capacitors (especially in power supplies and motor circuits) can store dangerous amounts of energy even after the power is switched off. Always discharge capacitors before working on a circuit. Use a discharge resistor — never short circuit directly.

Practice Problems

Apply capacitor calculations
Problem 1: Basic Capacitance

A capacitor stores a charge of 120µC when connected to a 24V supply. Calculate the capacitance.

📌 Solution

C = Q ÷ V = 120×10⁻⁶ ÷ 24 = 5µF

Problem 2: Series Capacitors

Three capacitors C1=2µF, C2=4µF, C3=8µF are connected in series to a 28V supply. Find: (a) total capacitance, (b) charge on each, (c) voltage across C1.

📌 Solution

(a) 1/CT = 1/2+1/4+1/8 = 4/8+2/8+1/8 = 7/8 → CT = 8/7 ≈ 1.14µF

(b) Q = CT × V = 1.14×10⁻⁶ × 28 = 32µC (same on each)

(c) V1 = Q/C1 = 32/2 = 16V

Problem 3: Parallel Capacitors

C1=10µF and C2=15µF are connected in parallel to a 20V supply. Find total capacitance and total charge stored.

📌 Solution

CT = 10+15 = 25µF

QT = CT × V = 25×10⁻⁶ × 20 = 500µC

Problem 4: Time Constant

A 470µF capacitor charges through a 4.7kΩ resistor from a 9V supply. Find: (a) the time constant, (b) voltage after one time constant, (c) time to fully charge.

📌 Solution

(a) τ = R×C = 4700 × 470×10⁻⁶ = 2.21 seconds

(b) V = 0.632 × 9 = 5.69V

(c) 5τ = 5 × 2.21 = 11.05 seconds
