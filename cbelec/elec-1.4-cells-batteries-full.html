The Simple Cell

How chemical energy becomes electrical energy

Principle of Operation

Cell: A device that converts chemical energy into electrical energy through an electrochemical reaction. It has two electrodes (anode and cathode) immersed in an electrolyte.
Battery: Two or more cells connected together to provide a higher voltage or current than a single cell.
EMF (Electromotive Force): The total voltage produced by a cell when no current is flowing. Measured in Volts (V).
Internal Resistance (r): The resistance within the cell itself. It causes a voltage drop when current flows: Terminal Voltage = EMF − (I × r)

Simple Cell Structure

✏️ Terminal Voltage Calculation

A cell has EMF = 1.5V and internal resistance r = 0.5Ω. Current drawn = 1A.

Terminal Voltage = EMF − (I × r) = 1.5 − (1 × 0.5) = 1.0V

The 0.5V is "lost" inside the cell due to internal resistance.

Series & Parallel Battery Arrays

Increasing voltage or current capacity

Cells Connected in Series

Series connection: Cells connected positive to negative in a chain. Voltage adds up, current capacity stays the same as one cell.

✏️ Series Example

3 × 1.5V cells in series → VT = 1.5+1.5+1.5 = 4.5V (same current capacity)

Cells Connected in Parallel

Parallel connection: All positive terminals joined together, all negative terminals joined together. Current capacity adds up, voltage stays the same as one cell.

✏️ Parallel Example

3 × 1.5V cells (each 2Ah) in parallel → V = 1.5V, Capacity = 2+2+2 = 6Ah

Use parallel when you need longer runtime but same voltage.

Use series when you need higher voltage.

The Lead-Acid Battery

The most common secondary battery in Kenya

Construction & Operation

Lead-acid battery: A secondary battery using lead (Pb) plates as electrodes and dilute sulphuric acid (H₂SO₄) as electrolyte. Each cell produces 2V. A 12V car battery has 6 cells in series.
ComponentMaterialRole
Positive plateLead dioxide (PbO₂)Cathode — oxidising agent
Negative plateSpongy lead (Pb)Anode — reducing agent
ElectrolyteDilute H₂SO₄ (30–40%)Ion conductor between plates
SeparatorMicroporous plastic/glassPrevents plates touching
CasePolypropyleneContains cells and electrolyte

State of Charge — Electrolyte Density

StateSpecific GravityCell Voltage
Fully charged1.26–1.28 g/cm³2.1V per cell
Half charged1.20–1.22 g/cm³2.0V per cell
Fully discharged1.10–1.15 g/cm³1.75V per cell

Use a hydrometer to measure specific gravity of the electrolyte — this tells you how charged the battery is.

⚠️ Lead-Acid Battery Hazards

• Sulphuric acid is highly corrosive — wear gloves and goggles when handling.
• Charging produces hydrogen gas — never charge in an enclosed space; keep away from sparks and flames.
• Lead is a toxic heavy metal — never dispose of in regular rubbish; use designated recycling points.

Correct charging keeps batteries healthy and safe

Lead-Acid Battery Charging Procedure

📋 Step-by-Step Charging Procedure

1. Inspect — Check for cracks, leaks, or corrosion on terminals.

2. Clean terminals — Remove corrosion with bicarbonate of soda solution and wire brush.

3. Check electrolyte level — Top up with distilled water only if low (not tap water).

4. Connect charger — Red (+) to positive terminal, Black (−) to negative terminal.

5. Set charge rate — Typically C/10 (10% of Ah capacity). E.g. 60Ah battery → 6A charge rate.

6. Charge in ventilated area — Hydrogen gas is produced during charging.

7. Monitor — Check voltage and temperature regularly. Stop if battery gets hot.

8. Disconnect — Remove charger when fully charged. Disconnect negative first.

⚠️ Overcharging & Undercharging

Overcharging — causes excessive gassing, plate damage, electrolyte loss, and heat. Can cause explosion.
Undercharging — causes sulphation of plates (lead sulphate crystals), permanently reducing capacity.

Improper disposal of hazardous waste including batteries is an offence under the Environmental Management and Coordination Act (EMCA). Always use NEMA-approved disposal channels.

Practice Problems

Apply knowledge of cells and batteries
Problem 1: Series Battery Array

Four 1.5V cells each with internal resistance 0.2Ω are connected in series. A load of 5Ω is connected. Find: (a) total EMF, (b) total internal resistance, (c) current flowing, (d) terminal voltage.

📌 Solution

(a) EMFT = 4 × 1.5 = 6V

(b) rT = 4 × 0.2 = 0.8Ω

(c) I = EMF ÷ (R+r) = 6 ÷ (5+0.8) = 6 ÷ 5.8 = 1.03A

(d) VT = EMF − I×r = 6 − (1.03×0.8) = 6 − 0.83 = 5.17V

Problem 2: Charging Time

A 12V, 45Ah lead-acid battery is to be charged at C/10 rate. Calculate: (a) the charging current, (b) approximate charging time.

📌 Solution

(a) I = 45 ÷ 10 = 4.5A

(b) Time = 45 ÷ 4.5 × 1.2 = 12 hours (×1.2 for charging efficiency)

Problem 3: Primary vs Secondary

State whether each application is best served by a primary or secondary cell, and give a reason:

  1. A TV remote control → Primary (alkaline) — low drain, not worth the cost of a charger
  2. A car starter motor → Secondary (lead-acid) — high current needed, must be recharged by alternator
  3. A laptop computer → Secondary (Li-ion) — needs recharging many times daily, high energy density
Problem 4: Maintenance Scenario

A car owner notices the battery terminals are covered in white/blue powder and the car is slow to start. What maintenance should they carry out? List the steps.

📌 Answer

1. Disconnect battery (negative first for safety).

2. Mix bicarbonate of soda in water to make a cleaning paste.

3. Apply paste to terminals and scrub with a wire brush to remove corrosion.

4. Rinse with clean water and dry thoroughly.

5. Apply petroleum jelly/Vaseline to terminals to prevent future corrosion.

6. Reconnect battery (positive first).

7. Check electrolyte level and charge state with hydrometer.
