Principle of Electromagnetism

Oersted's discovery — current creates magnetism

Oersted's Discovery (1820)

Electromagnetism: The relationship between electricity and magnetism. A moving electric charge (current) always produces a magnetic field around it. Conversely, a changing magnetic field can induce an electric current.
Oersted's Discovery: In 1820, Hans Christian Oersted discovered that a current-carrying wire deflects a nearby compass needle — proving that electric current produces a magnetic field around the conductor.

💡 Key Principle

Whenever current flows through a conductor, a circular magnetic field is created around it. The direction of the field depends on the direction of current flow. This is the basis of all motors, generators, transformers, relays, and electromagnets.

Factors Affecting Field Strength

FactorEffect on Field Strength
Current (I)More current → stronger magnetic field
Number of turns (N)More turns → stronger field (in a coil)
Core materialSoft iron core → much stronger field than air core
Coil lengthShorter, tighter coil → more concentrated field
Distance from wireField strength decreases with distance from conductor

Field Around a Current-Carrying Conductor

The Screw Rule and Right-Hand Rule

The Screw Rule (Maxwell's Corkscrew Rule)

Screw Rule: Imagine a right-handed screw being driven in the direction of conventional current flow. The direction of rotation of the screw gives the direction of the magnetic field (clockwise when viewed from front).

Right-Hand Grip Rule (for a Coil/Solenoid)

Right-Hand Grip Rule: Wrap the right hand around the coil so that the fingers point in the direction of conventional current flow in the coil. The extended thumb points toward the North pole of the electromagnet.

✏️ How to Apply the Right-Hand Grip Rule

1. Look at the coil and identify which way conventional current flows in the wires.

2. Curl the fingers of the right hand in the direction of current flow around the coil.

3. The thumb points to the North pole end of the solenoid/coil.

Remember: Conventional current flows from positive (+) to negative (−) in the external circuit.

The Solenoid

A coil that acts like a bar magnet

What is a Solenoid?

Solenoid: A cylindrical coil of wire wound in a helix. When current flows through it, it produces a magnetic field similar to a bar magnet, with a well-defined North and South pole at each end.
ParameterEffect on Solenoid Strength
Increase current (I)Stronger magnetic field
Increase number of turns (N)Stronger magnetic field
Add soft iron coreMuch stronger field (×100–1000)
Decrease coil lengthMore concentrated field per unit length
Reverse current directionNorth and South poles swap
Magnetomotive Force (MMF): MMF = N × I (Ampere-turns, At). The driving force that establishes magnetic flux in a magnetic circuit. More turns × more current = stronger electromagnet.

Electromagnetic Devices

Electric bell, relay, and magnetic pick-up

The Electric Bell

Electric bell: Uses an electromagnet to repeatedly attract and release a metal striker against a gong, producing sound. It uses a self-interrupting circuit (make-and-break mechanism).

📋 How the Electric Bell Works

1. Current flows → electromagnet energises → attracts the armature (spring metal strip).

2. Armature bends toward electromagnet → hammer strikes the gong (ding!).

3. Armature movement breaks the contact at the contact screw → circuit opens.

4. No current → electromagnet de-energises → spring pulls armature back → contact remakes.

5. Circuit closes again → cycle repeats rapidly → continuous ringing.

The Relay

Relay: An electromagnetic switch that uses a small current in a control circuit to switch a larger current in a separate load circuit. It provides electrical isolation between circuits.
PartFunction
Coil (control circuit)Creates magnetic field when energised by small control current
Iron coreConcentrates and strengthens magnetic field
ArmatureMetal lever attracted by the electromagnet
Contacts (NO/NC)Switch the load circuit — Normally Open closes when energised; Normally Closed opens
Return springReturns armature to rest position when coil is de-energised

💡 Why Use a Relay?

• A microcontroller outputting 5V/20mA can control a 240V/10A motor — electrical isolation.

• Remote switching — control a high-power circuit from a safe distance.

• Used in cars: starter motor relay, horn relay, headlight relay.

• Used in industrial control panels to switch motors and heaters.

Electromagnetic Induction

Magnetism creating electricity — Faraday's Law

Faraday's Law of Electromagnetic Induction

Electromagnetic induction: The production of an EMF (voltage) in a conductor when there is a change in the magnetic flux linking that conductor. Discovered by Michael Faraday in 1831.
Faraday's Law: The magnitude of the induced EMF is proportional to the rate of change of magnetic flux: EMF = −N × ΔΦ/Δt
Lenz's Law: The induced current flows in a direction such that it opposes the change in flux that caused it (hence the minus sign in Faraday's Law).

Conditions for Electromagnetic Induction

ConditionExample
Conductor moving through a magnetic fieldGenerator coil rotating in a magnet
Magnetic field changing around a stationary conductorTransformer — changing flux in core induces EMF in secondary
Conductor moving relative to the field (either can move)Moving magnet near a coil induces current
Fleming's Right-Hand Rule (Generator Rule): For a conductor moving through a magnetic field, point the thumb, index finger, and middle finger of the right hand at 90° to each other. Thumb = Motion, Index = Field (N→S), Middle finger = induced Current direction.

✏️ Induced EMF Calculation

A coil of 200 turns experiences a flux change of 0.05Wb in 0.1 seconds. Find the induced EMF.

EMF = N × ΔΦ/Δt = 200 × 0.05/0.1 = 200 × 0.5 = 100V

Fault-Finding Procedure

📋 Systematic Approach

1. Observe — Does the device produce any magnetic effect? Any unusual sounds, heat, or smell?

2. Isolate — Switch off supply before probing with ohmmeter.

3. Test coil resistance — Measure with ohmmeter. Compare to rated resistance on datasheet.

4. Check supply voltage — Is correct voltage reaching the coil terminals?

5. Check current — Use ammeter in series. Compare to rated current.

6. Inspect physically — Look for burnt insulation, broken wires, corroded terminals.

7. Test load contacts (for relay) — Check continuity of NO/NC contacts.

⚠️ Safety When Testing Electromagnetic Circuits

Always isolate the supply before using an ohmmeter — ohmmeters have their own internal battery and will give false readings (or be damaged) if external voltage is present. When using a voltmeter to check a live circuit, use insulated probes and keep one hand behind your back.

Practice Problems

Apply electromagnetism concepts
Problem 1: MMF Calculation

An electromagnet coil has 800 turns and carries a current of 1.5A. Calculate the MMF and state how you could double the MMF without increasing the current.

📌 Solution

MMF = N × I = 800 × 1.5 = 1200 At (Ampere-turns)

To double MMF without increasing current: double the number of turns to 1600.

Problem 2: Induced EMF

A coil of 500 turns has a magnetic flux that changes from 0.02Wb to 0.08Wb in 0.4 seconds. Calculate the induced EMF.

📌 Solution

ΔΦ = 0.08 − 0.02 = 0.06 Wb

EMF = N × ΔΦ/Δt = 500 × 0.06/0.4 = 500 × 0.15 = 75V

Problem 3: Relay Application

A 5V microcontroller output cannot directly switch a 240V, 5A motor. Explain how a relay solves this problem, and state what type of relay contact (NO or NC) would be used to switch the motor ON when the microcontroller outputs HIGH.

📌 Answer

The microcontroller energises the relay control coil (5V, low current). The coil creates an electromagnetic field that attracts the armature, closing the relay contacts. This allows the 240V motor circuit to complete — electrically isolated from the 5V circuit.

Contact type: Normally Open (NO) — when the coil is de-energised the motor is OFF; when energised the NO contact closes, switching the motor ON.

Problem 4: Fault Finding

A relay in a car's horn circuit stops working. The horn worked previously. Describe a systematic procedure to locate the fault, naming the instruments you would use at each step.

📌 Answer

1. Visual inspection — Look for burnt relay, corroded terminals, broken wires.

2. Voltmeter — Check supply voltage (12V) is reaching the relay coil terminals when horn button pressed.

3. Ohmmeter (power off) — Measure relay coil resistance; compare to spec. ∞ = open circuit coil. ~0Ω = short circuit.

4. Ohmmeter — Check continuity of load contacts (NO contacts should close when 12V applied to coil).

5. If relay is OK — check horn itself with direct 12V connection. If horn sounds → fault is in wiring, not relay.
