Methods of Generating Electricity

Converting other forms of energy into electrical energy

Key Definition

Electrical energy generation: The process of converting other forms of energy (kinetic, thermal, chemical, solar, nuclear) into electrical energy using generators or other devices.

💡 Kenya's Energy Mix (2024)

Kenya's electricity is predominantly renewable: Geothermal (~44%), Hydro (~27%), Wind (~13%), Solar (~3%), Thermal (~13%). Kenya targets 100% clean energy by 2030.

Step-up transformerRaises generator voltage (11kV) to transmission voltage (132kV–400kV)

Why Step Up Voltage for Transmission?

Power loss formula: Ploss = I² × R. Transmission lines have resistance. If voltage is stepped up, current is reduced for the same power (P = V × I), so line losses (I²R) are dramatically reduced.

✏️ Why High Voltage Saves Energy

Transmit 100MW over a line with resistance 10Ω:

At 11kV: I = 100MW/11kV = 9,091A → Ploss = 9091² × 10 = 826MW lost! (impossible)

At 220kV: I = 100MW/220kV = 455A → Ploss = 455² × 10 = 2.07MW lost (only 2%)

Higher voltage = much lower current = much less power wasted as heat in cables.

The 3-Phase 4-Wire System

How electricity is distributed to consumers

What is 3-Phase Supply?

3-phase supply: Three separate AC voltages of equal magnitude, each displaced 120° from the others. Generated by a 3-phase alternator with three sets of coils arranged 120° apart.
3-phase 4-wire system: Three phase conductors (L1, L2, L3) plus one neutral conductor (N). This provides both single-phase (240V) and three-phase (415V) supply from the same system.
ConnectionVoltageUsed For
Phase to Neutral (L-N)240V ACHomes, small offices — lighting, sockets
Phase to Phase (L-L)415V ACIndustrial motors, large machines, 3-phase equipment
3-Phase 4-wire system240V/415VDistribution to mixed domestic/industrial areas

✏️ Voltage Relationship

Line voltage (VL) = √3 × Phase voltage (Vph)

415V = 1.732 × 240V ✓

This relationship is fundamental to all 3-phase calculations.

The Kenya National Power Grid

How electricity reaches your home

What is a Grid System?

National Grid: An interconnected network of power stations, transmission lines, and substations that distributes electricity across the country. In Kenya, it is managed by KPLC (Kenya Power and Lighting Company) and KETRACO (Kenya Electricity Transmission Company).

Importance of the Grid System

💡 Why the Grid Matters for Kenya

1. Reliability — If one power station goes offline, others can compensate. Grid prevents total blackouts.

2. Efficiency — Power can be routed from where it is surplus to where it is needed, reducing waste.

3. Economic development — Reliable electricity enables industry, hospitals, schools, and businesses to operate.

4. Last Mile Connectivity — The grid enables rural electrification to reach every Kenyan home.

5. Regional integration — Kenya exports power to Uganda, Tanzania, Ethiopia — East Africa Power Pool (EAPP).

6. Renewable integration — Grid balances variable solar and wind with steady hydro and geothermal.

Transformers in the Grid

The key to efficient power transmission

The Transformer

Transformer: An electromagnetic device that transfers electrical energy between two circuits through electromagnetic induction, while changing (stepping up or down) the voltage. Works on AC only.
Turns ratio: N1/N2 = V1/V2 = I2/I1. The ratio of primary to secondary turns determines the voltage transformation. More turns on secondary = step-up. Fewer turns = step-down.
Transformer TypeVoltage ChangeTurns RatioWhere Used
Step-upIncreases voltage (decreases current)N1 < N2Power station output → transmission
Step-downDecreases voltage (increases current)N1 > N2Substations, distribution transformers
Isolating1:1 ratio (no change)N1 = N2Safety isolation in labs, workshops

Transformer Calculations

✏️ Transformer Worked Examples

Example 1: A step-up transformer has 500 primary turns and 10,000 secondary turns. Primary voltage = 11kV. Find secondary voltage.

V2 = V1 × (N2/N1) = 11,000 × (10,000/500) = 11,000 × 20 = 220,000V = 220kV

Example 2: A distribution transformer steps down 11kV to 240V. If primary current is 5A, find secondary current (assume ideal transformer).

I2 = I1 × (V1/V2) = 5 × (11,000/240) = 5 × 45.83 = 229.2A

⚠️ High Voltage Safety

Transmission line voltages (132kV–400kV) are instantly lethal even without direct contact. Electricity can arc through air over many metres at these voltages. Always observe exclusion zones around pylons and substations. Never fly kites, drones, or climb near power lines.

Practice Problems

Generation, transmission and transformers
Problem 1: Transformer Calculation

A step-up transformer at a power station has 200 primary turns and 4,000 secondary turns. The generator produces 11kV at 100A. Find: (a) secondary voltage, (b) secondary current, (c) power transmitted (assume ideal).

📌 Solution

(a) V2 = V1 × N2/N1 = 11,000 × 4000/200 = 11,000 × 20 = 220,000V = 220kV

(b) I2 = I1 × N1/N2 = 100 × 200/4000 = 100 × 0.05 = 5A

(c) P = V1 × I1 = 11,000 × 100 = 1.1MW (same on both sides for ideal transformer)

Problem 2: Transmission Line Loss

50MW is transmitted over a line with resistance 8Ω at 132kV. Calculate: (a) line current, (b) power lost in the line, (c) efficiency of transmission.

📌 Solution

(a) I = P/V = 50,000,000 / 132,000 = 378.8A

(b) Ploss = I²R = 378.8² × 8 = 143,489 × 8 = 1.148MW

(c) η = (P − Ploss) / P × 100 = (50 − 1.148) / 50 × 100 = 97.7%

Problem 3: 3-Phase Voltages

A 3-phase 4-wire distribution system has a phase voltage of 240V. Calculate the line voltage and explain what load would use each voltage level.

📌 Solution

Vline = √3 × Vphase = 1.732 × 240 = 415.7V ≈ 415V

240V (phase-neutral): Domestic use — lighting, TVs, fridges, phone chargers.

415V (line-line): Industrial motors, large air conditioners, 3-phase machinery.

Problem 4: Grid Importance

Explain why Kenya's government has invested heavily in expanding the national power grid to rural areas under the Last Mile Connectivity programme.

📌 Answer

• Economic development — Electricity enables small businesses, agro-processing, and cottage industries to operate, creating jobs and income.

• Education — Schools can use computers, projectors, and the internet for better learning outcomes.

• Healthcare — Rural health facilities can power medical equipment, refrigerate vaccines, and provide 24-hour services.

• Quality of life — Replaces kerosene lamps (which cause respiratory diseases and fires) with clean, safe lighting.

• Vision 2030 — Universal electricity access is a key pillar of Kenya's development blueprint.
