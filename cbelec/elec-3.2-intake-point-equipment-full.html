The Consumer Intake Point

Where the supply network meets your building

What is the Intake Point?

Consumer intake point: The point at which the electricity distribution network (KPLC) connects to the consumer's internal wiring. All metering and protection equipment is installed here.
Service cable: The cable connecting the KPLC distribution transformer pole to the consumer's intake point. It is owned and maintained by KPLC.

Correct Sequence of Equipment at the Intake Point

⚠️ Why Sequence Matters

Equipment must be installed in this exact order so that each device protects everything downstream from it. If the sequence is reversed, faults in earlier equipment cannot be isolated without cutting off devices that should remain active. The meter must always be AFTER the isolation switch so KPLC can isolate before reading.

Isolation Switch & Energy Meter

First two components at the intake point

① The Isolation Switch (Service Cutout)

Isolation switch / Service cutout: The first device at the intake point. It belongs to KPLC and is sealed. It completely disconnects the consumer from the supply network. Contains a service fuse to protect the supply cable.
FeatureDetail
OwnerKPLC — consumer must NOT tamper with it
TypeDouble-pole (disconnects both live and neutral)
PurposeIsolates entire installation from supply; protects service cable
RatingTypically 60A, 80A, or 100A for domestic
ContainsService fuse — protects the supply cable from overload

② The Energy Meter (kWh Meter)

Energy meter (kWh meter): Measures the electrical energy consumed by the customer in kilowatt-hours (kWh). Used by KPLC for billing. One unit on the bill = 1 kWh.
FeatureDetail
Unit measuredKilowatt-hours (kWh) — 1 kWh = 1 unit of electricity
TypesElectromechanical (spinning disc) or Electronic/Smart meter
OwnerKPLC — sealed, not to be tampered with
ReadingDifference between current and previous reading = units consumed
Smart metersPrepaid token system (M-Pesa payment) — increasingly used in Kenya

✏️ Reading a Meter

Previous reading: 1,245 kWh  |  Current reading: 1,412 kWh

Units consumed = 1,412 − 1,245 = 167 kWh

At KSh 25/kWh: Bill = 167 × 25 = KSh 4,175

Main Switch & Cartridge Fuse

Consumer-side control and protection

③ The Main Switch

Main switch: A double-pole switch that allows the consumer to isolate the entire internal installation from the supply. Unlike the service cutout (KPLC's), this belongs to the consumer and can be operated at any time.
FeatureDetail
TypeDouble-pole — switches both live and neutral simultaneously
OwnerConsumer
PurposeConsumer can isolate entire installation for maintenance or emergency
RatingMust be rated at least equal to the service fuse (e.g. 60A, 100A)
PositionAfter the meter — so KPLC can still read the meter when installation is isolated

④ The Cartridge Fuse

Cartridge fuse: A replaceable fuse in a ceramic or glass tube, providing overcurrent protection. It melts when current exceeds its rated value, breaking the circuit and protecting wiring from damage.
FeatureDetail
OperationThin wire inside melts when current exceeds rating
RatingCommon domestic ratings: 5A, 15A, 20A, 30A, 60A
Advantage over rewirableConsistent performance; no risk of wrong wire being fitted
After operationMust be replaced — cannot be reset like a circuit breaker
Selection ruleFuse rating must be just above normal load current but below cable rating

⚠️ Never Bypass a Fuse!

Inserting a wire or coin in place of a blown fuse removes all overcurrent protection. This can cause cable fires, electrocution, and complete destruction of the installation. Always replace with a correctly rated fuse.

The Consumer Control Unit (Distribution Board)

Heart of the internal wiring system

What is the Consumer Control Unit?

Consumer Control Unit (CCU) / Distribution Board (DB): A panel containing the main switch, individual circuit breakers or fuses for each final circuit, and the neutral and earth bus bars. It distributes electricity to all circuits in the building.
ComponentFunction
Main switch (in DB)Isolates all circuits simultaneously
MCBs (Miniature Circuit Breakers)Automatically trip on overload or short circuit; can be reset
RCD (Residual Current Device)Detects earth leakage current (as small as 30mA) and trips — protects against electrocution
Live bus barCommon live connection point for all MCBs
Neutral bus barCommon neutral connection for all circuits
Earth bus barCommon earth connection — bonded to earth electrode

Consumer Earthing System

Safety through earth connection

Why Earthing is Essential

Earthing: Connecting the metallic parts of an electrical installation to the general mass of earth (ground) via a low-resistance conductor. This ensures that if a live conductor touches metallic parts, fault current flows safely to earth instead of through a person.
Earth electrode: A conductor buried in the ground to make electrical contact with the earth. Common types: earth rod (copper-clad steel stake), earth plate (copper plate buried horizontally), earth tape (strip buried in trench).

How to Install an Earth Electrode (Earth Rod)

📋 Earth Rod Installation Procedure

1. Select a suitable location — moist soil away from paved areas; close to the DB.

2. Drive the copper-clad steel rod vertically into the ground using a driving tool. Minimum depth: 1.2m (deeper is better — more moisture = lower resistance).

3. If rock is encountered, drive at 45° angle or use earth plate instead.

4. Connect earth conductor (green/yellow cable, min 16mm² for main earth) to the rod terminal using a clamp.

5. Run the earth conductor inside conduit from the rod to the earth bus bar in the DB.

6. Test earth resistance with an earth tester — should be less than 1Ω for TN systems or less than 200Ω for TT systems.

7. Cover the rod head with an inspection pit or mark its location.

Earth Resistance Requirements

System TypeMax Earth ResistanceProtection Device
TN-S (separate earth from neutral)<1ΩMCB or fuse
TT (earth electrode separate from supplier)<200ΩRCD mandatory
Domestic (Kenya typical)<1Ω preferredMCB + RCD

⚠️ Without Proper Earthing

If a fault causes a live conductor to touch the metal casing of an appliance, anyone touching it will receive a potentially fatal electric shock. Proper earthing ensures the fault current flows to earth, trips the protective device, and makes the appliance safe.

Regulations & Compliance

Legal requirements for electrical installations in Kenya

KPLC Connection StandardsSpecifications for intake point equipment; metering requirements
IEE Wiring Regulations (BS 7671)International standard commonly referenced in Kenya

Key Compliance Requirements

📋 Before KPLC Connects Your Supply

1. All wiring must be done by a licensed electrician registered with EPRA.

2. An Electrical Inspection Certificate must be obtained from a licensed inspector.

3. The intake point equipment must be correctly installed in sequence.

4. Earthing must be tested and meet resistance requirements.

5. All wiring must use approved cable sizes and types as per KEBS standards.

6. KPLC installs the service cable and meter — the consumer provides everything after.

Activity A: Sequence Knowledge

List the equipment at the consumer intake point in the correct order from the supply, and state ONE function of each:

📌 Model Answer

1. Service cable — brings supply from KPLC distribution transformer to the premises.

2. Isolation switch/cutout — KPLC's device; isolates entire installation from supply; contains service fuse.

3. Energy meter — measures kWh consumed for billing purposes.

4. Main switch — consumer's double-pole switch to isolate internal installation.

5. Cartridge fuse — overcurrent protection before distribution board.

6. Consumer control unit — distributes supply to individual circuits via MCBs.

Activity B: Earthing Calculation

An earth rod is driven into soil. The earth tester reads 45Ω. Is this acceptable for a TT system? What steps can reduce the earth resistance?

📌 Answer

TT system max = 200Ω — 45Ω is acceptable for a TT system. However, lower is always better.

To reduce earth resistance:

• Drive the rod deeper into moist soil.

• Use multiple rods connected in parallel.

• Add bentonite clay around the rod to retain moisture.

• Use a larger surface area earth plate instead of a rod.

Activity C: Meter Reading

A house meter read 5,678 kWh in January and 5,923 kWh in February. KPLC charges KSh 20/kWh for the first 50 units and KSh 30/kWh for additional units. Calculate the bill.

📌 Solution

Units consumed = 5,923 − 5,678 = 245 kWh

First 50 units: 50 × 20 = KSh 1,000

Remaining 195 units: 195 × 30 = KSh 5,850

Total bill = 1,000 + 5,850 = KSh 6,850

Activity D: Fault Scenario

A homeowner's lights keep going off but the fridge and TV still work. Which protective device has operated and what is the likely fault?

📌 Answer

Only the lighting circuit MCB has tripped — not the main switch (fridge and TV still work, meaning other circuits are fine).

Likely fault: Overload or short circuit on the lighting circuit — perhaps too many lights connected, a faulty light fitting, or damaged cable causing a short circuit.

Action: Switch off all lights → reset the MCB → switch lights on one at a time to identify the faulty fitting → repair or replace it.
