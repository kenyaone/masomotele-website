The Semiconductor Diode

Structure, symbol and basic operation

What is a Diode?

Semiconductor diode: A two-terminal electronic device made from a PN junction that allows current to flow in one direction only (forward bias) and blocks it in the other direction (reverse bias). It acts like an electronic one-way valve.
Anode (A): The P-type terminal. Positive terminal. Current enters the diode here during forward bias.
Cathode (K): The N-type terminal. Negative terminal. Current exits the diode here. Marked with a band/stripe on the physical component.

Half-Wave Rectifier

Converting AC to DC — using one diode

Rectification

Rectification: The process of converting AC (alternating current) into DC (direct current) using diodes. Essential for all power supplies — phone chargers, computers, TVs all use rectifier circuits.
Half-wave rectifier: Uses ONE diode. Only the positive half-cycle of AC passes through — the negative half is blocked. Output is pulsating DC at the same frequency as the AC input.

✏️ Half-Wave Rectifier Key Values

• Average (DC) output voltage: Vdc = Vpeak / π ≈ 0.318 × Vpeak

• RMS output voltage: Vrms = Vpeak / 2 = 0.5 × Vpeak

• Ripple frequency = input frequency (50Hz in Kenya)

• Efficiency: ~40.6% — low, as half the cycle is wasted

Full-Wave Rectifier

Bridge rectifier — using four diodes

Full-Wave Bridge Rectifier

Full-wave rectifier: Uses FOUR diodes in a bridge configuration. Both positive and negative half-cycles are converted to DC. Output is smoother with less ripple and double the ripple frequency compared to half-wave.

The Zener Diode — Voltage Regulator

Zener diode: Specially designed to operate in the reverse breakdown region at a precise voltage (Zener voltage, Vz). Unlike regular diodes, it does not get damaged by controlled reverse breakdown. Used to provide a stable reference voltage.

✏️ Zener Voltage Regulator

A 5.1V Zener diode connected in reverse across a load will maintain the load voltage at exactly 5.1V regardless of input voltage variations (within limits) or load current changes. Used in chargers, voltage references, and microcontroller supply rails.

Testing a Diode with an Ohmmeter/DMM

📋 Diode Test Procedure

Using Ohmmeter (analogue):

1. Forward bias test: Connect red probe to anode, black to cathode → should read LOW resistance (10–50Ω for signal diode).

2. Reverse bias test: Reverse probes → should read HIGH resistance (>100kΩ ideally ∞).

3. Good diode: Low one way, high the other.

4. Open diode: High in BOTH directions.

5. Short circuit: Low in BOTH directions.

Using DMM Diode Mode: Forward should read 0.5–0.7V (Si); reverse shows OL (overload = open circuit = good in reverse). Much more reliable method.

⚠️ PIV Rating

PIV (Peak Inverse Voltage): The maximum reverse voltage a diode can withstand without breakdown. Always select a diode with PIV rating greater than the peak AC voltage in the circuit. For 240V AC mains: Vpeak = 240 × √2 = 339V — use a diode rated at least 400V PIV.

Practice Problems

Apply diode circuit knowledge
Problem 1: Rectifier Output

A full-wave bridge rectifier is connected to a 240V AC mains supply (50Hz). Each diode has a forward voltage drop of 0.7V. Calculate: (a) peak AC voltage, (b) peak output voltage, (c) average DC output voltage.

📌 Solution

(a) Vpeak = Vrms × √2 = 240 × 1.414 = 339.4V

(b) Vout peak = Vpeak − 2×Vf = 339.4 − 1.4 = 338V (2 diodes conduct at once in bridge)

(c) Vdc = 0.636 × Vout peak = 0.636 × 338 = 214.9V ≈ 215V DC

Problem 2: LED Circuit

An LED (Vf = 2.2V, If = 20mA) is to be connected to a 9V supply. Calculate the series resistor needed and its power rating.

📌 Solution

R = (Vs − Vf) / If = (9 − 2.2) / 0.020 = 6.8 / 0.020 = 340Ω → use nearest standard value: 330Ω or 360Ω

P = I² × R = (0.020)² × 340 = 0.000400 × 340 = 0.136W → use 0.25W or 0.5W resistor

Problem 3: Fault Identification

A technician tests a diode with an ohmmeter. In forward bias the reading is very high (∞). In reverse bias the reading is also very high (∞). What is the fault and what caused it?

📌 Answer

Fault: Open circuit diode — the junction inside has broken completely, so no current can flow in either direction.

Likely causes: Excessive forward current (overloaded without current limiting resistor), overheating, or physical damage (cracked package).

Action: Replace with a diode of the same or higher current and voltage rating.

Problem 4: Diode Selection

Select an appropriate diode for each application, justifying your choice:

📌 Answers

a) Mains power supply rectifier (240V AC): 1N4007 rectifier diode — PIV 1000V (safe margin above 339V peak), 1A rating sufficient for domestic PSU.

b) 5V reference voltage: 5.1V Zener diode — provides stable 5.1V in reverse breakdown for voltage regulation.

c) TV remote control transmitter: IR LED (infrared) — emits 940nm infrared light invisible to humans but detected by IR receiver in TV.

d) Road traffic light (green): High-power green LED — energy efficient, long life, fast switching, exact wavelength control.
