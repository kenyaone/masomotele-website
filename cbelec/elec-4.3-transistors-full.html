Bipolar Junction Transistor (BJT)

Structure, terminals and types

What is a Transistor?

Transistor: A three-terminal semiconductor device that can amplify signals or switch electronic circuits. The word comes from "transfer resistor" — it transfers a signal across a resistor. Invented in 1947 at Bell Labs — the most important invention of the 20th century.
BJT (Bipolar Junction Transistor): Has two PN junctions. Three terminals: Base (B), Collector (C), Emitter (E). Current flow involves both electrons and holes (bipolar). Two types: NPN and PNP.

BJT Operation — Current Relationships

How a small base current controls a large collector current

The Key Transistor Principle

Current amplification: A small base current (IB) controls a much larger collector current (IC). The ratio IC/IB is called the DC current gain (hFE or β). A transistor amplifies current.
Emitter current: IE = IB + IC. The emitter current equals the sum of base and collector currents (Kirchhoff's Current Law at the transistor).

✏️ Current Gain Example

A BC547 transistor has β = 200. A base current of 50µA flows.

IC = β × IB = 200 × 50µA = 10mA

IE = IB + IC = 50µA + 10mA ≈ 10.05mA

The 50µA base current controls 10mA — a gain of 200×!

Transistor as a Switch

Digital control — fully ON or fully OFF

Switching Operation

Transistor switch: When operated in cut-off (OFF) or saturation (ON) regions, the transistor acts as a digital switch. A small base signal controls a large load. Used in relays, motor drivers, LED controllers, and digital logic.

Designing a Transistor Switch

✏️ Switch Design Example

Design a transistor switch to drive a 100mA relay coil from a 5V microcontroller output using a BC547 (β = 200, VCE(sat) = 0.2V, VBE = 0.7V). Supply = 12V.

Step 1: Required collector current IC = 100mA

Step 2: Required base current: IB = IC/β = 100mA/200 = 0.5mA

Step 3: Use IB = 2× to ensure saturation: IB = 1mA

Step 4: Base resistor RB = (Vin − VBE)/IB = (5 − 0.7)/0.001 = 4,300Ω → use 4.7kΩ

Step 5: Collector resistor: Use relay coil resistance directly (relay is the load).

⚠️ Freewheeling Diode for Inductive Loads

When switching inductive loads (relays, motors, solenoids), always connect a freewheeling diode (flyback diode) in reverse across the load. When the transistor turns OFF, the collapsing magnetic field generates a high-voltage spike that can destroy the transistor. The diode clamps this spike safely.

Transistor as an Amplifier

Making small signals bigger

Amplification Principle

Amplifier: A circuit that increases the amplitude of a signal. A transistor in the active region produces an output signal that is a scaled-up version of the input signal.
Voltage gain (Av): Av = Vout / Vin. The ratio of output voltage to input voltage. A gain of 100 means the output is 100× larger than the input.
Common Emitter (CE) amplifier: The most common BJT amplifier configuration. Input applied to base, output taken from collector. Provides high voltage gain with 180° phase inversion (output is inverted).

✏️ CE Amplifier Voltage Gain Calculation

A CE amplifier has collector resistor RC = 4.7kΩ and emitter resistor RE = 470Ω.

Approximate voltage gain: Av = −RC / RE = −4700 / 470 = −10 (minus = 180° inversion)

If Vin = 50mV peak, then Vout = 10 × 50mV = 500mV peak

Field Effect Transistor (FET)

Voltage-controlled transistors

What is a FET?

FET (Field Effect Transistor): A three-terminal transistor controlled by voltage rather than current. Three terminals: Gate (G), Drain (D), Source (S). Unipolar — current flows by one type of carrier only.
MOSFET (Metal Oxide Semiconductor FET): The most common type of FET. Gate is insulated by a thin oxide layer — draws virtually no gate current. N-channel MOSFET conducts when gate voltage is positive. Used in logic gates, power switching, and all digital ICs.

💡 Why MOSFETs Rule Digital Electronics

Every smartphone contains billions of MOSFETs — Apple's A17 chip has ~19 billion. MOSFETs are ideal for digital circuits because: they switch fully ON/OFF with just a voltage signal, consume almost no power in steady state, can be made extremely small, and switch billions of times per second. The entire digital world runs on MOSFETs.

Practice Problems

Apply transistor circuit knowledge
Problem 1: Current Gain

A BC547 NPN transistor has β = 300. A base current of 20µA flows. Calculate: (a) collector current, (b) emitter current.

📌 Solution

(a) IC = β × IB = 300 × 20µA = 6,000µA = 6mA

(b) IE = IB + IC = 20µA + 6mA = 20µA + 6000µA = 6,020µA ≈ 6mA

Problem 2: Switch Design

A transistor switch is to drive a 12V, 200mA relay from a 3.3V microcontroller output. The transistor has β = 150 and VBE = 0.7V. Calculate the base resistor needed (use saturation factor of 2).

📌 Solution

IB required = IC/β = 200mA/150 = 1.33mA

With saturation factor × 2: IB = 2.67mA

RB = (Vin − VBE)/IB = (3.3 − 0.7)/0.00267 = 2.6/0.00267 = 974Ω → use 1kΩ

Problem 3: CE Amplifier

A common emitter amplifier has RC = 10kΩ and RE = 1kΩ. Calculate the voltage gain and find the output voltage if the input is 20mV.

📌 Solution

Av = −RC/RE = −10,000/1,000 = −10 (inverting)

|Av| = 10

Vout = Av × Vin = 10 × 20mV = 200mV (inverted — 180° phase shift)

Problem 4: BJT vs FET

A student needs a transistor to switch a 24V, 15A DC motor from a microcontroller. Should they use a BJT or MOSFET? Justify your answer.

📌 Answer

MOSFET is better for this application. Reasons:

• High current (15A) — power MOSFETs like IRF540 handle 28A easily; BJT at this current needs significant base drive current.

• Voltage controlled — microcontroller just provides gate voltage, no significant gate current needed.

• Lower on-resistance (RDS(on)) → less heat, higher efficiency at high currents.

• Faster switching — better for PWM motor speed control.
