4. Role picker — who can do what

TikTok Business Centre has three user roles — simpler than Meta's six. Assigned per asset. Click each role to reveal what they can and cannot do.

Who: Brian and a true business partner only. Full control of the Business Centre.

CAN
  • Add and remove users
  • Add and remove assets (ad accounts, pixels, catalogues)
  • Change billing methods and top up
  • Grant Spark Ad authorisations
  • Create, edit, kill any campaign
  • Access all reports and event data
CANNOT
  • Nothing — Admin is unrestricted
  • (But: least-privilege rule says grant Admin sparingly)

Who: Brian's video editor. Day-to-day work inside an assigned asset.

CAN
  • Create and edit campaigns on the assigned ad account
  • Upload creative and edit ads
  • Request budget changes
  • View reports for that ad account
CANNOT
  • Add or remove users
  • Change billing
  • Change Business Centre settings
  • Access pixel data (unless separately granted)

Who: Brian's accountant. View-only.

CAN
  • View reports on the assigned asset
  • View pixel event totals
  • Export CSV reports
CANNOT
  • Create, edit, or pause any campaign
  • Upload creative
  • Change any setting
  • Add users or top up billing
Rule of thumb: Admin only for humans who could sign a cheque. Operator for anyone editing ads. Analyst for anyone reviewing spend. And always grant pixel access separately — customer data is not for creative freelancers.

5. Attribution windows — 1-day vs 7-day vs 28-day click

The attribution window decides how long after a click TikTok will credit itself with a conversion. Longer windows credit TikTok with more conversions but include more indirect assists. Switch the window to see how Chapa Fitness's November purchase count shifts.

Kenya trap: A rookie switches to 28-day click chasing "better" numbers. Ninety per cent of those extra conversions would have happened anyway. Seven-day click + one-day view is the honest professional default.

6. Kenya trap — USD-only billing + timezone

Two Ads Manager settings are hard for Kenyan advertisers.

  • Currency: TikTok Ads Manager for Kenya only offers USD. You cannot pick KES on the ad account. Top up via Visa card or M-Pesa → Visa conversion. Every report is in USD. Convert to KES internally for pricing decisions.
  • Timezone: Pick Africa/Nairobi at account creation. If you leave the default UTC, day-parting and "yesterday" reports will confuse everyone.
  • Attribution: Set to 7-day click + 1-day view at creation. Locked once conversions accumulate.
Once, forever: These three settings are set once at account creation. Changing them later either breaks reports or is simply not permitted. Get them right on day one.

7. Self-check quiz

Eight questions on the deep tour. Pick and lock — instant feedback when you mark.

1. Where does Brian create a proper TikTok Business Centre?

Correct — the Business Centre lives at business.tiktok.com. The in-app Business account is a testing tool only.

2. Which role should Brian's video editor get on the ad account?

Correct — Operator handles day-to-day work without the risk of admin actions. Least privilege.

3. What is the professional default attribution window?

Correct — 7-day click + 1-day view balances honest credit with reasonable window. Longer windows over-credit.

4. Which currency does TikTok Ads Manager use for Kenya?

Correct — TikTok Kenya bills in USD only. Top up via Visa or M-Pesa conversion.

5. Where is the audience targeting set?

Correct — audience, placement, budget, and schedule all live at Ad Group.

6. Which asset must Brian ALSO add to the Business Centre so all ad accounts can use it?

Correct — the catalogue lives at the Business Centre so any child ad account can reuse it.

7. What happens if Brian's personal TikTok is disabled tomorrow, but he set up Business Centre on day one?

Correct — the Business Centre owns the assets. Any admin can log in and continue.

8. Post authorisation in Business Centre powers which feature?

Correct — post authorisation is the plumbing behind Spark Ads (covered in 12.3).
