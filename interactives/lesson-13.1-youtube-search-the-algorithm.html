3. Long-tail vs short-tail intent

Short-tail — "ugali recipe"

Huge volume. Brutal competition. Won by big global channels with millions of subscribers.

Est monthly searches KE: ~180K

Competing videos: 4.2M

Sarah's realistic rank: page 5+

Long-tail — "how to make ugali without lumps"

Lower volume. Much lower competition. Higher intent — viewer has a specific problem.

Est monthly searches KE: ~2.4K

Competing videos: 8.7K

Sarah's realistic rank: top 3 ✓

Small-brand rule: Long-tail is where you win. Ten long-tail videos ranking top-3 beats one short-tail video ranking on page 5.

5. Sarah's takeaway

Sarah has been posting her three-minute ugali tutorials to the Karibu Kitchen Facebook Page. She gets around 800 views, the post decays after 48 hours, and disappears. That same tutorial on YouTube would:

  • Keep collecting views for years — not 48 hours
  • Appear on YouTube Search for "how to make ugali"
  • Appear on YouTube Suggested next to related cooking videos
  • Appear on Google Search for the same query (Google shows YouTube carousels)
Sarah's decision: How-to content lives on YouTube first. Facebook becomes the cross-post, not the primary. One insight worth the module.
