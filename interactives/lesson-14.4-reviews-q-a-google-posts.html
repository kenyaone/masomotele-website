1. Reviews — the biggest Prominence signal

Volume, recency, star rating and your responses all matter — Google reads them and searchers read them. Sarah's target: 1–2 fresh reviews per week, forever. The trick is asking every satisfied customer individually via a personal WhatsApp thank-you with the g.page/r/… short review link.

Never buy reviews. Fifty shillings a review from a Facebook group looks tempting. Google's algorithm detects them at scale, removes them, flags the profile, and in severe cases suspends the entire listing. Competitors also report each other.

3. Kenya DPA 2019 — never publish a customer's full name

The Data Protection Act 2019 makes it unlawful to publish a customer's personal data — including their full name — without their consent. When Sarah responds to a review, she does not include the customer's surname in her public response.

Rules: Use only the first name they used to sign the review, OR a neutral form ("Thank you for the kind words"). Never add surnames, phone numbers or estate names. The fine is up to KES 5 million per breach.

DPA-safe example: "Asante for the kind words. So glad the pilau met the mark."
DPA-breach example: "Asante John Kamau. Karibu tena to Elgon View." — surname + estate = personal data.

6. Mwema Coffee House — the recovery cameo

The Mwema Coffee House recovery, Nairobi — Kilimani, 8 weeks

Three months ago Mwema Coffee House had three brutal 1-star reviews after a staff shortage caused slow service across a full weekend. Rank collapsed from position 2 to position 7 for "coffee shop Kilimani". Sarah walked them through the fix.

Before — Position 7
  • Three 1-star complaints, unanswered
  • No Google Posts in 2 months
  • Average rating: 3.9
  • 15 reviews, no fresh velocity
After — Position 2
  • All 3 negatives publicly acknowledged with private-channel fix offer
  • 2 Google Posts per week for 8 weeks
  • Average rating: 4.4 (14 fresh 5-stars)
  • 29 reviews, weekly velocity

Lesson: Response strategy IS recovery strategy. Never argue in public. Move the fix to WhatsApp. Ask happy customers weekly. Post twice a week. Ranking follows.
