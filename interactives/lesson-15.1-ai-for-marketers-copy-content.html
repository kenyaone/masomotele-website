1. Why AI matters for marketers in 2026

The productivity shift in marketing since 2023 is comparable to the shift from print to Google Ads. Marketers who use AI properly ship 3–5× more content for the same hours. Those who don't fall behind. Fast.

What changes: ad copy A/B tests, email subject lines, blog outlines, meta descriptions, translation, customer-support drafts, social captions, image generation, video scripts. All of these move from hours to minutes.

2. What AI CAN and CAN'T do

✓ AI CAN

  • Draft ad copy variants in seconds
  • Generate 20 headlines to A/B test
  • Translate between English, Swahili, Sheng
  • Summarise long documents
  • Suggest content ideas at scale
  • Draft emails, replies, SEO metas
  • Debug spreadsheet formulas

✗ AI CANNOT (yet)

  • Know your specific customer without you telling it
  • Make strategic decisions for you
  • Verify facts — will confidently invent things (hallucinations)
  • Replace your brand voice — you must train it
  • Replace human judgment on ethics + tone
  • Understand your business results
The golden rule: AI is a junior assistant, not a strategist. You direct. You verify. You are responsible for what ships.

3. The 3 tools

Claude (Anthropic) ★

Free tier · Paid Pro ~$20/mo

Best for long-form writing, nuanced marketing copy, strategic thinking. Strong reasoning.

Best for: blog posts, strategy docs, thoughtful email copy

ChatGPT (OpenAI) ★

Free tier · Paid Plus $20/mo

Most-used. Broad capability. Image generation built in (DALL-E). Voice mode.

Best for: general marketing, quick drafts, images, brainstorming

Gemini (Google)

Free · Advanced $20/mo

Integrated with Google Workspace. Access to real-time Google Search.

Best for: research with citations, Google Workspace users
Practical picks: Free tier of any of the three is enough to start. Get Pro on ONE (not all three) once you use it daily.

4. The 5 prompt patterns

These are the templates professional marketers use every day. Learn them by heart.

1. Role + Task + Context
You are a Kenyan copywriter for a lunch delivery business. Write 5 Facebook ad headlines for a KES 350 ugali & beef stew delivery in Eldoret CBD.
2. Constraint + Format
Under 40 characters each. Include the price. Use emoji sparingly. Output as a numbered list.
3. Voice + Audience
Voice: warm, friendly, Kenyan English. Audience: office workers 25–45. Sarah is the founder — feel personal.
4. Show, don't tell
Here are 3 examples of ads I love: [paste 3 examples]. Match this style for Karibu Kitchen.
5. Iterate
"That's good but #3 sounds too corporate. Rewrite it more casual, like Sarah is texting a friend."

6. Ethics + Kenya DPA 2019 implications

⚠ 5 rules for Kenyan marketers using AI

  1. Never paste customer PII (names, phone numbers, addresses, order history) into public AI tools. DPA 2019 violation.
  2. Always verify facts. AI invents statistics, quotes, product features. Cross-check before shipping.
  3. Disclose AI use when it materially affects the customer (e.g., AI-generated support replies). Not legally required yet in Kenya — ethically required.
  4. Keep human judgment on tone. AI can produce technically-correct copy that feels tone-deaf about local context.
  5. Own the output. If AI writes something misleading, that's still on you legally. AI is not a defence.
Safer alternative: paid Claude Pro / ChatGPT Team plans promise not to train on your data. Use those for sensitive work. Free tiers may use your data for training.
