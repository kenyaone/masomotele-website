2. The "going viral" fantasy

Sarah's cousin says Karibu Kitchen must "go viral" — 20M views, orders pouring in.

That fantasy has a name: the lottery. Professional marketers don't try to go viral. They build slow, steady, growing audiences. Viral = lightning. Steady = sunrise. Only one you can plan around.

4. 5 daily content practices

  1. Post regularly — consistency > sporadic brilliance
  2. Post relevant — what YOUR audience cares about, not what YOU find interesting
  3. Research — follow competitors, customers, industry leaders
  4. Run competitions — a KES 500 giveaway can bring 20 followers in an hour
  5. Use visuals — text-only posts get half the engagement

5. Influencer reality check

Before you pay an influencer, run 3 checks:

1
Is their audience YOUR audience? Beauty influencer's followers won't buy Eldoret lunch delivery.
2
Is their engagement real? Engagement rate under 1% = bought followers.
3
Can you TRACK the result? Unique discount code or trackable URL. If not — walk away.
Influencer marketing without tracking is guessing. Most Kenyan SMEs waste money here.

6. URL shortener trick

Every serious marketer uses shorteners for 2 reasons:

  1. Shorter links look cleaner in posts
  2. Shorteners TRACK clicks — you see every click from every post

Free tools: bit.ly, rebrandly, tinyurl. Sarah adds a tracked link to every website post → knows which content works.
