2. Four principles of professional engagement

Every reply — positive or negative — follows these four rules.

  1. Reply within 24 hours. Faster if possible. Silence is worse than any reply.
  2. Professional but personable tone. Sarah is not a lawyer; she is a business owner on her best day.
  3. Never delete a negative comment unless it contains threats, abuse, or spam. Deletion is a red flag every customer notices.
  4. Take sensitive issues offline. Publicly acknowledge, privately resolve — DM or email.
Deletion rule: The moment you delete a legitimate complaint, screenshots start circulating. Two hours of professional reply beats two years of reputation repair.

3. Six CTAs that convert on Kenyan Facebook & Instagram

Every business post needs a Call To Action — a specific instruction. Never post a beautiful lunch photo without telling the reader what to do next.

Get a quote
Sign up now
Order now
Send us a DM
Tag a friend who needs this
Comment YES if you agree
Sarah's rule: For every KES 500 plate photo she posts, the caption must end with "WhatsApp 0712-XXX-XXX to order — delivery in 30 min across Kapsoya, Elgon View & Racecourse." Explicit CTA + phone number + delivery zone = orders.

4. Angry comment & competitor spam workflow

The three-sentence formula for any negative comment:

  1. Acknowledge with empathy: "I am so sorry your meal was not up to our standards."
  2. Offer to make it right: "This is not the experience we want any customer to have."
  3. Take it offline: "Please DM me the details — I would like to make it right personally. — Sarah."
Competitor spam: When Nyakinyua Kitchen leaves "Better food at Nyakinyua" under Sarah's posts, do NOT delete. Report first (with screenshot evidence) → wait 2 weeks → mute/restrict if Facebook does not act. Deletion is last resort, and only for genuine abuse.
