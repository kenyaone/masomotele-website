30%saves growth target

Twelve posts across four weeks. Three per week — Monday, Wednesday, Friday. Batched in one four-hour Sunday session. Target: 30% growth in saves month-on-month.

Why 12 not 30: Sarah is one person with a kitchen to run. Twelve well-planned posts out-perform 30 rushed ones on every metric. Quality > quantity, especially for local businesses.
Kenya calendar hooks to plan around: Madaraka Day (1 June), Mashujaa Day (20 October), Jamhuri Day (12 December), Ramadhan & Eid al-Fitr, mid-month payday (15th), end-month payday (28th–1st), Christmas (25 Dec). Sarah pins a promo to each of these.

2. The four content pillars

Every post fits into exactly one pillar. Colour-coded so the calendar tells you at a glance whether the month is balanced.

Behind-the-scenes (30%)
Menu spotlight (25%)
Customer story (25%)
Educational tip (20%)
Target split: 30 / 25 / 25 / 20. For 12 posts that's ~4 behind-the-scenes, 3 menu spotlights, 3 customer stories, 2 educational tips. The tool below flags any pillar that goes off-target.
Common mistake: new marketers post 90% menu spotlights (all sell, no soul) or 90% behind-the-scenes (all soul, no sell). Neither converts. The 30/25/25/20 mix is the professional balance because it earns trust (behind-the-scenes + educational) and asks for the order (menu + customer stories).

What each pillar is (and is not)

  • Behind-the-scenes — Sarah and her team at work: plating, cooking, morning market runs. Builds trust that a real human is on the other side. Not: staged glossy shots.
  • Menu spotlight — dishes with clear price and CTA. Direct-response — this is where orders come from. Not: aspirational lifestyle shots without a price.
  • Customer story — reviews, tagged photos, thank-you screenshots, order recaps. Social proof — the closer to a real name and location, the better. Not: fake testimonials.
  • Educational tip — recipes, storage advice, "what to eat when …" carousels. Wins on saves. Positions Sarah as the expert, not just a supplier.

4. The batching workflow

Batching means doing similar tasks in a single focused session. Sarah's monthly rhythm:

  • Sunday, 2–4 PM (2h): Food photography — shoot all 12 photos + b-roll clips in one lit-window session.
  • Monday, 8–10 AM (2h): Canva design — build all 12 posts using her three templates (from 7.4).
  • Monday, 10–10:30 AM (30m): Scheduling — bulk-load into Meta Business Suite, drop TikTok drafts.
Total: 4.5 hours once per month. The other 24 days of the month Sarah does not touch Canva. She never misses a Wednesday post because she was too tired. Weekends stay her own.
Batching killer: answering DMs mid-batch. Turn on airplane mode for the two design hours. Leads still arrive; they'll still be there at 10 AM.

5. The 80/20 rule applied

20% of Sarah's posts drive 80% of her engagement. Identify last month's top three and copy the format — do not invent new templates every month.

  • Sarah's top post last month: chapati & stew photo — plan two more chapati photos this month.
  • Second top: 30-second Sunday cooking Reel — plan one more Reel in the same format.
  • Third: customer thank-you screenshot — proactively ask this month's happy customers for reviews.
  • Kill list: any post format that under-performs twice in a row. Do not revive it out of nostalgia.
  • Kenya-specific top format: WhatsApp Status re-shares. Sarah's customers screenshot her Reels and post them to their own Status — free reach. Design every Reel so the last frame is screenshottable.
Rule of thumb: if a post format wins twice in a row, add it to your permanent rotation. Do not chase novelty at the cost of what works.

The four-step planning process (before you even touch the calendar)

  1. Purpose: awareness, engagement, or conversion? Sarah's month = conversion (drive WhatsApp orders).
  2. Audience: Eldoret office workers, 25–50, food-photo lovers, allergic to over-produced ads.
  3. Content series: a consistent look, coordinated campaigns, recurring themes.
  4. Calendar: plan weeks in advance, batch similar content, schedule around key dates (payday, holidays, Ramadhan, Christmas), but leave 1–2 slots for timely content.
Amateurs feel inspired. Professionals feel prepared. Sarah's post frequency dropped from 6/week to 2/week when she waited for inspiration. Plans beat inspiration every single time.
The one slot to leave open: reserve every Friday afternoon slot as "reactive" — for a customer photo that arrives that day, a viral trending sound, or a same-week special. A rigid plan without a reactive slot misses the moments that matter.
