1. The 6 shot types every phone-filmmaker uses

Brian's rule: a Reel is a stack of 3-4 shots, never one long take. Learn what each shot does before you pick a phone up.

WIDE

Establish the setting — Sarah's kitchen or the delivery scooter.

MEDIUM

Waist up — great for talking-head + gesture.

CLOSE-UP

Emotion + reaction — the smile after the first bite.

B-ROLL

Cutaways of ingredients, hands, packaging. Hides jump cuts.

POV

Customer POV — as if you are the one lifting the fork.

OVERHEAD

Flat-lay of the plated dish. Instagram gold.
Brian's rule of 4: a 12-second Reel usually mixes 3-4 different shot types. Never repeat the same angle twice in a row.

5. Self-check quiz

Correct — if the hook fails, everything after it is wasted.

2. Best shot type for a plated-food flat-lay on Instagram is...

Correct — overhead flat-lay is the food photographer's default.

3. Roughly how many words fit in a 3-second spoken hook?

Correct — ~5 words per second, so 13-15 is the cap.

4. Which is NOT a phone-video rule Brian teaches?

Correct — horizontal is only for YouTube long-form. Reels need vertical.

5. Best shot to establish a scene at the start of a Reel?

Correct — Wide establishes context in one glance.

6. Best shot to capture emotion after Sarah's customer tastes lunch?

Correct — Close-up on the face = emotion. Every time.

7. TikTok says what % of users watch with sound ON?

Correct — sound ON is the norm; captions cover the exceptions.
