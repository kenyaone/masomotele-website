1. Why keyword research first — always

Every professional SEO campaign starts with keyword research. Skip this step and everything after is guesswork. Two reasons:

  1. Keywords tell you what customers actually type — not what YOU think they type. Sarah calls her food "artisan lunch". Nobody in Eldoret searches for that. They search "cheap lunch delivery Kapsoya".
  2. Keywords tell you which searches are worth targeting. Some keywords have high volume but impossible competition. Others have low volume but you can rank #1 in weeks.
Rule: Research first. Then write. Never write, then guess.

2. How to open Google Keyword Planner

Google Keyword Planner is the professional standard. Free. Official. Used by every serious digital marketer in the world.

  1. Sign up for a Google Ads account at ads.google.com. You do NOT need to spend a shilling on ads to use the Planner.
  2. Log in. Click Tools and Settings in the top-right corner.
  3. Choose Keyword Planner.
  4. Choose Discover new keywords.
  5. Type your seed keyword — for Sarah, lunch delivery Eldoret — and click Get Results.
Seed keyword: the short, obvious phrase that describes your product. Google will expand it into dozens of related ideas.

7. Assign each keyword to a page

Each keyword becomes a specific page task. One page. One keyword. Never target two "money" keywords on the same page — Google can't tell which one to rank.

Home page

The single most important page. Should target the highest-intent commercial keyword.

lunch delivery Eldoret CBD

Menu page

Branded — obvious win. People searching for you specifically.

Karibu Kitchen menu

Blog post 1

Solve a specific neighbourhood problem.

cheap lunch delivery Kapsoya

Menu sub-page: Vegetarian

Serve a specific dietary + neighbourhood need.

vegetarian lunch Elgon View

Blog post 2

Local, specific dish + neighbourhood.

chapati delivery Racecourse

Office bulk page

Different intent — office managers ordering for teams.

office lunch delivery Eldoret
Pattern: Home + Menu are the two "money" pages. Blog posts handle the long-tail keywords that build traffic over time.

8. Self-check quiz

Ten questions. Pick and lock — mark to see feedback.

1. Which is a "seed keyword" for a Sarah's business?

Correct — a seed is a short, obvious phrase that describes the product. Google expands it into related ideas.

2. Which pair of columns matters most in the Planner for an SEO decision?

Correct — volume tells you demand; competition tells you if you can rank.

3. Sarah sees "food delivery" — 10,000+ searches, HIGH competition. Should she target it?

Correct — high-volume + high-competition is a trap for small businesses. Go long-tail instead.

4. Which is the strongest long-tail keyword for Sarah?

Correct — 4+ words, location-specific, dietary-specific. Rankable in weeks and every searcher is close to buying.

5. Do you need to run Google Ads to use Keyword Planner?

Correct — an Ads account is required to access the Planner, but there is no spend requirement.

6. What is the "one page, one keyword" rule?

Correct — targeting two money keywords on one page splits Google's ranking signal between them. Both underperform.

7. Sarah's Home page should target…

Correct — Home page = money keyword. Branded keywords (Karibu Kitchen) go on the Menu/About pages.

8. What does "competition" refer to in the Keyword Planner?

Correct — competition is an ads metric. It's a useful proxy for SEO difficulty but not a direct SEO score.

9. What's a good target volume for a Kenyan SME long-tail keyword?

Correct — for a local SME, 10–1,000 monthly searches is the sweet spot.

10. What should Sarah do BEFORE writing a page?

Correct — research first, write second. Reverse this and you'll waste weeks.
